# Hook test batch: 5 new hook types (scripts for approval, 15 Oct 2026)

Owner: "after this video renders we try all those new hook types and see how it performs." From the DecodingYT guide:
judge each Short by **swipe-away rate** (Studio: "Viewed vs. swiped away") and **average % viewed** (aim 80%+).
Each script tests ONE hook type, keeps our proven parts (story, a character, a twist, a comment question) and ends so
the last line loops back into the first (rewatches push APV toward 100%). Turbo voice + tags, 2D style, 35-45 s.
Never two of the same character in a row.

## H1. Hook type: CONTRADICT A BELIEF — "This Teacher LET Her Class Cheat" (~40 s, original story, fiction)

| # | Narration | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | This teacher let her whole class cheat on the final exam. | Frame 0: teacher writing "CHEATING ALLOWED" on the board. Hook text: **SHE LET THEM CHEAT** |
| 2 | One rule. You can bring one cheat sheet. The size of a sticky note. | A tiny yellow sticky note held up, ruler showing how small |
| 3 | [gasp] The class went crazy. | Kids cheering, papers flying |
| 4 | That night, everyone tried to squeeze the whole book onto one tiny note. | Kid at a desk at night, writing smaller and smaller, lamp |
| 5 | They shrank every formula. Picked only the most important ones. Wrote them again and again to make them fit. | Sticky note filling up with microscopic writing, crossed out, rewritten |
| 6 | On exam day, the teacher said: take out your cheat sheets. | Exam hall, every kid holding a sticky note |
| 7 | Nobody looked at theirs. | Close: notes untouched, kids writing fast |
| 8 | By making the cheat sheet, they had already learned everything on it. [chuckle] | Teacher smiling, arms crossed |
| 9 | So was it cheating? Or the smartest homework ever? | Two cards: CHEATING / GENIUS |

Titles: "This Teacher LET Her Class Cheat 📝" · "The Cheat Sheet Trick That Backfired (On Purpose)"

## H2. Hook type: "DID YOU KNOW" — "A Cat Was Mayor for 20 Years" (~40 s, true)

| # | Narration | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did you know a town in Alaska had a cat as its mayor? For twenty years. | Frame 0: orange cat in a tiny tie behind a desk, nameplate "MAYOR". Hook text: **THE MAYOR IS A CAT** |
| 2 | His name was Stubbs. The town is Talkeetna. | Map pin on Alaska, snowy small town |
| 3 | The story goes, in 1997 people didn't like the human candidates. So they voted for the cat. | Ballot box, paws stamp |
| 4 | It was honorary. But the town loved it. | "HONORARY" stamp |
| 5 | His office? A general store. His favourite drink? Water in a wine glass, mixed with catnip. [chuckle] | Cat lapping from a wine glass |
| 6 | Tourists came from all over the world just to meet him. | Line of tourists with cameras |
| 7 | In twenty years, Stubbs never raised taxes. Never broke a promise. Never gave a single speech. | Checklist with three ticks |
| 8 | Honestly? Better record than most politicians. | Cat yawning, "BEST MAYOR" medal |
| 9 | If your town could pick any animal as mayor, which one would you vote for? | Cards: DOG / CAT / GOAT / ? |

Sources: Talkeetna's honorary mayor Stubbs (1997-2017), widely reported (e.g. NPR, Anchorage Daily News); "the story
goes" because the write-in origin is town legend.
Titles: "A Cat Was Mayor for 20 Years 🐱" · "Did You Know a Cat Ran an Alaskan Town?"

|---|---|---|

## H4. Hook type: IDENTITY CALL-OUT — "Last-Row Students, Stop Scrolling" (~38 s, Last Bencher, original story)

## H5. Hook type: ABSTRACT CONCEPT — "The Zeigarnik Effect" (~42 s, true, with a loop ending)

| # | Narration | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | There's a reason you can't stop thinking about an unfinished show. It's called the Zeigarnik effect. | Frame 0: a TV paused on "TO BE CONTINUED…". Hook text: **YOUR BRAIN HATES THIS** |
| 2 | In the 1920s, a psychologist named Bluma Zeigarnik sat in a busy café in Berlin. | 1920s café, waiters rushing |
| 3 | She noticed something strange about the waiters. | Zoom on a waiter with a notepad |
| 4 | They remembered every order that wasn't paid yet. Perfectly. | Floating order bubbles over tables |
| 5 | But once a table paid, they forgot it almost instantly. | Bubbles pop as coins land |
| 6 | So she tested it. People remembered unfinished tasks much better than finished ones. | Two piles: DONE (fading) / NOT DONE (glowing) |
| 7 | Your brain keeps a tab open for anything unfinished. That's why cliffhangers work. | Browser tabs stacking up in a head |
| 8 | And that's exactly why I'm going to stop this video right… | Hard cut to black mid-word; loops to frame 0 |

Sources: Bluma Zeigarnik, 1927 study on interrupted tasks (the café story is the classic account; later replications
are mixed, so the script says "people remembered", not "always").
Titles: "Your Brain Can't Let This Go 🧠" · "Why You Can't Stop Thinking About Cliffhangers"
