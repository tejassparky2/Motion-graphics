## The finding: the first line decides whether people stay
Average viewed is high (85.7%; recent videos 90-94%), so people who stay watch to the end. The loss is the swipe in
the first seconds. Grouped by how the video opens:

| Opening | Stayed to watch | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A character asks the impossible question from the first words | ~72% (3) | "Sir, can God make a rock..." 71.0; "Sir, how many hairs..." 68.2; "A kid in the last row raises his hand, and asks the teacher" 77.9 (15.6K views) |
| Narrator sets a scene first | ~62% (11) | Nazis medal 69.3; Archimedes 69.8; Napoleon chess 50.5; lucky coin 48.3; court 51.6 |
| Talks to the viewer / names a topic / "Meet" / "Did you know" / numbered list | ~47% (14) | "Three riddles..." 40.6; "Did you know a town..." 49.8; "If you ever sat in the last row, stop scrolling" 50.0; "Meet Frane Selak" 43.9; "Three doors..." 43.1 |

The last nine posts (21-25 Oct) averaged 49% stayed: five were the hook tests (talk-to-viewer openers) and none opened
on a character asking. Only videos at 70%+ took off (Hotel 75% → 28K, Last Row Kid 77.9% → 15.6K, Teacher Bluffed
77% → 15.3K); those three brought 279 of the 335 new subscribers.
