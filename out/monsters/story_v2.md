# Cute Things Kill Here: the new story (v2), for your approval

Built from the three episodes you gave me and the research (`reports/Pokémon episodes story and animation.md`). Each
choice below follows one of its rules. Pure anime: no health bar, no menus, no fact cards. Every episode still ends on
an **A or B** that viewers choose in the comments.

## What changed from v1, and why
- **No game screens.** What Kai knows shows only in what he does and in one short line. The anime study and the
  critics of game-menu isekai both point this way.
- **Episode 1 now proves his edge.** He survives the snap with the fact planted at his death; in v1 he only ran into
  it. In all three episodes, the climax pays off something planted in the first third.
- **The vote comes at the next problem, not at a soft ending.** The episode's danger is solved first, then a new
  threat appears and you choose. That is where Pokémon places its ad break.
- **Both options always have a real upside and a real risk.** Neither is a "wrong button". Whichever loses still shows
  up later in the story.
- **The first friendship is earned by saving each other,** the way Pikachu and Ash began. In v1, surviving its power
  was enough.
- **Danger comes from nature; cruelty, if any, from people.** Creatures are never evil.

## The world in 6 lines
- **The world:** pastel, soft and full of small, adorable creatures. Every one has a power taken from a real animal,
  and every one has one small wrong detail you can spot first.
- **Kai (19):** a marine-biology student who dies in a lab accident during a storm. He has no powers, only real
  animal facts and the nerve to stay still.
- **The bond:** when a person and a creature save each other, a thin glowing mark appears on the person's wrist. Nobody
  explains it; the locals fear it.
- **The mystery (one clue per episode):** the sky has a hairline crack in exactly the shape of the crack in the tank
  where Kai died.
- **Recurring cast (kept small):**
  - Kai and his first partner, the aqua puff-shrimp.
  - Wren, a village bell-keeper girl who sees creatures as disasters to calm with bells, never as friends. She is the
    sceptic Kai has to convince.
  - Later, a rival who controls creatures with collars: the opposite of bonding.
- **Hero's look:** the restyled model you approved (brown hair, amber eyes, sage tunic).

**The real facts:**
- **Pistol shrimp:** the snap makes a collapsing bubble that briefly reaches about 4,700 °C, against about 5,500 °C at
  the sun's surface, and it only forms in water (Lohse et al., *Nature* 2001; Versluis et al., *Science* 2000).
- **Shrikes ("butcher birds"):** they impale prey on thorns to store it.

## Episode 2: both branches (you approve both outlines; only the winner gets written and rendered)

Both branches meet the same next event, the village (the branch-and-bottleneck structure in the report), but carry
different consequences.

**2A "SAVE"** (Kai gets hurt, the bond is made by rescue):
- **Open:** "You said save it. So I ran at the butcher bird."
- **The rescue:** the bird dives at the shrimp. Kai cups the shrimp in his hands, risking the wet claw against his
  palm. The talons rake his arm; the cost stays as a scar.
- **The fact:** a pistol shrimp fires straight ahead, so Kai turns it outward between his fingers. Snap: the bird is
  blasted into the thorn bush, dazed, feathers puffed out. It isn't killed.
- **The bond:** the shrimp taps his nose with its claw, the frame-0 threat turned into affection. A thin coral mark
  glows on his wrist.
- **The next problem:** bells. Wren and villagers with nets arrive: "Step away from the snapper. It goes in the lake."
  Kai knows deep water makes it unstoppable.
- **Ending:** "Hand it over, or hide it in my shirt?"

**2B "RUN"** (Kai is wrong first, the bond is made by care):
- **Open:** "You said run. So I ran."
- **The fact backfires:** running makes him the prey. Many predators chase what runs, and the bird leaves the shrimp
  and dives at him. He dives into a hollow log; the beak stabs through the wood.
- **The save:** a snap outside. The shrimp blasted the bird away; it was its enemy too.
- **The cost:** the shrimp's claw is cracked from the bird's first peck, so it can't snap for an episode.
- **The care:** "Shrimp breathe through gills. They have to stay wet." Kai carries puddle water to it in his hands.
- **The bond:** the nose-tap; the mark glows.
- **The next problem:** the same bells and Wren.
- **Ending:** "Hand it over, or hide it?" *(Hiding a shrimp that can't defend itself is a different bet.)*

**State log after Episode 2:**
- **A:** Kai's arm is scarred; the shrimp is healthy; the bond came from rescue.
- **B:** the shrimp's claw is cracked; Kai owes it his life; the bond came from care.

## The first arc (8-12 Shorts), with fixed turning points
1. **The first bond** (Episode 2).
2. **The village and Wren** (Episodes 3-4). Wren's bell rules against Kai's facts. One village superstition fails on
   screen while the real animal reason works.
3. **The first apex predator** (around Episodes 5-7). Its tell is spotted early and a small creature shows the same
   trick first. A second bond is made a different way: a bargain or food.
4. **The arc climax** (Episodes 10-12). The collar rival, and the crack in the sky widens.

Outcomes rotate (a clean win, a costly win, a creature refusing, the rival winning) and the dilemmas vary: risk vs.
safety, who to trust, fight vs. hide, helping a stranger. No two episodes in a row end the same way.

## How it will look (from the frame-by-frame study)
- **Talk and feeling:** shots cut every 3-4 s, held on characters drawn on 3s.
- **Action:** shots cut every 1-2 s, creatures on 2s.
- **Every snap and hit follows the measured pattern:**
  1. an eye close-up;
  2. a wind-up held 0.5-1 s;
  3. 1-3 white or inverted impact frames;
  4. the result held 1-2 s;
  5. the reaction, with the shake on the person reacting.
- **Silence:** a second of silence before every big reveal.
- **Cute to scary:** done with light alone (shadow from above, glowing eyes), on the same cute model.
- **Laughs:** held reaction shots (2 s), never fast cuts.
- **The choice:** a top/bottom split screen with a jagged divider, like the anime's face-offs. Never a menu.
- **On-screen text:** only captions and the frame-0 hook line.
