# New channel: countries explain the news (concept + pilot script, for approval)

## What the reference does (@omaragamyy reel, 52 s, analysed frame by frame)

- **Format:** a dialogue. The creator is on camera in front of a flag and asks the country "what's new?" The country
  answers as a waving flag emoji with a human mouth and eyes composited on.
- **Pace:** about 220 words a minute, cutting between the two speakers every 1–3 s.
- **Look:**
  - Big all-caps captions: white for the host, red for the country.
  - A black background with cut-out news photos of the people involved.
- **Tone:** casual. He ends with his own opinion ("Honestly, pretty good law").

## How ours is different (so it's our own channel, not a clone)

| | Reference | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Host | the creator on camera | an animated host, voiced by **you** (best) or the channel voice |
| Countries | flag emoji with a real human mouth | **hand-drawn flag characters with personality**, e.g. California = the grizzly bear from its flag, Japan = the red sun, Canada = the maple leaf |
| Pictures of people | real news photos | drawn scenes and labels only. News photos are copyrighted and would get claimed |
| Opening line | "Hey USA, what's new with you?" (his catchphrase) | our own hook, a question about the news itself |
| Opinion | the host's take | **neutral**: we say who claims what ("the governor's office says…") and leave the debate to the comments |
| Sources | none shown | sources in every description |

Copying his exact look and catchphrase would make viewers call it a knock-off, and a channel that copies a bigger
channel's style is exactly what YouTube's reviewers flag (see `reports/Upload frequency and monetization risk.md`).

## Pilot script: "California Just Banned Politician Meme Coins"

**Speakers:** HOST (animated host) and CALIFORNIA (the flag's grizzly bear, holding its star). About 115 words, about 40 s.

> **HOST:** California, why is everyone talking about you?
> **CALIFORNIA:** I just banned my politicians from making meme coins.
> **HOST:** Wait. Politicians make meme coins?
> **CALIFORNIA:** Well, the President did. And according to the New York Times, nearly **one million** people who bought
> it lost more than **three billion dollars**. **[on screen: ~1,000,000 buyers · −$3 BILLION]**
> **HOST:** Ouch.
> **CALIFORNIA:** Same report says he made about **six hundred thirty-six million**. **[on screen: +$636M]**
> **HOST:** So what does your new law do?
> **CALIFORNIA:** Two things. No public official in California can launch a meme coin. And crypto companies can't list
> coins that use an official's face.
> **HOST:** Did anyone name this law something subtle?
> **CALIFORNIA:** It's part of **eleven** bills my governor signed, in a package his office called... "The Opposite of
> Trump." **[on screen: 11 BILLS]**
> **HOST:** Subtle.
> **CALIFORNIA:** So... should every state do this?

**Visual idea:**
- The bear bursts out of the flag.
- A meme coin with a cartoon crown and no real face drops like a stone on a crashing chart.
- A stack of 11 bill papers gets stamped.
- The host's jaw drops at "$3 billion".

**Fact notes, all from the governor's office press release, 27 Sep 2026 ([gov.ca.gov](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/27/the-opposite-of-trump-governor-newsom-signs-bills-to-crack-down-on-corruption-including-meme-coins-by-public-officials/)):**
- AB 2409 prevents "any California public officials from issuing meme coins" and prevents "companies from listing any
  meme coin that uses the likeness or image of a public official".
- It was one of 11 bills in the package.
- The ~1 million buyers, >$3 billion lost and ~$636 million profit are the release's figures, citing a New York Times
  article from 4 Jul 2026. That's why the script attributes them to the Times.
- Also covered by [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-28-california-gov-gavin-newsom-bans-public-officials-from-launching-memecoins-takes-aim-at-trump-416970)
  and [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379404/california-bans-public-officials-from-issuing-meme-coins-under-new-newsom-law).
- **Left out on purpose:** whether people can still buy the existing coin. Reports differ on whether the listing ban
  covers only new coins, so the script doesn't claim either way.

## How news production would work

- **Timing:** news is only worth posting for about 24–48 hours. Each morning I'd find 2–3 verified, non-tragic stories,
  draft the scripts, and you'd approve them. I'd animate the same day.
- **Avoid wars, disasters and deaths.** YouTube limits ads on those, and a cartoon tone doesn't fit them. The best stories
  are strange laws, records, money, tech, and "countries doing weird things".
- **Politics stays neutral and attributed.** That keeps the whole US audience, and advertisers, comfortable.
