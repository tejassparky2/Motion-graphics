**Description**
```
What actually happens when you hiccup? 3 steps. 😳

1. Something irritates the nerves to your diaphragm, like eating fast or fizzy drinks.
2. Your diaphragm (the big breathing muscle under your lungs) suddenly twitches, and you suck in air.
3. Your vocal cords snap shut, and that makes the "hic" sound.

A hiccup is a reflex. Most hiccups stop on their own within minutes, but if they last more than two days (48 hours), see a doctor. Scaring someone? Not proven.

Sources: Steger, Schneemann & Fox, "Hiccups: a common problem with some unusual causes and cures" (PMC5072913); Penn State Health, "The Medical Minute: causes and cures of hiccups remain elusive".

**Alternative titles to test**

- `Does Brain Freeze Freeze Your Brain? 🧠`

**Description**
```
Brain freeze doesn't freeze your brain. Here's what really happens, in 3 steps. 🧊🧠

1. Something very cold touches the roof of your mouth.
2. The blood vessels there quickly squeeze, then open wide again.
3. A big nerve in your face (the trigeminal nerve) senses the change, and your brain feels it as pain in your forehead.

Doctors call it a cold stimulus headache. Scientists still study the exact details, but it's harmless and usually passes within a minute. To avoid it, eat cold things slowly.

Sources: Serrador et al., "Cerebral vascular blood flow changes during 'brain freeze'", FASEB Journal (2012, abstract); Medical News Today, "Brain freeze: the science behind ice cream headache".

Your foot doesn't really fall asleep. Its nerve gets blocked. 3 steps. 🦶⚡

1. Pressure squeezes a nerve, and the blood vessels that feed it.
2. The nerve can't send messages properly, so your foot goes numb.
3. When the pressure is gone, the nerve restarts and sends messy signals: that's the pins and needles.

It's harmless and usually fades within minutes. But if it happens often, without any pressure, see a doctor.

Sources: Cleveland Clinic, "What that pins-and-needles feeling really means"; UPMC HealthBeat, "Why does my foot fall asleep?"
