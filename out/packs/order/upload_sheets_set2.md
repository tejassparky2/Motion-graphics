```
Moral science class. The teacher says lying is wrong, because God says so. Then the Last Bencher raises his hand: "Ma'am, is lying wrong because God says so? Or does God say so, because it's wrong?" 🤔
```

```
Exam morning. A girl folds her hands and prays to pass. Her little brother: "Doesn't God already know if you'll pass? And his plan is perfect? Then why pray? You can't change a perfect plan." 🙏

If prayer changes God's plan, the plan wasn't perfect. If it can't, why pray?

Thomas Aquinas answered this over 700 years ago: we don't pray to change God's plan; our prayers are part of the plan. So God's plan includes her studying too. 😄
```

**Description**
```
A girl finishes her time machine, and her first plan is terrible: go back and stop her parents from meeting. ⏳

If they never meet, she's never born. If she's never born, she never builds the machine, so nobody stops them. So they meet, she's born, she goes back... and stops them again.

It's called the grandfather paradox, and physicists take it seriously. One idea: the past fixes itself (whatever she tries, her parents meet anyway). Another: she lands in a different branch of the universe where she's never born, and her own world is untouched. Neither is proven.

💬 Which is it: a past you can't change, or a second world? 👇
```
