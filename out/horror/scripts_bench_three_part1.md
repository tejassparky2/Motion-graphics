# Bench Three, Part 1: script for approval (no render until the owner approves)

Midnight with Nancy, a new interactive series. It replaces the "Wrong Number" draft
(`out/horror/scripts_wrong_number_part1.md`, never approved). The owner said "Yes" on 28 Oct 2026 to my question
"Want me to turn "Wrong Number" into "Bench Three" for your approval".

It's built on the research report (`reports/Psychological death game design.md`) and keeps the premise from the
owner's reference Short: a huge temptation, an unseen texter, a clock, and a two-sided vote.

**What changed from the "Wrong Number" draft, and why** (the report's critique):
1. **One sender becomes two numbers, one digit apart** (···0141 and ···0142). The vote becomes a deduction viewers can
   argue: are they two people or one?
2. **The texts no longer contradict each other.** The draft had "I can see the envelope is gone" and "I never saw your
   face" together. Here every line the second number sends is literally true of the sender, which you only see on a
   second look.
3. **Both choices cost something stated in the script.** In the draft, keeping the money was safer and richer at the
   same time.
4. **"You" isn't given a decision or a backstory.** "You go anyway" and "your mom's medicine" are gone. You're broke
   only through an object on screen: a FINAL NOTICE taped to your door.
5. **The clues stay on screen longer.** The pen on the envelope's back is held about 1 s, and the flip cuts back to that
   frame.
6. **The vote mechanic is on the card only, not spoken.**
7. **"Five hundred hundred-dollar bills" is cut** (the Whisper check would flag the double "hundred").
8. **No title gives the twist away.**

**Format:** second person, about 60 s, about 160 words, a face or hands in every shot, the vote card held 2 s.

**Why both sides can be argued** (each has a cost, a moral pull and evidence on screen):
- **A, put it back.**
  - It isn't your money. Returning it is the honest thing, and it ends your part in this.
  - If the texter is one man, he already knows your face and your bench. Keeping his money only gives him a reason to
    come to your door.
  - The park is public, and you'd go before midnight.
- **B, keep it.**
  - The two numbers are one digit apart, and the photo is three weeks old. So the "wrong number" and the "witness"
    are probably one person, who planned this.
  - The only thing he wants is you, alone, at that bench tonight. Behind a locked door you're not where he planned.
  - The money pays the notice on your door.
- **The open question everyone will ask:** if it was planned, why pay $50,000 just to make you come?

**Clues on screen, never spoken:**
- The first text arrived at exactly 9:00:00, as if it was scheduled.
- The two numbers differ by one digit (···0141, ···0142).
- The photo shows someone on the next bench holding two phones.

**The truth sheet** (fixed before Part 1 posts, so both branches tell the same story; your call if you want another
truth):
- **One man owns both phones,** and a third one (···0143). The texts call him "a man who hurts people", and every line
  ···0142 sent is literally about the sender himself:
  - it's his money;
  - he hurts people;
  - he'll be at that bench at midnight;
  - "he never learns your name" is the only lie (he's known your face for three weeks).
- **For three weeks he has sat on the next bench at lunch,** watching who sits on bench three alone, every day, and
  who's broke (he has seen the notice on your door).
- **The money is bait that makes you come twice:** once out of greed (to take it), once out of fear (to return it).
  "Wrong number" makes taking it feel like luck, not a choice he made for you.
- **"Thank you for coming" was written before you came.** He always knew you would.
- **Why not just call the police?** The answer goes in Part 2's first lines: "And tell them what? That you took fifty
  thousand dollars from under a bench?" You're the one holding the cash.

**Both Part 2 openings, written now:**

  - On the next bench sits a man you've seen every lunch for three weeks. Two phones in his lap. Both light up.

- Each branch says the vote result in its first line, and both lead to the same man. The vote changes where you meet
  him, never whether your choice counted.
