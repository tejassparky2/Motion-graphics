# Wrong Number, Part 1: script for approval (no render until the owner approves)

Midnight with Nancy, a new interactive story. The owner, 28 Oct 2026, sent a reference Short (84 s, a man finds two
bags with $20 million, a hidden phone texts him, "keep one, bring the other back", deadline midnight, vote return or
keep) and asked: "I want story something like for clever psychological tension for Nancy story this is nothing to do
with dma it's for might night with nancy".

**What we take from the reference** (its structure, not its story):
- it opens on a huge temptation with a counter;
- the ordinary, rational moves come next (lock the door, the list of what the money fixes);
- an unseen watcher speaks only through texts and knows things they shouldn't;
- a deadline you can see, with the clock and the battery;
- it ends on a two-sided vote that decides Part 2.

**What's ours:**
- a wrong-number text instead of found bags;
- the bench is the one you sit on every day (they know your habits);
- a fair-play clue: the note on the back of the envelope is on screen for half a second at line 4, before the
  narrator reads it;
- a late flip, as in Tower 9: it was never a wrong number;
- the vote is a deduction with evidence on both sides.

Second person, ~60 s, ~175 words, a face in every shot, the vote card held 2 s.

**Why both sides can be argued:**
- **Put it back.** The angry man might be real, and returning the money ends your part in it. You'd go before
  midnight while there's still a crowd near the gate.
- **Keep it.** The note proves the texts are lying ("Who is this?"), and they want you back in the park alone at
  midnight. You're safer behind a locked door, and the money changes your life.
- **The open question:** why would anyone pay you $50,000 to come, and then demand it back?

**Clues on screen, never spoken:**
- the first text arrived at exactly 9:00:00, as if it was scheduled;
- the envelope is dry, but the ground under the bench is wet (it was put there minutes before you came).
