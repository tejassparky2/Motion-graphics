# Non-religion slots 75, 77, 79, 81: scripts for approval (29 Oct 2026)

These sit between the God videos (74, 76, 78, 80, 82). Built on the rules from 26 and 29 Oct (CLAUDE.md, from our
export and the Meanwhile Mayank deep dive):
- **Frame 0** is one subject alone on a plain background.
- **Hook text** has two tiers (small setup words + one big red key word, marked as it is spoken).
- **Line 1** is a character's line with the contradiction inside it.
- The **premise** comes out early and only the payoff stays hidden.
- There is a **visible change every 1-1.5 s**, densest in seconds 3-15.
- One **re-hook** question comes at ~60%.
- The **closing either/or question** has "you" in it.

Turbo stock female narrator with up to 1 sound tag on a few narrator lines; characters in their own Turbo voices.
About 40-45 s each. Nothing is rendered until you approve.

## 75. operation_mincemeat: "This Spy Fooled Hitler. He Was Already Dead. 💼"
Alt titles: "A Dead Man Changed World War Two 💼" / "Nobody Asked Him. He Saved Thousands. 💼"

**Frame 0:**
- One grey hand with a briefcase handcuffed to the wrist, alone on plain navy.
- Hook text: small "THE SPY WAS" + big red **"DEAD"**.
- A red circle draws round the handcuff as "dead man" is spoken.

**Characters:** a British officer (unnamed), a German officer, the narrator. Dramatized, real events.
**Outcome:** the trick works, then its cost comes out.

| # | Who | Line | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | British officer | We need a dead man. And he has to lie to Hitler. | Hand + briefcase, red circle on the cuff |
| 2 | Narrator | London, [nineteen forty three.\|1943.] British spies dress a dead man as an officer. Major William Martin. | Uniform drops onto a silhouette; ID card stamps "MAJOR W. MARTIN" |
| 3 | Narrator | His briefcase holds fake secret plans. Attack Greece. Not Sicily. | Map: red arrow to Greece, Sicily crossed out |
| 4 | Narrator | A submarine leaves him floating off the coast of Spain. | Periscope, splash, the body drifts to a beach |
| 5 | German officer | The plans are real. Move the troops! | Tanks slide on the map toward Greece |
| 6 | Narrator | Hitler believes it. In July, the Allies land in Sicily. | Flags pop onto Sicily |
| 7 | Narrator (re-hook, ~60%) | But who was the dead man? For fifty three years, nobody knew. | A blank gravestone, "?" |
| 8 | Narrator | Then a historian found his name. Glyndwr Michael. A poor Welsh man. Nobody had asked him. [sigh] | Name card appears on the gravestone |
| 9 | Narrator | So, would you let them use your body to save thousands? Or is that never okay? | Buttons **YES / NEVER** |

**Sources:**
- Ewen Montagu, *The Man Who Never Was* (1953).
- Ben Macintyre, *Operation Mincemeat* (2010).
- The body was released from HMS Seraph off Huelva on 30 April 1943.
- Roger Morgan identified Glyndwr Michael from Public Record Office files in 1996. His family disputes parts of the
  official account of his death, so the video doesn't say how he died.

**Voice risk:** "Glyndwr" (say GLIN-door). If the voice slurs it, I'll use "a poor Welsh man named Glyndwr Michael"
with the name on a card.

## 77. toxo_rat: "Her Rat Walked Straight Up to the Cat 🐀"
Alt titles: "This Germ Makes Rats Love Cats 🐀" / "Something Inside It Wants to Be Eaten 🐀"

**Frame 0:**
- One small rat alone on plain cream, walking toward two cat eyes at the edge of frame.
- Hook text: small "THIS RAT IS NOT" + big red **"AFRAID"**.
- AFRAID gets a red strike-through on "isn't brave".

**Characters:** a girl with her pet rat, a vet, the narrator. Setting: vet clinic.
**Outcome:** the parasite wins; the question turns to us.

| # | Who | Line | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Girl | Doctor, why does my rat walk straight up to our cat? | Rat on the table, nose toward a cat photo |
| 2 | Vet | Your rat isn't brave. Something inside it wants the cat to eat it. | Zoom into the rat's head, a tiny glowing blob |
| 3 | Narrator | It's a parasite called Toxoplasma. It can only breed inside a cat. | Parasite icon → arrow → cat's belly, "BREEDS ONLY HERE" |
| 4 | Narrator | So it changes the rat. The smell of a cat stops meaning danger. | Cat-smell wave reaches the rat: red ✗ on "DANGER" |
| 5 | Narrator | In [two thousand,\|2000,] Oxford scientists tested it. Healthy rats avoided cat smell. Infected rats stopped avoiding it. Some even went looking for it. | Two maze boxes: healthy rat runs away, infected rat walks in |
| 6 | Girl (re-hook, ~60%) | Wait. Can people catch it too? | Girl looks at her own hands |
| 7 | Vet | About one in three people carry it. Mostly from undercooked meat, or cat litter. | Counter "1 IN 3 PEOPLE", plate + litter box icons |
| 8 | Narrator | Scientists still argue whether it changes people at all. [gasp] | Two scientists with "?" between them |
| 9 | Narrator | So, if a germ can change what you want, is it still your choice? Or the germ's? | Buttons **MY CHOICE / THE GERM'S** |

**Sources:**
- Berdoy, Webster & Macdonald, "Fatal attraction in rats infected with Toxoplasma gondii", *Proc. R. Soc. B* (2000).
- About a third of people worldwide carry the parasite (estimates vary).
- Effects on human behaviour are disputed, so the video says so and claims none.

## 79. same_you: "Grandpa Says the Boy in This Photo Is Gone 📷"
Alt titles: "Is This Still You? Your Body Says No 📷" / "Most of You Is New. Except This. 📷"

**Frame 0:**
- One old photo of a little boy, held in a wrinkled hand, alone on plain warm grey.
- Hook text: small "IS THIS BOY" + big red **"GRANDPA?"**.
- A red circle is drawn round the boy's face as "me" is spoken.

**Characters:** Grandpa, his granddaughter, the narrator.
**Outcome:** reframe (both sides).

| # | Who | Line | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grandpa | This little boy is me. But most of his body isn't in me anymore. | The photo, red circle on the face |
| 2 | Girl | How? You're right here. | Pull back: Grandpa and the girl on a sofa |
| 3 | Narrator | Your red blood cells only live about four months. | A red blood cell with a 4-month calendar, then it pops |
| 4 | Narrator | The outer layer of your skin is replaced every few weeks. | Skin layers flake off and refill |
| 5 | Narrator | Even your bones are slowly taken apart and rebuilt. | A bone with tiny builders swapping pieces |
| 6 | Girl (re-hook, ~60%) | So is anything still the same? | She holds the old photo up next to his face |
| 7 | Narrator | Yes. Most of the brain cells you think with are as old as you. Scientists proved it with carbon left over from old atom bomb tests. [gasp] | A glowing brain; a tiny mushroom cloud icon, "2005 STUDY" |
| 8 | Grandpa | So the boy's brain stayed. The rest of him moved out. | Grandpa taps his head, smiling |
| 9 | Narrator | So, are you still the person you were at five? Or someone new with the same name? | Buttons **SAME ME / SOMEONE NEW** |

**Sources:**
- Spalding, Bhardwaj, Buchholz, Druid & Frisén, "Retrospective birth dating of cells in humans", *Cell* (2005):
  carbon-14 from 1950s-60s bomb tests showed cortical neurons are as old as the person.
- Red blood cells live about 120 days.
- The skin's outer layer renews in weeks; bone is remodelled continuously.
- The popular "every cell is replaced every 7 years" claim is a myth, so the video doesn't use it.

## 81. ai_art: "She Won the Art Prize. A Computer Drew It. 🖼️"
Alt titles: "Is AI Art Really Yours? 🖼️" / "She Typed It. It Won. Whose Is It? 🖼️"

**Frame 0:**
- One painting with a gold "1ST PRIZE" ribbon, alone on a plain classroom wall.
- Hook text: small "1ST PRIZE. PAINTED BY" + big red **"AI"**.
- A red circle is drawn round the ribbon as "won" is spoken.

**Characters:** a girl, the art teacher, the class, the narrator. Not The Last Bencher: a new kid.
**Outcome:** open debate.

| # | Who | Line | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Girl | Ma'am, I won the art contest. But a computer drew it. Is the prize still mine? | The painting + ribbon, red circle |
| 2 | Narrator | She typed what she wanted. The AI painted it in seconds. | A laptop: typed words fly into a painting |
| 3 | Teacher | Then the computer is the artist. | Teacher points; "ARTIST: COMPUTER?" card |
| 4 | Girl | A film director never holds the camera. Is the film not theirs? | Clapperboard + director's chair pop in |
| 5 | Narrator | This really happened. In [twenty twenty two,\|2022,] an AI-made picture won first prize at a US state fair. | A fair ribbon stamps "2022" |
| 6 | Teacher (re-hook, ~60%) | So who owns it? | Class turns to the teacher |
| 7 | Narrator | The US Copyright Office said: not him. Not enough human work. He took it to court. | A rubber stamp "REFUSED", then a gavel |
| 8 | Narrator | Some say the idea is the art. Others say the skill is. [chuckle] | Two cards: **IDEA** vs **SKILL** |
| 9 | Narrator | So, if you only had the idea, is it your painting? Or the machine's? | Buttons **MINE / THE MACHINE'S** |

**Sources:**
- Jason Allen's "Théâtre D'opéra Spatial", made with Midjourney, won the digital-arts category at the 2022 Colorado
  State Fair.
- The US Copyright Office refused registration (December 2022). Its Review Board upheld that on 5 September 2023, as
  the human creative input was insufficient.
- Allen then sued in federal court.
- He says he used over 600 prompts; the video leaves that number out.
