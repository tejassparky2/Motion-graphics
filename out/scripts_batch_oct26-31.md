## 61. Bat-People on the Moon (~45 s) · funny history, Abhay narration style
**Title:** 1835 News: Bat-People Found on the Moon 🌙
**Frame 0:** a newspaper front page "LIFE ON THE MOON!" with bat-winged people; big text "1835 BREAKING NEWS"

1. Eighteen thirty-five. A New York newspaper prints the biggest news in history. There is life. On the moon.
2. And not just germs, bro. Unicorns. Beavers walking on two legs. And people. With bat wings. [laugh]
3. Who found them? A famous astronomer, with a giant new telescope. Very scientific. Very serious.
4. New York goes crazy. Everyone wants a copy. The paper is selling faster than it can print.
5. Just one tiny problem. The astronomer was real. But he was busy in South Africa, and had no idea any of this was printed in his name. [chuckle]
6. The whole thing was made up. In an office. Bat-people. Invented by a writer with a deadline.
7. Bro invented clickbait almost two hundred years ago. Before the internet. Before the light bulb.
8. Today it's called the Great Moon Hoax.
9. So who's really to blame for fake news? The people who make it, or the people who share it?

**Facts:** The Sun (New York), six articles from 25 Aug 1835, credited to Sir John Herschel, who was observing at the
Cape of Good Hope; man-bats, unicorns and two-legged beavers are in the articles; the paper's sales jumped. Writer
usually credited: Richard Adams Locke (said as "a writer", since it's not certain).

## 66. They Put a Dead Pope on Trial (~48 s) · Halloween funny history, Abhay narration style
**Title:** They Put a Dead Pope on Trial in 897 💀
**Frame 0:** a candlelit court, a skeleton in robes on a throne; big text "THE DEFENDANT IS DEAD"

1. Eight ninety-seven. Rome. The court is packed. And the man on trial has been dead for nine months.
2. This is Pope Formosus. Bro was already buried. And the new pope, Stephen, said: dig him up.
3. They dress the corpse in full robes. And sit him on a throne. In court. [gasp]
4. Now you're thinking, how does a dead man answer questions? Easy. They make a deacon stand next to him, and answer for him.
5. Imagine being that guy. Your job today: talk for a corpse. [laugh]
6. The verdict? Guilty. Obviously. He didn't say one word in his defence. [chuckle]
7. They cut off the three fingers he blessed people with. And throw him in the river.
8. But plot twist. A few months later, Rome turns on Stephen. He's thrown in prison. And never comes out.
9. History calls it the Cadaver Synod. The creepiest court case ever.
10. But here's the real question. We judge dead people all the time, in history books. Is that fair, or not?

**Facts:** Cadaver Synod, January 897; Formosus died April 896; a deacon answered for him; found guilty, his acts
annulled, the three blessing fingers of his right hand cut off, body thrown into the Tiber; Stephen VI was deposed
and strangled in prison later in 897 ("never comes out" keeps it ad-friendly).
