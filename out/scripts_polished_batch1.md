## Video 2 (Sat Oct 17, 4:30 AM): Paradox, "Achilles and the Tortoise"

**Title:** A Tortoise Beats The Fastest Runner Alive?! 🐢 (Zeno's Paradox)

| # | Line | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Greek thinker once proved that the fastest runner alive can never catch a tortoise. | Achilles in sandals and helmet sprinting; tortoise strolling; "IMPOSSIBLE?" stamp |
| 2 | His name was Zeno. And the runner was Achilles, the fastest hero in Greek stories. | Zeno with a scroll; Achilles flexing; a "SWIFT-FOOTED" tag |
| 3 | Here's the race. Achilles runs ten meters every second. The tortoise runs one. | Speed cards: 10 m/s vs 1 m/s |
| 4 | So the tortoise gets a hundred meter head start. | A race track with a 100 m ruler; the tortoise waves from the front |
| 5 | Achilles runs those hundred meters. That takes him ten seconds. | Achilles dashes; timer counts 0 to 10 s |
| 6 | But in ten seconds, the tortoise moved ten meters. It's still ahead. | The tortoise sits 10 m further; "STILL AHEAD" sign |
| 7 | Achilles runs those ten meters. One second. | Timer goes +1 s |
| 8 | The tortoise moved one more meter. Still ahead. | Tortoise 1 m ahead, smug |
| 9 | Every time Achilles reaches where the tortoise was, it has moved a little further. | Steps shrink: 100, 10, 1, 0.1 … |
| 10 | The steps never end. So Zeno said, Achilles never catches it. | Infinite stack of steps; Achilles exhausted |
| 11 | Sounds right? Here's the trick. Add up the times. | Calculator card |
| 12 | Ten seconds. Plus one. Plus a tenth. Plus a hundredth. | Counter adds up: 10, 11, 11.1, 11.11 |
| 13 | The steps never end. But the total never even reaches eleven point two seconds. | Counter crawls but stops below an "11.2" line |
| 14 | So after about eleven seconds, Achilles is level. Then he zooms past. | Achilles overtakes; the tortoise's hat flies off |
| 15 | Endless steps. But a short race. | Two cards: ∞ steps = 11.1 s |
| 16 | So, did Zeno fool you? Yes, or no? | Yes / No buttons |

*Check of the numbers:* after 100/9 ≈ 11.11 s, Achilles is at 111.1 m and the tortoise at 100 + 11.1 = 111.1 m.
Every number above comes from line 3.

*Sources:* Aristotle, *Physics* VI.9 (Zeno's four paradoxes); [Britannica: Achilles paradox](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Achilles-paradox);
[Wikipedia: Zeno's paradoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno%27s_paradoxes). Zeno lived in the 5th century BC.
Achilles is "swift-footed" in Homer's *Iliad*.
*Mishearing risk:* "Zeno" may be heard as "Xeno" (same sound; the caption shows Zeno).

**Title:** Mark Twain Had To Tell The Papers He Wasn't Dead. Twice. 💀

*Sources:*
- 1897 note (dated May 31, published in the New York Journal on June 2, 1897), with the exact words:
  [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/literature/authors/reports-mark-twains-quote-about-mark-twains-death-are-greatly-exaggerated)
  and [WIST](https://wist.info/twain-mark/51247/). The "grossly exaggerated" version comes from Paine's 1912 biography.
- 1907: [NYT, May 4, 1907, "Twain and Yacht Disappear at Sea"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times/Twain_and_yacht_disappear_at_sea)
  and [NYT, May 5, 1907, "Mark Twain Investigating"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times/Mark_Twain_Investigating),
  which has the quote word for word and says the yacht "slipped out of Hampton Roads during a fog" while Twain "was safe in his rooms in Fifth Avenue".
- He died on April 21, 1910.

*Mishearing risk:* "apprise" could be heard as "a prize". It's his real word, so I'd keep it; if Whisper flags it,
the caption still shows "apprise". In line 6, his original semicolon becomes a full stop so the voice pauses.

**Title:** This Jellyfish Can Turn Back Into A Baby. Again And Again. 🪼

*Sources:*
- [Wikipedia: Turritopsis dohrnii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turritopsis_dohrnii): it reverts to a polyp when stressed, injured, sick or old, by transdifferentiation, and can still be killed by predators and disease.
- [Texas A&M](https://stories.tamu.edu/news/2021/03/12/this-jellyfish-can-defy-death-and-turn-back-time/).
- Size 4.5 mm: Nathaniel Rich, NYT Magazine, Nov 2012 ([excerpt](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=20183)).
- Ten reversals in two years: Shin Kubota, Kyoto University, *Biogeography* 13 (2011) ([PDF](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dspace/bitstream/2433/179209/1/Biogeography_13_101.pdf)).

*Left out on purpose:* the claim that "it lives forever". The 1988 discovery is also out; it's true, but it slows the hook.
*Mishearing risk:* none expected. The video never says "Turritopsis"; it appears only in the description.
