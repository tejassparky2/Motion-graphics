About 1 in 4 people sneeze when they step into bright sunlight. Here's why, in 3 steps. ☀️🤧

1. Bright light hits your eyes, and a nerve carries the signal to your brain.

It's called the photic sneeze reflex, and it often runs in families. The exact cause isn't proven yet. And never stare at the sun.

Sources: Cleveland Clinic, "Photic sneeze reflex (ACHOO syndrome)"; Eriksson et al., genome-wide association study of the photic sneeze reflex (PLoS Genetics, 2010).

```
Wrinkly bath fingers aren't just water soaking in. Your nerves do it on purpose. 3 steps. 🫧

1. When your fingers stay in water, a nerve signal is sent.
2. Tiny blood vessels in the fingertips squeeze, so there's less tissue under the skin.

**Description**
```
Your heart makes its own electricity. Every beat, in 3 steps. ⚡❤️

1. A tiny spot called the SA node fires an electric spark.
2. The spark spreads across the top chambers, and they squeeze.
3. It pauses in the middle (the AV node), then spreads down, and the big chambers pump blood out.

That's why a transplanted heart still beats, even with its nerves cut. Your nerves only speed it up or slow it down. At rest, it's about 60 to 100 beats a minute.

Sources: Cleveland Clinic, "Heart conduction system"; Medical News Today, "The heart's natural pacemaker".

Why do you yawn when someone else yawns? 3 steps. 🥱

About half of adults catch yawns. It spreads most between friends and family, and even dogs catch human yawns. Scientists still debate exactly why it happens.

Sources: Brown et al., "A neural basis for contagious yawning", Current Biology (2017); Norscia & Palagi, PLoS ONE (2011); Joly-Mascheroni et al., Biology Letters (2008).
