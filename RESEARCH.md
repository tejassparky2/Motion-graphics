# Sparky3dcraft Mini Me ad: research brief and creative strategy

This brief explains why each second of the ad is built the way it is. Every factual claim below links to where it came from. Anything I could not verify is marked as unverified.

> **Research limits (honest note):** Reddit blocks the research tools used here. Both direct Reddit access and Reddit-filtered search returned errors, so **no Reddit threads were read**. For customer voice I used Trustpilot reviews of figurine sellers, the Snapmaker and Bambu Lab forums, and hands-on reviews of the U1 instead. If you want the Reddit angle, search r/3Dprinting, r/snapmaker and r/EtsySellers for "custom figurine" and look for the complaints listed in section 2.

## 1. Facts about the Snapmaker U1 (verified; the only printer claims used in the video)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| 4 toolheads, each with its own nozzle for one filament; direct swaps in about 5 s | [Snapmaker official U1 page](https://www.snapmaker.com/en/snapmaker-u1) |
| "Up to 85% less waste" than purge-based multicolour | [Snapmaker official U1 page](https://www.snapmaker.com/en/snapmaker-u1) |
| Each nozzle only prints its own filament, so there is no flushing between colours, which cuts waste and the risk of **colour bleed** | [Snapmaker US product page](https://us.snapmaker.com/products/snapmaker-u1-3d-printer) |
| It still uses a **small prime tower** (shown in the animation for accuracy) | [Snapmaker US product page](https://us.snapmaker.com/products/snapmaker-u1-3d-printer) |
| 270 × 270 × 270 mm build volume, CoreXY, up to 500 mm/s | [Snapmaker official U1 page](https://www.snapmaker.com/en/snapmaker-u1) |
| A reviewer printed a figurine with about 12 colours plus 2 mixed shades on the U1 by splitting it into sections | [3DWithUs hands-on](https://3dwithus.com/snapmaker-u1-hands-on-toolchanging-3d-printer) |
| Most-funded 3D printer on Kickstarter: US$20,161,265 from 20,206 backers | [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/snapmaker-u1-becomes-most-funded-3d-printer-in-kickstarter-history-244988/), [Manufactur3D](https://manufactur3dmag.com/snapmaker-u1-3d-printer-project-kickstarter/) |

**Why this matters to a buyer:** purge-based multicolour systems can leave dark filament in the nozzle, which shows up as grey streaks in white or light areas ([Bambu Lab forum thread](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/ams-multi-color-print-with-black-white-colors-messed-up/8050)). On a Mini Me, a grey smudge on a face is the one flaw everyone notices. That is why the ad says "crisp colour edges – no muddy mixing".

**Deliberately left out:** Kickstarter numbers, speed and waste percentages. They matter to printer buyers, not to someone buying a gift. The printer in the animation is a stylised drawing, not the real product design or logo. The brand is named only in text, as a factual "printed on" statement.

## 2. What buyers of custom figurines fear (from real reviews)

Recurring complaints in Trustpilot reviews of figurine-from-photo sellers:

- **"It doesn't look like the preview or the photo."** ([KibiFig](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/kibifig.com), [Piknico](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/piknico.com))
- **Wrong or muddy colours**, for example both people in a couple printed in the same skin tone ([My3dfigure](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/my3dfigure.com))
- **Bait and switch**: a 3D statue shown, a flat cut-out delivered ([Piknico](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/piknico.com))
- Competitors' buyer guides stress that stylised "art toy" proportions look better than hyper-realistic faces, which can feel uncanny, and that a **digital preview before printing** is the main thing buyers check for ([SnapFigures buyer guide](https://snapfigures.com/blogs/news/custom-figurines-buyers-guide-2026). This is a competitor's marketing, so weigh it lightly.)
- Sellers who do well stress communication, getting details like skin tone and eye colour right, and sending proofs ([Etsy market pages](https://www.etsy.com/market/custom_3d_printed_figurine))

**How the ad answers them:**

| Fear | Answer in the video |
|---|---|
| "Will it look like me?" | Scene 3 shows the photo turning into a sculpt, and scene 5 calls out "your hairstyle / your outfit / your smile" |
| "Will the colours be muddy?" | Scene 4 shows 4 separate toolheads printing layer by layer, with "crisp colour edges" |
| "Is it a real 3D figure?" | The printing scene shows it being built in 3D, and your real photos (scene 5b) prove it |
| "Is it complicated?" | Scene 3 has only 2 steps: "Send us one photo", "We sculpt your Mini Me" |

## 3. The psychology behind each scene

| Time | Scene | What it uses | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **"Wait… is that YOU?"** with a Mini Me popping in | **Pattern interrupt plus self-reference.** Meta-focused guides agree that the first 3 seconds decide whether people keep watching, so the ad opens on action and a question. People pay more attention to anything about themselves. | [Coinis – first 3 seconds](https://coinis.com/how-to/first-3-seconds-facebook-video-ad), [LeadsBridge – Meta best practices](https://leadsbridge.com/blog/meta-ads-best-practices/) |
| 0–33 s | Big head, big low-set eyes, round cheeks and body | **Baby schema (Kindchenschema).** Lorenz's "cute" features trigger attention and a caring response. In product-design studies, cuter designs raise purchase intent and make people more forgiving of flaws. | [Baby schema overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_schema), [IJDesign – baby-schema effects in product design](https://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1023/363), [Nittono & Ihara 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244017709321) |
| 3–7 s | "Your best moments… are stuck in your camera roll." | **Emotional problem and tangibility.** Turns thousands of unseen photos into a small, gentle loss. The product is the fix: a memory you can hold. | Creative reasoning (not a cited study) |
| 7–11 s | 1 photo → 3D sculpt | **Low effort, easy to follow.** Two simple steps make the purchase feel easy. | Creative reasoning |
| 11–19 s | Multicolour print with 4 toolheads swapping | **Visual proof and "oddly satisfying" process footage.** Showing how it's made builds trust and holds attention. Each colour lines up with its own toolhead, so the claim is shown, not just stated. | Supported by the verified U1 facts in section 1 |
| 19–24 s | Reveal with "your hairstyle / your outfit / your smile" | **Personalisation cues.** Repeating "your" keeps the viewer picturing themselves. | Creative reasoning |
| 24–28 s | "A gift that says 'I see you.'" plus occasions | **Vicarious pride in customised gifts.** A 2024 study (Univ. of Bath, emlyon, SKEMA, USI) found that customised gifts raised the recipient's appreciation and self-esteem, and that recipients felt "vicarious pride" in what the giver made. | [University of Bath press release](https://www.bath.ac.uk/announcements/personalised-gifts-create-lasting-emotional-connections-and-enhance-self-esteem-new-research/), [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/12/241218132144.htm) |
| 28–33 s | Brand, one call to action, the waving Mini Me from the opening | **Peak-end rule and closure.** It ends on a warm, happy moment and brings back the figure from the first question. There is **one** call to action, so the viewer has nothing to decide between. | Creative reasoning |

**Format decisions:**

- **9:16 vertical, 1080×1920.** Covers Reels, Stories and mobile Feed ([Benly – Meta video ads guide](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/video-ads-guide)).
- **Works with the sound off.** Every idea appears as on-screen text, because a large share of feed video plays muted ([Brandwatch](https://www.brandwatch.com/blog/facebook-video-ads-best-practices/)).
- **Safe zones.** Key text sits between about y=290 and y=1500. That keeps it clear of the Reels top bar and the bottom caption/buttons area (~250 px top, ~420 px bottom) ([Outfy safe-zone guide](https://www.outfy.com/blog/instagram-safe-zone/)), and inside the centre 4:5 crop Meta uses in Feed.
- **Music.** Original, synthesised by `scripts/make_music.py` (no licence issues). It is 120 BPM, so scene cuts land on the beat, and it adds printer "ticks" on every tool swap. You can also swap in a trending track from Meta's Sound Collection in Ads Manager.

## 4. Claims to check before running the ad

These lines describe **your** service. Change them in `src/config.ts` if they don't match what you do:

1. **"Colour printed in – not painted on"**: true only if you don't hand-paint.
2. **"Send us one photo"**: change it if you need several angles.
3. **"We sculpt your Mini Me"**: fine if you (or a partner) make the 3D model.
4. **`showPreviewApproval`** is **off**. Turn it on **only** if you really show customers a 3D preview to approve. It is the strongest answer to the #1 buyer fear, so if you can offer it, do.

## 5. How to get the most from this ad (Meta / Instagram)

1. **Add real proof.** This is the biggest improvement you can make. Put 1–3 photos of real finished Mini Mes in `public/photos/`, list them in `realPhotos` in `src/config.ts`, and re-render. A "Real prints from our workshop" scene is inserted automatically and the music re-syncs. The animation shows the idea; your real photos prove it.
2. **Test several opening hooks.** Guides recommend running 3–4 hook variants per ad set and letting the algorithm pick a winner ([LeadsBridge](https://leadsbridge.com/blog/meta-ads-best-practices/)). Change `hookSmall`/`hookBig` in the config, for example:
   - "Your photo → a tiny YOU"
   - "The gift they'll actually keep"
   - "We 3D print people. In colour."
3. **Pair it with a real clip.** A 5–10 s phone video of the U1 printing a real figure, posted as a second ad, adds user-generated-content style proof. Meta guides report this style often performs well.
4. **Seasonal timing.** Gifting is the main emotional driver, so push the ad before birthdays season peaks, Mother's/Father's Day, Valentine's Day, weddings and Christmas.
