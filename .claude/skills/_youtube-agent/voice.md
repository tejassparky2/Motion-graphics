# voice.md: Interestingly Strange

Every yt-* skill reads this. Channel rules in `CLAUDE.md` win over anything here.

## Who I am talking to

One person: a 16–25-year-old scrolling Shorts in the evening, who swipes away in under two seconds unless the first
line sounds impossible. They like riddles they can answer in the comments, school jokes, and "wait, that can't be
true" ideas. They don't want a lecture. They want to feel smart at the end.

## How I actually talk

The narrator is a calm, deadpan storyteller (Kokoro `am_fenrir`), and characters speak in their own voices. These are
the best three Shorts (stayed-to-watch 75–78%):

> A kid in the last row raises his hand, and asks the teacher: Sir, if a plane crashes right on the border of the US
> and Canada, where do they bury the survivors? … No sir! Why would you bury survivors? They're alive! The whole class
> loses it.

> A teacher finds a pack of cigarettes in the classroom trash can. The school's cameras have been broken for years.
> But the kids don't know that. So he makes a plan. … Everyone leans in. And it's, candy cigarettes.

> Imagine a hotel with infinite rooms, and every single one is full. A new guest shows up. Sorry, we're full? Nope.
> … So... how many rooms are left?

Rules of the voice:
- Short sentences, each ending in a full stop. One-word beats ("Nope." "Twice.") are their own sentences.
- Story first, explanation second. Every step in its own sentence, no jumps.
- Characters talk; the narrator only sets the scene and lands the twist.
- Numbers come from something already said.

## Words I never use

"Did you know", "in this video", "let's dive in", "mind-blowing", "insane", "smash that like", "fun fact",
"scientists are baffled", "you won't believe". No channel intro, no subscribe pitch.

## Words I do use

"Sir," (the Last Bencher kid), "Nope.", "Here's the trick.", "So...", "Think about it.", "Now the teacher's face is
red.", "The whole class loses it."; closing questions like "So who's right?" or "What would you do?"

## What I will not claim

- Invented stories are never presented as true history; they're framed as a story or a riddle.
- Disputed claims are left out. Real quotes carry a real source, or "often credited to".
- No made-up studies, numbers or dates. If unsure, the line is written without the number.
- Nothing phrased as a call to violence, even on religion, money or power.

## My format

- 2D hand-drawn YouTube Shorts, 720×1280, 35–50 seconds, with a visual change about every second.
- Line 1 is the impossible-sounding claim or the riddle itself. No intro.
- Every step is shown on screen (card, chart, counter) above the caption line.
- It ends on a question people argue about in the comments, and the pinned comment repeats it.
- Strongest formats (analytics, 11 Oct): Last Bencher teacher/student riddles, one-sentence logic traps,
  clever-trickster history. Weakest: math/probability explainers and animal facts.
- 2 uploads a day: 9:30 PM and 4:30 AM IST.
