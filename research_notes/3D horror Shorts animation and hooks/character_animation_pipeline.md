## 1. Rigged character models that work with Quaternius UAL: licences, rig compatibility, face blendshapes

### Takeaway
The only free human bodies that play UAL clips with no retargeting are Quaternius's own UAL2 mannequins and Universal Base Characters (UBC). They share UAL2's 65-joint UE5-style skeleton, but neither shows any sign of facial blendshapes, so faces still have to be built by hand. For readable fear and lip-sync on a real face, the best legal option is MPFB/MakeHuman humans (CC0 core assets, add-on ARKit "face units" and Oculus viseme packs), retargeted to UAL. CC0 VRMs (100Avatars) are a lighter choice but carry only A/E/I/O/U and Blink shapes, with no emotions. Ready Player Me is gone (shut down 31 Jan 2026), and ActorCore free-content terms for video could not be verified.

### Inferences
- Quickest route to good bodies: put UBC (free Standard: 2 bodies) or the UAL2 mannequin on the UAL2 skeleton. All UAL2 clips play natively. UAL1 clips need a one-time Rigify-to-UE name map plus a rest-pose check (§3).
- Faces: none of UBC, UAL mannequins, Kenney or Mixamo has blendshapes. Keep our procedural face kit, now parented to the `Head` bone: decal or texture eyes, brows and mouth driven from code. For realistic faces with ARKit-style fear (browInnerUp, eyeWide, mouthStretch) and Oculus visemes, MPFB + faceunits01 + visemes02 is the only CC0-core option found. Because the face packs' licence is unstated, confirm it before use.
- Licence risk is low for all CC0/QAL Quaternius assets. Both CC0 and QAL explicitly allow a monetized video "Product"; QAL only bans redistributing the raw assets. Keep the `License.txt` files with the download date as evidence of which licence applied.

Mixamo characters and animations can be used royalty-free in commercial videos with no credit required. The only bans are redistributing the raw character or animation files and (per the FAQ) training ML models. The service is free with an Adobe ID but unmaintained and has had outages, so download what we need and archive it rather than depending on it live.

## 3. Retargeting clips onto other skeletons in three.js: SkeletonUtils pitfalls, fixes, Blender alternative

### Takeaway
`SkeletonUtils.retargetClip` copies each source bone's world orientation onto the target. It only works directly when both rigs have the same bone-axis conventions and a matching rest pose; otherwise you hand-tune `localOffsets` per bone, as the official example does. The proven alternative for humanoids is three-vrm's rest-pose-normalized formula (`parentRestWorld × q × restWorld⁻¹`), or retargeting in Blender and exporting baked glTF. Main traps: A-pose vs T-pose, cm vs m scale, root motion stored on `root` rather than the pelvis, and unmapped finger bones.

### Cited Findings
- r169 `retarget()` options and defaults: `hip: 'hip'`, `hipInfluence: Vector3(1,1,1)`, `scale: 1`, `hipPosition`, `preserveBoneMatrix: true`, `preserveBonePositions: true`, `useTargetMatrix: false`, `localOffsets` (map bone name to Matrix4), `names` (target-to-source map) or `getBoneName(bone)`. `retargetClip` adds `fps`, `trim: [start,end]` and `useFirstFramePosition`. The algorithm calls `target.skeleton.pose()`, then for each target bone takes the source bone's world matrix relative to the target root (`relativeMatrix.copy(target.matrixWorld).invert(); relativeMatrix.multiply(boneTo.matrixWorld)`), strips scale, applies `localOffsets`, and converts back to local space. It is **not** a rest-pose delta. `retargetClip` samples the source mixer frame by frame and writes `.bones[name].quaternion` tracks plus a hip position track. — [SkeletonUtils.js r169](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js)
- History: `getBoneName()`, `hipInfluence` and `trim` were added 16 Sep 2024 (#29416, #29423, #29424, by sunag). The `webgpu_animation_retargeting` example was added 6 Sep 2024 (#29338). "fix retargetClip duration and last frame (#27653)" landed 1 Feb 2024. After 2024 there have only been JSDoc commits (through 4 Apr 2026). — [three.js commit history for SkeletonUtils.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/commits/dev/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js)
- The official example (Mixamo "Michelle", animated, onto Mixamo-rigged "Soldier") needs hand-tuned per-bone `localOffsets`, e.g. `mixamorigLeftArm: rotateCW45`, `mixamorigRightArm: rotateCCW180`, legs `rotateCW180`, feet `Euler(45°,180°,0)`. It also uses `hip: 'mixamorigHips'`, `scale: 1 / targetModel.scene.scale.y`, and a comment saying to "Apply the mixer directly to the SkinnedMesh, not any ancestor node". — [webgpu_animation_retargeting.html](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/examples/webgpu_animation_retargeting.html)
- The Mixamo FBX to Ready Player Me example needs only `getBoneName` plus `scale: .01`, with the comment "mixamo use centimeters, readyplayer.me use meters". Same-convention rigs work with a name map alone. — [webgpu_animation_retargeting_readyplayer.html](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/examples/webgpu_animation_retargeting_readyplayer.html). Both examples are in r169's `files.json`. — [r169 files.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/files.json)
- Known failures: retargeting between two identical Mixamo YBots gives "inverted feet, backward hands" (Aug 2023). — [forum 54892](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/skeletonutils-retarget-doesnt-work-with-mixamorig-skeleton-inverted-feet-backward-hands/54892). Retargeting Michelle onto a second copy of Michelle fails the sanity check. sunag's diagnosis (9 May 2024): "The retarget() is not being able to deal with the bind matrices of glTF". His proposed fix used bind (T-pose) matrices to compute per-bone offsets, but no merged fix appears in the thread. — [forum 65149](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/fixing-skeletonutils-retarget-and-retargetclip-functions/65149). "if the target skeleton has bones with different roll or orientation, the rotation will not work as expected". — [forum 45895](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/retargeting-issues/45895). A target with fewer bones than the source twists (Oct 2024). — [forum 73410](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/retargetclip-twists-character/73410)
- three-vrm's Mixamo-to-VRM loader computes `parentRestWorldRotation × trackQuat × restRotationInverse` (from `getWorldQuaternion(...).invert()`), scales hip position tracks by `vrmHipsHeight / motionHipsHeight`, and for VRM 0.x negates the x/z quaternion and position components. — [three-vrm loadMixamoAnimation.js](https://github.com/pixiv/three-vrm/blob/dev/packages/three-vrm/examples/humanoidAnimation/loadMixamoAnimation.js). Alan_Kent posted the same idea in the forum. — [forum 65149](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/fixing-skeletonutils-retarget-and-retargetclip-functions/65149)
- Hands-on report retargeting Quaternius UAL (65 bones, true T-pose) onto a 213-bone CC4-style A-pose rig in three.js (1 May 2026). Steps: (1) a bone-name map; (2) force the target arms into a T-pose to capture its bind, because otherwise shoulders and elbows over-rotate about 30°; (3) scale position tracks separately, horizontal by limb ratio and vertical by hip-height ratio (a single ratio was about 12% off). Root motion lives on `root`, not `pelvis`, so sum both into the hip track. Mapping `root` (Y=0) caused NaNs. Unmapped fingers stay in bind pose until finger bones are added (30 bones). A constant 2.5° hand offset was harmless. — [Cinevva blog part 17 (vendor)](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-05-01-open-world-browser-part-17-animations-and-search)
- Blender route: import the animation, retarget in Blender (Rokoko add-on is commonly used), and "export an animated glTF file and import it via GLTFLoader". — [forum 21898](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/retargeting-a-bvh-animation/21898); search summary of [forum 6172](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/retargeting-animation-to-mixamo-rig/6172)
- Ossos (MIT, sketchpunklabs) offers "IK Animation Retargeting using IK Rigs" and "Basic Animation Retargeting for similar skeletal meshes", with three.js examples. Its activity date is unknown. — [sketchpunklabs/ossos](https://github.com/sketchpunklabs/ossos)
- three-vrm's `humanoidAnimation` example and the Open Source Avatars `integrations/animationLoader.js` + `mixamo-rig-map` (tested on VRM 0.x only; Mixamo FBX clip named `"mixamo.com"`). — [vrm-gotchas.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ToxSam/open-source-avatars/main/integrations/vrm-gotchas.md)

### Inferences
- For our pipeline the most robust approach is an offline "bake" script in headless Chromium or Node. Load the source clip and the target, sample at 30 fps with the three-vrm formula (rest-pose normalized, using each rig's T-pose from the `A_TPose` clip or bind pose), then save the result as a glTF clip once. Render-time code just plays baked clips.
- Use `mixer.setTime(frame/30)` per frame, not wall-clock deltas, so frame-by-frame SwiftShader renders are deterministic.
- UAL1 Rigify names to UAL2 UE names is a straightforward 1:1 map for body bones (`DEF-upper_arm.L` → `upperarm_l`, `DEF-shin.L` → `calf_l`, etc.). Rest-axis conventions differ between Rigify and UE exports, so expect to need the rest-normalized formula, not bare `retargetClip`.

### Gaps
- No upstream fix to SkeletonUtils' bind-matrix problem was found as of the latest commit (Apr 2026).
- I found no documented headless Blender + Rokoko retargeting recipe. Blender's built-in tools and scripts are untested here.

## 4. VRM + @pixiv/three-vrm: expressions, visemes, licences of VRM sources, playing Mixamo/Quaternius clips

### Takeaway
three-vrm 3.5.5 (MIT, 9 Jul 2026, peer `three >=0.137`, so it works with r169) gives 5 emotion presets, 5 visemes, blink and lookAt through `vrm.expressionManager.setValue(name, weight)`. There is no "fear" preset, so fear has to be a custom expression or a mix of presets. VRoid Studio exports can be used in monetized videos ("generate profit from videos"), but pixiv's own base content "is not CC0". Truly CC0 VRMs exist (100Avatars, Halloween Rising and others via Open Source Avatars), though some carry only A/E/I/O/U/Blink. Mixamo clips play on VRMs via three-vrm's official example; Quaternius UAL would need the same treatment with a UAL-to-VRM bone map.

### Cited Findings
- Preset names in the source: `aa, ih, ou, ee, oh, blink, happy, angry, sad, relaxed, lookUp, surprised, lookDown, lookLeft, lookRight, blinkLeft, blinkRight, neutral`. — [VRMExpressionPresetName.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pixiv/three-vrm/dev/packages/three-vrm-core/src/expressions/VRMExpressionPresetName.ts)
- VRM 1.0 spec: emotions happy (was "joy"), angry, sad (was "sorrow"), relaxed (was "fun"), surprised (new in 1.0). Lip sync aa/ih/ou/ee/oh, blink/blinkLeft/blinkRight and lookUp/Down/Left/Right are "procedural". `overrideMouth / overrideBlink / overrideLookAt` = `none | block | blend`, which lets an emotion suppress lip sync or blinking. `isBinary`. Binds can be `morphTargetBinds`, `materialColorBinds` (color, emissionColor, shadeColor, matcapColor, rimColor, outlineColor) and `textureTransformBinds`. Custom expressions go under `expressions.custom` and cannot reuse preset names. — [VRM 1.0 expressions spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/expressions.md)
- npm `@pixiv/three-vrm` latest is 3.5.5 (published 2026-07-09), MIT, with peerDependency `three >=0.137`. ### Inferences
- Fear face on a VRM: VRM has no fear preset. The approximation is untested. VRoid-made avatars have many separate eye/brow/mouth morphs to build from; generic CC0 VRMs like 100Avatars do not.
- A VRoid avatar we build ourselves (not a pixiv sample) can be used in monetized videos under the guidelines, but its base assets aren't CC0, so we can't redistribute the model as an asset.
- MToon (VRM toon shader) is more shader work per pixel. On SwiftShader we could swap materials to MeshToon/MeshStandard if render time suffers (untested).

## 5. Offline CPU lip-sync from audio: Rhubarb and alternatives, driving three.js/VRM mouths

### Takeaway
Rhubarb Lip Sync (MIT, v1.14.0, Apr 2025) runs fine offline on our CPU. Its outputs are explicitly the user's own. On an 8.6 s clip, PocketSphinx mode took 14.4 s and phonetic mode 1.5 s. Since we always have the TTS script, pass it with `-d` for better accuracy. Its 6+3 mouth shapes map simply onto VRM aa/ih/ou/ee/oh or Oculus visemes. WhisperX (BSD-2, CPU int8) gives word timing via wav2vec2 alignment, and TalkingHead (MIT) shows word-to-viseme conversion for three.js.

### Cited Findings
- Licence: "Rhubarb Lip Sync is released under the MIT license." "When you run Rhubarb Lip Sync on an audio file, the resulting lip sync data belongs to you alone ... if you use Rhubarb Lip Sync in the production process of a video game, an animated cartoon, or a similar product that doesn't ship with lip sync functionality, you don't even have to care about the MIT license." — [LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync/master/LICENSE.md)
- Shapes: A (closed: P/B/M), B (slightly open, clenched teeth: most consonants, EE), C (open: EH/AE), D (wide: AA), E (slightly rounded: AO/ER), F (puckered: UW/OW/W), plus extended G (F/V), H (long L) and X (idle/rest). Recognizers: PocketSphinx (default, English only) or phonetic ("language-independent", less precise). `-d/--dialogFile` takes the known text: "It is always a good idea to specify the dialog text." Outputs are TSV, XML and JSON (`mouthCues` with start/end/value). Threads default to one per core. — [rhubarb-lip-sync README](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync)
- Latest release v1.14.0 (Spine/After Effects demo fixes). The tag page shows "03 Apr", and the binary inside the release zip is dated 2025-04-03. — [release v1.14.0](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync/releases/tag/v1.14.0), local zip
- **Local benchmark** (this container, 4 cores, Linux binary 1.14.0, 8.57 s narration `out/voiceover/sample_3d.wav`): PocketSphinx with `--extendedShapes GHX` took **14.4 s wall** (12.9 s user, so effectively one core, about 1.7× real time) and produced 45 cues (B 15, C 8, X 5, E 5, F 5, A 4, G 2, D 1). The phonetic recognizer took **1.5 s**. — local run, binary from [releases](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync/releases/download/v1.14.0/Rhubarb-Lip-Sync-1.14.0-Linux.zip)
- Widely copied Rhubarb-to-Oculus-viseme mapping (Wawa Sensei tutorial): `A→viseme_PP, B→viseme_kk, C→viseme_I, D→viseme_AA, E→viseme_O, F→viseme_U, G→viseme_FF, H→viseme_TH, X→viseme_PP`, applied by looking up the current `mouthCue` at the audio time and lerping `morphTargetInfluences`. — [wass08/r3f-lipsync-tutorial Avatar.jsx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wass08/r3f-lipsync-tutorial/main/src/components/Avatar.jsx)
- WhisperX: BSD-2-Clause, "Accurate word-level timestamps using wav2vec2 alignment", runs on CPU with `--compute_type int8 --device cpu`. Numbers and currency like "2014." or "£13.60" "cannot be aligned and therefore are not given a timing". — [m-bain/whisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)
- Montreal Forced Aligner gives phone-level forced alignment from transcript + audio (Kaldi-based). MIT licence per a third-party index (UNVERIFIED first-hand). It has no built-in viseme mapping. — [MFA user guide](https://montreal-forced-aligner.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/), [ecosyste.ms index](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fmontrealcorpustools%2Fmontreal-forced-aligner)
- TalkingHead (MIT, three.js): "The avatar must have a Mixamo-compatible rig and ARKit and Oculus viseme blend shapes". Word timestamps are converted to Oculus visemes by language modules (English among them). Moods: "neutral, happy, angry, sad, fear, disgust, love, and sleep". — [met4citizen/TalkingHead](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead)
- wawa-lipsync (MIT) does "real-time lipsyncing with JS and web audio API", using `connectAudio` and calling `processAudio` every frame. — [wass08/wawa-lipsync](https://github.com/wass08/wawa-lipsync)
- Kalidokit (MIT, MediaPipe face/pose to VRM, mouth `shape {A,E,I,O,U}`, blink, brow) is "officially deprecated". — [yeemachine/kalidokit](https://github.com/yeemachine/kalidokit)
- MPFB lip sync goes through the separate Blender "Lip Sync" extension, which was not yet updated for Blender 5.x as of May 2026. — [MPFB lip sync docs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/docs/lipsync.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended: run Rhubarb with `-d script.txt` once per narration line (PocketSphinx; about 1.7× real time single-core is acceptable for 40-50 s videos, and lines can run in parallel on 4 cores). Save the JSON next to the WAV and, in the page, sample the cue at `t = frame/30` with a 40-60 ms crossfade. Mapping to VRM: A/X→0; B→ih 0.3; C→ee/ih 0.6; D→aa 1.0; E→oh 0.7; F→ou 1.0; G→ih 0.2 (F/V); H→aa 0.4. To our home-made mouth kit: map A..X straight to 9 drawn mouth shapes. These numbers are a design proposal, untested.
- Use `-r phonetic` only for non-English (the Hindi channel) or quick previews.
- Real-time Web Audio analysers (wawa-lipsync) don't fit a frame-stepped headless render, because audio isn't actually playing in step with frames. Precomputed cues are deterministic.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy comparison of Rhubarb vs WhisperX+viseme mapping on TTS voices. Phoneme-level output from WhisperX (vs word-level) isn't documented.

## 6. Markerless motion capture from ordinary video on CPU

### Takeaway
Treat CPU-only video mocap as a source of rough blocking, not final animation. MediaPipe Pose (33 landmarks, runs on-device) is the realistic CPU option but jitters and needs smoothing plus a landmark-to-rotation solver. FreeMoCap (AGPL-3.0, CPU install path) admits a single camera "will not produce reliable 3D estimates"; it needs 2-3 calibrated cameras. The high-quality monocular methods (GVHMR, WHAM, 4D-Humans) need CUDA GPUs and the SMPL body model, whose licence is non-commercial research only, so they're out for a monetized channel without a commercial SMPL licence.

### Cited Findings
- MediaPipe Pose Landmarker: Lite/Full/Heavy bundles (float16), "33 3-dimensional pose landmarks" in image coordinates plus `WorldLandmarks`, optional segmentation mask, and modes IMAGE/VIDEO/LIVE_STREAM. It is "optimized for on-device, real-time fitness applications". The page gives no CPU latency numbers. — [Pose Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker). MediaPipe is Apache 2.0 (search summary). — [MediaPipe Python guide](https://developers.google.cn/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/python)
- FreeMoCap: licence AGPL-3.0, with CPU-only install `uv sync --no-default-groups --group cpu`. Its quickstart warns "It might break! Work in Progress lol". — [freemocap/freemocap](https://github.com/freemocap/freemocap). "Single-camera recordings will not produce reliable 3D estimates but will produce solid two-dimensional tracking, suitable for 2D animators." Reliable 3D needs "at least two cameras ... we recommend using three" plus a printed Charuco board (docs last modified 22 Apr 2026). — [FreeMoCap hardware requirements](https://docs.freemocap.org/documentation/software-hardware-prerequisites.html). After recording "it will pop up a Blender scene if Blender was properly detected". — [single-camera guide](https://docs.freemocap.org/documentation/single-camera-recording.html). Its tracking backend skellytracker bundles "MediaPipe, RTMPose, YOLOX, Aruco, Charuco". — [FreeMoCap docs index](https://docs.freemocap.org/). The Blender step produces keyframed empties and a Rigify-based armature (search summary). — [madenmud/freemocap fork notes](https://github.com/madenmud/freemocap)
- GVHMR licence: "for educational, research and non-profit purposes only. Any modification based on this work must be open-source and prohibited for commercial use." Install uses CUDA 12.1 and requires registering for SMPL and SMPL-X. — [GVHMR LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zju3dv/GVHMR/main/LICENSE), [INSTALL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zju3dv/GVHMR/main/docs/INSTALL.md)
- WHAM code is MIT but needs SMPL/SMPLify registration. 4D-Humans needs the SMPL neutral model and was "trained on 8 A100 GPUs for 7 days". — [WHAM LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yohanshin/WHAM/main/LICENSE), [WHAM README](https://github.com/yohanshin/WHAM), [4D-Humans README](https://github.com/shubham-goel/4D-Humans)
- SMPL model licence: "This software/data and the associated license is for academic research purposes. The software/data is also available for commercial licensing through Meshcapade.com." The title is "Software Copyright License for non-commercial scientific research purposes". — [SMPL model license](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)
- Practical reports: users report "significant jerking" in MediaPipe output from smooth source videos. The BlendArMocap (MediaPipe-in-Blender) author "cannot actively maintain it anymore". Smoothing by frame averaging is the usual fix. — [Blender Artists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/mediapipe-for-blender-python-programming/1312620), [BlendArMocap](https://github-redirect.dependabot.com/cgtinker/BlendArMocap), [mediapipe-pose-smooth](https://github.com/yousufkalim/mediapipe-pose-smooth)
- Survey (HKU, 2026): FreeMoCap "triangulates body positions from multiple camera angles" and is "mostly for recordings, not realtime". Non-open alternatives: DeepMotion "Free version tracks up to 2 people", Rokoko Vision "Free for up to 15 seconds". — [HKU bookstack: camera/AI mocap](https://bookstack.hku.nl/books/motion-capture-motion-tracking/page/camera-ai-based-mocap)
- Mocapy (MediaPipe to VRM bones, BVH export) exists. Its licence, maintenance and quality are UNVERIFIED. — [justinjohn0306/Mocapy](https://github.com/justinjohn0306/Mocapy)

### Inferences
- For short horror acting beats (flinch, look back, cover mouth, backing away), a phone video of the owner or a friend → MediaPipe world landmarks → smoothing → solve upper-body rotations → bake to the UAL2 skeleton is feasible on CPU. Expect to clean up by hand; legs and depth will be unreliable from one camera. UAL/Mixamo clips plus procedural layering (§7) are likely better value.
- FreeMoCap's AGPL covers the software, not the motion data it outputs (the AGPL is triggered by distributing or serving the software). This is my reading; not legal advice.

### Gaps
- No CPU latency benchmark for Pose Landmarker found. No independent 2025-26 Reddit experiences found (searches returned none).
- FreeMoCap's direct BVH/glTF export options are not documented on the pages I could fetch (export appears to go through Blender).

## 7. Procedural helpers for life-like idle motion (breathing, look-at, blinking, IK)

### Takeaway
three.js r169 already ships the building blocks: additive clip blending (`AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive`), `CCDIKSolver`, morph-target face examples and BVHLoader. three-vrm adds `VRMLookAt` and preset blink/viseme expressions. TalkingHead is an MIT reference for the whole set (eye contact, head movement, blinking, breathing, moods incl. fear). For deterministic frame renders, every procedural signal should be a pure function of the frame time.

### Cited Findings
- r169 examples include `webgl_animation_skinning_additive_blending`, `webgl_animation_skinning_ik`, `webgl_animation_skinning_morph`, `webgl_morphtargets_face`, `webgl_loader_bvh`, `webgpu_animation_retargeting` and `webgpu_animation_retargeting_readyplayer`. `examples/jsm/animation/CCDIKSolver.js` exists at r169. — [r169 files.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/files.json), [CCDIKSolver.js r169](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/jsm/animation/CCDIKSolver.js)
- three-vrm `VRMLookAt`: set `lookAt.target = object` with `autoUpdate = true`, or drive `yaw`/`pitch` directly (Euler order YXZ). — [VRMLookAt.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pixiv/three-vrm/dev/packages/three-vrm-core/src/lookAt/VRMLookAt.ts). Blink and gaze are "procedural" expressions, and emotion expressions can block or blend them via `overrideBlink` / `overrideLookAt`. — [VRM expressions spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/expressions.md)
- TalkingHead (MIT) implements eye contact and head movement while idle and speaking, idle behaviour, blinking, mood-driven loops including breathing, hand gestures, poses, and dynamic bones for hair. Its moods include "fear". — [met4citizen/TalkingHead](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead)
- Ossos (MIT) includes Bone Springs and IK animation additives. — [sketchpunklabs/ossos](https://github.com/sketchpunklabs/ossos)
- A common pattern (secondary skill-doc): find a bone with `skeleton.getBoneByName`, add a sine-based offset to its rotation each frame, and layer breathing as an additive clip via `AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive` + `AdditiveAnimationBlendMode`. — [threejs-animation skill doc (secondary)](https://playbooks.com/skills/cloudai-x/threejs-skills/threejs-animation)
- The VRM viewer docs say automatic blink competes with eye blendshapes ("use one or the other"). — [VRoid/VRM viewer doc](https://nishlumi-vrmviewmdoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/man2/operation_vrm2.html)

### Inferences
- A suggested horror idle layer stack (untested design):
  1. Base UAL clip via `mixer.setTime(t)`.
  2. Breathing: chest/spine_02 additive pitch of about 1.5° at 0.25 Hz calm, rising to 0.6-0.8 Hz with larger amplitude when scared.
  3. Head look-at toward the threat with a 150-250 ms lag (critically damped), then quick glances.
  4. Blinks from a seeded schedule (every 2-6 s, 120-150 ms close), suppressed during the fear stare.
  5. Fear face ramp (brows up and together, eyes wide, mouth stretched) with `overrideBlink: block`.
  6. Two-bone IK (CCDIKSolver) to pin a hand to a door handle or flashlight.
  Use a seeded noise function of `t`, never `Math.random()` or `Date.now()`, so re-renders match frame for frame.
- In headless SwiftShader renders, `SkinnedMesh` and morph targets are CPU-light compared with shading. Keep character counts low and avoid MToon outlines if SwiftShader frame times climb (untested).

### Gaps
- I found no maintained, standalone MIT "idle motion" library for plain three.js outside TalkingHead (which assumes ARKit+Oculus blendshapes and a Mixamo-compatible rig).
