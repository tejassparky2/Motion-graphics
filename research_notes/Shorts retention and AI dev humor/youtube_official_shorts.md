# What YouTube itself has said (2023-2026) about how Shorts are recommended, measured and counted

Scope: YouTube's own statements only (YouTube Help, YouTube Official Blog, Creator Insider / YouTube Liaison, Shorts product leads), with the applicable date, as they bear on a US-facing channel of 30-75 s animated comedy skits with synthetic voices. Anything from a third party is labelled "(third party)". Current as of 18 Oct 2026. Where a statement has since been superseded it is marked SUPERSEDED.

Reading note for the report writer: the single most important structural fact is that between March 2025 and August 2026 YouTube split "views" into (a) a public, play-start count and (b) "engaged views" (stayed past the initial seconds, loops excluded) which is what pays and (as "qualified views") what qualifies a channel. The Studio metric "Stayed to watch" is the ratio form of (b). Nothing official gives a target value for it.

## Q1. What signals does YouTube say drive Shorts recommendations?

### Takeaway
YouTube has never published a Shorts-specific list of ranking signals or weights. Its official statements are: a general Help page listing eight "primary signals" for recommendations overall (watch/search history, subscriptions, likes, dislikes, "Not interested", "Don't recommend channel", satisfaction surveys), a May 2025 blog statement that Shorts and long-form have "separate recommendation systems optimized for each", and the 2023 Todd Sherman / Rene Ritchie Creator Insider Q&A ("the audience is the algorithm"; a new Short is shown to a seed audience and reach grows or tapers on their response). In October 2026 YouTube added that the Shorts system now "further prioritize[s] original content".

### Cited Findings
- Official Help page "How YouTube recommendations work" (undated, live 2026) lists these "primary signals": watch history, search history, channel subscriptions, likes, dislikes, "Not interested" selections, "Don't recommend channel" selections, and satisfaction surveys. On surveys: "User surveys that ask you to rate videos that you watched helps the system understand satisfaction, not just watch time." On dislikes: "Our system uses videos you dislike to inform what to avoid recommending in the future." On Shorts: "The Shorts Feed is personalized to reflect what we think you want to see next." The page names no Shorts-specific signals and does not mention shares, clicks or "valued watchtime". — [YouTube Help: How YouTube recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en)
- Official blog, "Shorts truths: Debunking common myths about YouTube Shorts" (The YouTube Team, 6 May 2025), Myth 6: "we've built separate recommendation systems optimized for each" format; the systems account for cross-format viewing but mainly serve each format's own viewing patterns; "we haven't seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance." Myth 7: trending sounds can help discovery but "they're not essential." Myth 2: Shorts are "a powerful tool for reaching new and diverse viewers." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)
- Todd Sherman (then Director of Product Management, YouTube Shorts), Creator Insider Q&A with Rene Ritchie, published ~23-28 Aug 2023. Direct quotes reported by Search Engine Journal (28 Aug 2023): "The audience is the algorithm." / "We want [viewers] to feel satisfied with [the videos]" / "At the core, we're trying to get videos to people they value, both in long form and short form." / "There's a lot of things that we do in short form that sort of are different". — [Search Engine Journal, 28 Aug 2023](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- Same Q&A, additional quotes taken by a third party from the video's auto-generated transcript (filler words removed, so wording is approximate): seed audience: "Find a set of people that may enjoy your video" (reach then grows or "taper[s] off" on that audience's response); early views for new creators were called "exploratory"; on flops: "If that didn't go how you wanted to go, learn from it and move forward and try again." Rene Ritchie in the same video: early Shorts views are "more like impression than traditional views." (third party, transcript-derived) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- MediaPost's write-up of the same Q&A (third party paraphrase): Shorts may gain a few hundred or thousand views and then drop off as the system tests for an audience; creators do best focusing "on audience over algorithm." — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388588/youtube-shorts-product-lead-offers-insights-for-cr.html)
- Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison), Shorts FAQ series, quoted by Tubefilter 26 Aug 2024: "The algorithm follows the audience, so please the audience." / "Check your Audience tab in Analytics, see which videos are growing your audience." / "Make more videos like those and videos people who watch those videos would just love to watch next." / "And then the algorithm will take care of itself." — [Tubefilter, 26 Aug 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- Rene Ritchie, "Rene's Top Five" (official blog, 28 Mar 2024): performance in one traffic source tends to affect that same source: "search affects search, home affects home." Also: "Shorts don't trigger notifications on upload." — [blog.youtube, 28 Mar 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-28-2024-edition/)
- YouTube Help Community post "Prioritizing original content on Shorts" (1 Oct 2026), as quoted by ppc.land: "we're updating our recommendation systems to further prioritize original content" … "ensuring creators publishing original content have the best chance to grow and succeed" … "Channels identified as primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes" … "will likely see less distribution within the Shorts feed" … "Rather than making minor technical edits or template-based changes simply to alter a video" … "focus on bringing your own voice, storytelling, or distinct point of view to the conversation." No start date was given. (quotes relayed by third party; the Help Community URL was not located) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/); also reported by [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/418466/) and [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-shorts-promoting-original-content-reposts-3718668/)
- Official blog guide (The YouTube Team, 8 Apr 2025): "Though shorts are mostly viewed in feed" (hashtags and trending audio can aid discovery). — [blog.youtube, 8 Apr 2025](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/your-guide-to-getting-started-with-youtube-shorts/)

### Inferences
- The only retention-type signal YouTube names in writing for Shorts is the engaged/"stayed to watch" split (Q2/Q3); its public Shorts-recommendation language is about audience satisfaction and value, not a formula. Third-party lists of "Shorts ranking signals" (AVD, APV, loops, shares) are not attributable to YouTube.
- Because YouTube says satisfaction surveys are a primary signal and "not just watch time", a skit that is watched fully but leaves viewers annoyed (misleading hook, no payoff) is a plausible downside even with good retention. (inference)
- For an animated original-comedy channel the Oct 2026 originality change is favourable; the risk is only if episodes look templated (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No official document enumerates Shorts-specific ranking signals or their weights; YouTube has said only what is quoted above.
- I could not locate the Creator Insider video URL for the Aug 2023 Sherman Q&A; all quotes are via press/transcript (SEJ's are the most reliable).
- The official URL of the 1 Oct 2026 Help Community post was not found in any result.

## Q2. What exactly is "Viewed vs. swiped away" / "Stayed to watch" and is there an official good value?

### Takeaway
YouTube Help defines "Stayed to watch" as "The percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away" (Reach report) and, on the Content-performance page, as "The percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short." YouTube does not publish the number of "initial seconds" and gives no benchmark or "good" value anywhere official.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Help, "Understand your YouTube video reach": "Stayed to watch · The percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away." The label "Viewed vs. swiped away" itself no longer appears on this page (it was the earlier card name). — [YouTube Help: video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en-GB&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
- YouTube Help, "Understand your YouTube content performance": Stayed to watch / "How viewers engaged": "The percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short." Engaged views: "How many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops." Average view duration: "Average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch. This is calculated from engaged views and their corresponding watch time." Average percentage viewed is defined the same way (among those who stayed; from engaged views). The Shorts section has no audience-retention / key-moments report; that exists only for Videos. The page gives no benchmark and no "good/bad value" statement. — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)
- An older Help wording of the card, "How many chose to view": "Highlights the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away", and "Shown in feed": "The number of times that your Shorts showed in the Shorts Feed." (quoted by a third party from YouTube Help) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- The metrics ("Shown in feed", "Viewed vs. swiped away") were introduced via a Creator Insider update in early 2023 and were in Studio by March 2023 (third-party reporting; primary announcement not located). — [Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/youtube-offers-creators-detailed-insights-into-their-content-performance-with-new-shorts-audience-demographic-metrics); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-analyze-your-youtube-shorts/)
- Todd Sherman, Aug 2023 (SUPERSEDED for the public count since 31 Mar 2025, but still describes the engaged-view idea): "What we try and do with a view is have it encode for your intent of watching that thing" / "so that creators feel like that view has some meaningful threshold that the person decided to watch." He did not state the threshold. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- Rene Ritchie, Creator Insider (Aug 2026), on a qualified view: one where someone "didn't just see the first frame and swipe or cancel out." (quoted by third party) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-a-view-an-impression-across-all-formats-from-august-24/)
- Benchmarks such as "aim for 75-80% viewed" or "below 40-50% means a weak hook" come only from third-party blogs, not YouTube. — [Subscribr (third party)](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-viral); [CoSchedule (third party)](https://coschedule.com/social-media-marketing/youtube-metrics)

### Inferences
- "Stayed to watch" is engaged views divided by feed presentations; because engaged views exclude loops, replays cannot raise it. Average view duration and average % viewed for Shorts are computed only over people who stayed, so a Short with a weak first second but strong body can show a high APV and a low stayed-to-watch at the same time.
- The "initial seconds" threshold is the first thing a skit must survive; everything YouTube publishes treats the swipe decision as a binary event, not a curve.

### Gaps
- YouTube does not disclose how many seconds "the initial seconds" are, and no official benchmark for Stayed to watch exists. Treat any number as a third-party heuristic.

## Q3. How has the definition of a Shorts "view" changed, and which metric drives monetization / YPP?

### Takeaway
31 Mar 2025: public Shorts views began counting every start or replay with no minimum watch time; the old intent-based count survived as "engaged views" (Advanced Mode) and remains the basis for pay. 24 Aug 2026: the play-start definition was extended to all formats ("views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats"). YouTube states YPP earnings stay on "engaged views"/"engaged watch hours" and eligibility on "qualified views". From 1 Feb 2027 Shorts revenue sharing itself requires 10M qualified Shorts views per rolling 90 days.

### Cited Findings
- March 2025 change (effective 31 Mar 2025): TechCrunch (26 Mar 2025), paraphrasing YouTube: "Shorts views will now count the number of times a creator's Short starts to play or replay"; the original metric is reachable in Advanced Mode as "engaged views"; earnings and YPP eligibility remain "based on engaged views rather than the updated metric." (third-party report of YouTube's announcement; no direct quote in the piece) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); YouTube advised using engaged views when comparing content published before 31 Mar 2025 (per [Search Engine Journal](https://beta.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/), third party)
- August 2026 change, official Help "How engagement metrics are counted": "Beginning August 24, 2026, views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats" (Shorts, long-form, live). "This update won't impact your YouTube Partner Program (YPP) earnings or eligibility." "YPP earnings will still be based on "engaged views" and "engaged watch hours"." "YPP eligibility will still be based on "qualified views."" Also: "YouTube may temporarily slow down, freeze, or change your metric count, and discard low-quality playbacks" and "It takes our systems some time to figure out which views, likes, dislikes, and subscriptions are legitimate." — [YouTube Help: engagement metrics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en)
- Official Help "content performance": "Beginning August 24, 2026, views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats, including Shorts." Monetization terms on the page: earnings use "engaged Shorts views", eligibility uses "qualified Shorts views" (neither defined there). — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)
- YouTube Help Community post "An update to how we count public views across YouTube" (Natasha, TeamYouTube, Aug 2026): "A view will be counted the moment a video begins to play," across all formats; YouTube framed it as showing "true exposure"; engaged views stay in Analytics Advanced Mode. (quotes relayed by third party; post URL not located) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-a-view-an-impression-across-all-formats-from-august-24/); also [NewscastStudio, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/18/youtube-view-count-metric-change/)
- Help "About YouTube ads and view metrics" (ads context only, 2026): "Both earned views and organic views are counted when a user watches a video for 10 seconds, except for YouTube Shorts." Public views are counted "the moment a video ad starts to play across Shorts ads and in-stream ads." For a Shorts ad, a TrueView view is "A viewer watches 10 seconds or until the end of the video, whichever comes first." (This page is about ad-campaign counting, not organic Shorts; it still carried a 10-second organic threshold at fetch time, i.e. partly SUPERSEDED by the 24 Aug 2026 change.) — [YouTube Help: ads and view metrics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2375431?hl=en)
- Shorts monetization policies (official Help): payments use "eligible 'Engaged views'"; ineligible views include "Artificial or fake views of Shorts, such as from automated click or scroll bots", "Non-original Shorts, such as unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows", "reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or other platform", "compilations with no original content added", and views of content that does not follow advertiser-friendly guidelines. Creator Pool is allocated by each creator's share of engaged views per country; creators keep 45%, "regardless if music was used or not." From 1 Feb 2027: at least 10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days needed to earn Creator Pool and Premium revenue. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- YPP eligibility (official Help): 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or "10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days"; qualified Shorts views count only public Shorts viewed in the Shorts Feed; Shorts Feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hours. Updated terms take effect 1 Feb 2027 (accept by 31 Jan 2027). — [YouTube Help: YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)
- Official Google/YouTube post "New Opportunities to Earn and Changes to the YouTube Partner Program" (11 Aug 2026): "New creators applying for YPP will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days" or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days; "This update won't impact creators already in YPP." Shorts ad/subscription revenue sharing from 1 Feb 2027 goes to "creators who have 10M qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days"; "Channels below this threshold remain in YPP and continue earning on long-form content" and sharing resumes automatically once back over 10M. — [blog.google (official)](https://blog.google/intl/en-mena/product-updates/connect-communicate/new-opportunities-to-earn-and-changes-to-the-youtube-partner-program/)
- Third-party gloss of "qualified" from the Aug 2026 coverage: qualified Shorts views must be engaged and public (ppc.land). YouTube's own pages do not define "qualified" beyond "public" + "Shorts Feed". — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-a-view-an-impression-across-all-formats-from-august-24/)
- Help-page inconsistency noted by ppc.land (third party): the Help glossary says engaged views apply only to Shorts and equal views for long-form, while the community post treats engaged views as cross-format. — same ppc.land article.

### Inferences
- For a channel whose income will depend on Shorts, the public view counter is now a vanity number; the Studio numbers that matter are engaged views, Stayed to watch and (for 2027) the 10M/90-day qualified-view bar. A channel must average ~111k qualified views a day over 90 days to keep Shorts revenue sharing after Feb 2027.
- Loops/replays inflate the public counter but, by definition, not engaged views or pay.

### Gaps
- YouTube's dedicated Help article on the March 2025 Shorts change could not be fetched (the support URL returned 404), so the 2025 wording is via press; the 2026 Help pages supersede it anyway.
- The number of "initial seconds" behind engaged/qualified views is not published.

## Q4. Length (3-minute Shorts), aspect ratio, and whether longer Shorts are disadvantaged

### Takeaway
Since 15 Oct 2024 a Short is any upload up to 3 minutes "that [is] square or taller in aspect ratio" (Todd Sherman, official blog). YouTube promised to "improve recommendations for longer Shorts in the coming months" and has published nothing on how retention is judged relative to length; Sherman's 2023 guidance was "To what length do you need to tell your story?"

### Cited Findings
- Official blog "Tall updates coming to Shorts" (Todd Sherman, Director of Product Management, YouTube Shorts, 3 Oct 2024): "Starting on October 15, you can upload Shorts up to 3 minutes long." Applies to "videos that are square or taller in aspect ratio" and "won't affect any videos you uploaded before October 15." "We'll be working to improve recommendations for longer Shorts in the coming months." Also introduced "Show fewer Shorts" (three-dot menu on a Home-feed Shorts grid) and remix templates. — [blog.youtube, 3 Oct 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)
- Official Help "Get started creating YouTube Shorts": Shorts can be "up to 3 minutes long"; title "max 100 characters"; max upload resolution 1080p; discovered via the Shorts tab ("never-ending stream of videos in the Shorts Feed"), channel homepage ("Featured on your channel homepage"), subscriptions, search (incl. TVs/consoles), homepage, notifications. No hashtag requirement stated. — [YouTube Help: Get started creating Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)
- Official blog guide (8 Apr 2025): "Shorts are capped at 3 minutes, so get your message across quickly." "Film in a 9:16 aspect ratio (vertical). This format is optimized for mobile viewing." — [blog.youtube, 8 Apr 2025](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/your-guide-to-getting-started-with-youtube-shorts/)
- Shorts monetization Help: "Shorts over one minute in length that contain claimed content are blocked and not eligible for monetization"; 3-minute Shorts uploaded before 15 Oct 2024 need an active first-party claim to monetize, those after monetize under the Shorts model. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Todd Sherman, Aug 2023 (pre-3-minute era, partly SUPERSEDED): on ideal length, "To what length do you need to tell your story?" (transcript quote via third party); press reported he emphasised keeping under 60 s to stay distinct from long-form (third party paraphrase). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [etcentric](https://www.etcentric.org/youtube-unravels-shorts-demystifying-discovery-algorithm/)
- Average percentage viewed for Shorts is calculated among viewers who stayed (from engaged views). — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)

### Inferences
- For 30-75 s skits the length rules are not a constraint; the only official metric sensitivity to length is that APV is relative to the Short's own duration while engaged views are absolute, so a 75 s skit and a 30 s skit are judged "stayed" on the same first-seconds test.

### Gaps
- YouTube has published nothing on whether 60-180 s Shorts are recommended differently or on any length-normalised retention target; the Oct 2024 promise to improve longer-Short recommendations has no public follow-up I could find.

## Q5. Subscribers from Shorts, the Subscribe button, related-video links, Shorts-to-long-form, and the feed vs the channel Shorts shelf

### Takeaway
YouTube's official positions: Shorts and long-form run on separate recommendation systems and Shorts do not hurt long-form; Shorts uploads do not trigger subscriber notifications; the "Related video" link lets a Short point to a long-form video, livestream or another Short; Studio's Subscribers report breaks out subscribers gained per content type including Shorts. YouTube publishes no figure on how Shorts-gained subscribers behave, and I found no official statement about the Shorts Subscribe button or the channel Shorts shelf.

### Cited Findings
- Separate systems / no evidence of harm: official blog, 6 May 2025 (quoted in Q1). — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)
- Rene Ritchie, 28 Mar 2024 (official blog): "Shorts don't trigger notifications on upload, so that part won't make a difference"; "search affects search, home affects home." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-28-2024-edition/)
- Related video link, official blog "YouTube related videos traffic guide" (Julia Lee Harter, 14 Jul 2026): the feature "allows you to attach a clickable link directly to your Short" to a long-form video, livestream or another Short; set in Studio > Content > the Short > "Related video". The post calls Shorts reach "undeniable" ("billions of daily views") but gives no conversion or subscriber numbers. — [blog.youtube, 14 Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- Launch context (third party, Tubefilter, 1 Sep 2023): related links arrived after YouTube removed clickable links from Shorts descriptions/comments in Aug 2023 to fight spam. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/09/01/youtube-related-links-multi-format-creators-shorts-live-long-form/)
- Studio: the Shorts key-metrics card shows likes and subscribers, and "The Subscribers report tracks subscribers gained from each content type, including Shorts." — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)
- Help "How YouTube recommendations work": channel subscriptions are a primary signal ("The YouTube channels you've subscribed to help to recommend other videos you may like"); the homepage mainly relies on watch history. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en)
- Myth 4 (official blog, 6 May 2025): revenue per view may be lower than long-form but Shorts can drive traffic to long-form. Myth 8: Shorts "can foster a strong sense of community." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)
- 2022 (older, context only): YouTube told press that channels uploading both Shorts and long-form saw better watch time and subscriber growth than long-form-only channels (third-party report of YouTube statement). — [Tubefilter, 15 Jun 2022](https://tubefilter.com/2022/06/15/youtube-shorts-multi-format-creator-ian-boggs-rosanna-pansino/)
- Official community/engagement features for Shorts (blog, 7 Jul 2026): Quiz sticker can "keep them on your video a few seconds longer"; "A thriving community isn't built on monologues; it's built on conversations"; Add Yours chains expose "your prompt and channel to entirely new corners of the platform"; video replies notify the commenter. — [blog.youtube, 7 Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/grow-youtube-channel-interactive-shorts/)

### Inferences
- Because Shorts do not notify subscribers and the two formats have separate systems, subscriber count from Shorts mostly matters for YPP thresholds and the subscriptions feed, not for pushing the next Short. (inference from the Ritchie and May 2025 statements)

### Gaps
- No official statement found on the Shorts Subscribe button's effect, on the channel-page Shorts shelf ordering, or on how subscribers gained from Shorts are treated by recommendations.
- The "Add a related video to your YouTube Shorts" Help article was not fetched (only referenced).

## Q6. Posting frequency and time, hashtags, titles/descriptions, "inauthentic content" (July 2025) and AI-content labelling, for an animated parody channel with synthetic voices

### Takeaway
Sherman (2023): posting time "is not something that we think about optimizing for" (except news); no upload-count threshold in the system; hashtags not required; most thumbnails never seen; re-uploading a deleted Short risks being treated as spam. YouTube's 2025 guide says "Post Shorts regularly" and lists #Shorts / #YouTubeShorts as popular hashtags. The 15 Jul 2025 YPP change renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" (mass-produced/templated videos, including AI templates, are ineligible; recurring-character series with distinct storylines are fine). YouTube's AI-disclosure rule requires a label only for realistic synthetic media; "clearly unrealistic or animated content" and cloning your own voice do not require disclosure, while generating "a synthetic voice for narration" using "a realistic person's likeness" does.

### Cited Findings
- Posting time / frequency (Todd Sherman, Aug 2023, transcript quotes via third party): timing: "It's not something that we think about optimizing for."; volume: "There is nothing in our systems that's like when you hit the five video or 10 short threshold"; reposting: "There is a risk there that that gets seen as spam in our systems."; thumbnails: "Most thumbnails are never seen because people are in the feed."; hashtags: "Are they required? No, of course not." — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/). MediaPost paraphrase: time of publishing does not matter "unless they're covering news"; hashtags "aren't actually necessary" but help associate content with real-world events and topics; quality over quantity. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388588/youtube-shorts-product-lead-offers-insights-for-cr.html)
- Official blog guide (8 Apr 2025): "Post Shorts regularly to build an audience." "Write clear and engaging titles that capture attention." "Popular hashtags include #Shorts, #YouTubeShorts, and niche-specific tags." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/your-guide-to-getting-started-with-youtube-shorts/)
- Official Help: Shorts title "max 100 characters"; no hashtag requirement stated. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)
- Inauthentic content (official Help, channel monetization policies, notice dated 15 Jul 2025): "We are also renaming this policy from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content."" Content must not be "mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative." Allowed examples: same intro/outro with different main content; series with recurring characters or review channels where each video has its own storyline, focus or concept. Not allowed: repetitive content with minimal variation; "videos that put characters in the same situation with the same outcome over and over"; image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with little narrative or educational value; AI content using generic or unoriginal templates that gives "the impression of mass production" without the creator's original perspective. The reused-content policy was not changed. — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's July 2025 clarification (TeamYouTube, quoted by ppc.land): "a minor update to our longstanding guideline on repetitious content"; the policy "includes repetitive or mass-produced content that users often consider spam"; "This content was never eligible for monetization, as originality and authenticity have always been YPP requirements"; YouTube "allows creators to use AI tools in content to enhance their storytelling"; reused content "can continue to be monetized if you added significant original commentary or modifications". YouTube's example of what fails: "Channels that upload narrative stories with only superficial differences between them, or slide shows that all have the same narration." — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)
- Shorts monetization Help lists as ineligible: "Non-original Shorts", reuploads, "compilations with no original content added"; channels must also follow the "repetitious and reused content" policies. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Oct 2026 Shorts originality ranking change (see Q1): template-based changes do not count as original; "your own voice, storytelling, or distinct point of view" does. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)
- AI disclosure, official blog (18 Mar 2024): disclosure required for "realistic content" a viewer could mistake for a real person, place, scene or event, including "Using a realistic person's likeness, such as swapping faces or generating a synthetic voice for narration." Not required: "Clearly unrealistic or animated content, including fantastical scenes", productivity uses "such as scripts, content ideas, or automatic captions", colour/lighting/beauty filters. Label normally in the expanded description; more prominent on-video label for sensitive topics (health, news, elections, finance). YouTube may add a label itself and said it would consider enforcement for creators who consistently fail to disclose. — [blog.youtube, 18 Mar 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- AI disclosure, official Help "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content" (live 2026): required when content makes "a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "Alters footage of a real event or place", "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", or "Creates music that's the main focus of the video." Not required for non-realistic content (examples: a unicorn ride through a fantasy world; "an AI-generated or altered missile animation within a fully animated video") or minor help (scripts, outlines, thumbnails, titles, captions, voice/audio repair, "cloning one's own voice for voiceovers or dubs"). Label placement: for AI music or photorealistic content the label may appear in the player; "For non-music AI audio or non-photorealistic or animated AI content, a label may appear in the expanded description." YouTube may auto-label content made with its GenAI tools, content with C2PA metadata or content its systems detect; "Disclosure alone doesn't limit audience or monetization eligibility." — [YouTube Help: altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Help "How YouTube recommendations work" states "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel" are primary negative signals. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en)

### Inferences
- An animated skit with an obviously synthetic/cartoon narrator falls under "clearly unrealistic or animated content" and needs no label; a label becomes required if a skit impersonates a real, identifiable person's voice (a parody of a named real developer or CEO with a realistic cloned voice would be the risk case). Selecting "Yes" for AI use would place the label in the expanded description only, and YouTube says this alone does not affect reach or monetization.
- The owner-chosen "vary the character and setting every time" rule maps directly onto YouTube's allowed-vs-disallowed examples: a recurring cast is explicitly fine as long as each episode has "its own storyline, focus, or concept"; "same situation with the same outcome over and over" is the disallowed pattern.
- Nothing official supports a best time of day; YouTube's only frequency advice is "regularly".

### Gaps
- No official statement found on hashtags or descriptions affecting Shorts ranking beyond "can boost discoverability"; no official statement on an optimal cadence.
- The Help page does not address synthetic voices of fictional characters or parody explicitly; the "animated content" exemption is the closest text.

## Q7. Loops/rewatches and the first seconds (hook)

### Takeaway
YouTube's only official statements on loops are definitional: engaged views are counted "not including any loops", while the public counter (since 31 Mar 2025) counts each "play or replay". YouTube's own creator guides repeatedly state the first-seconds rule ("Capture attention in the first few seconds to prevent viewers from scrolling"; "hook your viewers in the first couple seconds") but publish no seconds threshold and no claim that loops are a ranking signal.

### Cited Findings
- Engaged views: "How many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops." — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)
- Public Shorts views since 31 Mar 2025 count each time a Short "starts to play or replay" (TechCrunch paraphrase of YouTube). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)
- Official blog guide (8 Apr 2025): "Capture attention in the first few seconds to prevent viewers from scrolling." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/your-guide-to-getting-started-with-youtube-shorts/)
- Official blog (7 Jul 2026): creators have heard the advice to "hook your viewers in the first couple seconds"; a Quiz sticker can "keep them on your video a few seconds longer." — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/grow-youtube-channel-interactive-shorts/)
- Stayed to watch is defined on "the initial seconds of a Short" (Q2). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)
- Rene Ritchie on what a qualified view is not: someone who "didn't just see the first frame and swipe or cancel out." (third-party quote) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-a-view-an-impression-across-all-formats-from-august-24/)
- Claims that YouTube "weights replay rate and loop count more heavily than raw watch-time" appear only in third-party blogs that cite no YouTube source; I found no YouTube statement to that effect. (third party, unverified) — e.g. [videobgremover (third party)](https://videobgremover.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Designing a skit to loop may lift the public view counter and total watch time, but by YouTube's definitions it does not add engaged views, does not move Stayed to watch, and has no documented ranking effect. The documented lever is the first-seconds swipe decision.
- Since Shorts have no retention-curve report in Studio, the per-second drop-off inside a Short is invisible to the creator; Stayed to watch (first seconds) and average percentage viewed (among stayers) are the two numbers to watch.

### Gaps
- YouTube has made no official statement on whether rewatches/loops influence Shorts recommendations.
- No official seconds value for the hook window; "first few seconds" / "first couple seconds" is the most specific official language.
