# Case studies: short animated / character-driven comedy skit channels (2023-2026), with dev-humor focus

Research date: 18 Oct 2026. All "live" channel numbers below were pulled from the public YouTube `/about` pages on 18 Oct 2026 (cited as `youtube.com/@handle/about, fetched 2026-10-18`). Press numbers carry the article date. Third-party trackers (CreatorDB, Social Blade, vidIQ) are marked as estimates. Nothing here is invented; channels that could not be verified are listed as such in the Gaps.

Method note: Wayback Machine has no snapshots for the `@handle` channel URLs, so dated trajectories come from press articles plus today's live counts rather than Social Blade histories (Social Blade pages are login-gated for this session).

## Key question 1: Animated / 3D / low-poly skit accounts: which found Shorts growth and how?

### Takeaway
The animated channels that grew fastest on short-form all share four traits: (1) a daily-to-several-times-a-week cadence of 10-60 s pieces, (2) a small cast of fixed, cheap-to-animate recurring characters, (3) a serialized world or format so viewers come back rather than consume one-offs, and (4) the same clip pushed to TikTok, Shorts and Instagram at once. The named target "@agentpoly / Agent Poly Studios" cannot be used as a case study: the YouTube channel has 1 Short with 183 views and the TikTok handle has 1 follower (see Cited Findings).

### Cited Findings

- YouTube channel "Agent Poly": joined 5 May 2025, "1 video", "183 views", one Short titled "People MOCKED her, but 20 years later..... #story #agent #fun #inspira"; description says "AI-driven videos... Each video takes 6-10 hours to craft using cutting-edge AI tools" and claims Fair Use of existing media. No subscriber count is shown (below display threshold). — [youtube.com/@agentpoly/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@agentpoly/about)
- TikTok @agentpoly: `followerCount: 1`, `videoCount: 0`. — [tiktok.com/@agentpoly, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.tiktok.com/@agentpoly)
- Web search for "Agent Poly Studios" low-poly Claude/Codex skits returned nothing. — [search 1](https://www.robert-glaser.de/claude-skills-in-codex-cli/) (irrelevant hits only)

**Nutshell Animations (2D, daily "animation memes", Toronto)** — the clearest short-form animation blueprint
- Aug 2022: ~20M TikTok followers (started TikTok June 2020), YouTube just hit 1M subs, ~300M views/month across accounts; YouTube channel dates from 2018. — [Tubefilter, 11 Aug 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)
- Live 18 Oct 2026: 11M subscribers, 5,322 videos, 12.94B views; channel description is literally "DAILY ANIMATION MEMES!" — [youtube.com/@NutshellAnimations/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@NutshellAnimations/about)
- Format: "little bite-size animation memes" built on short comedic audio clips; one animation per day for almost three years; a week of content made in 3-4 days; 3-6 hours per piece; no storyboarding (rough animation -> linework -> lip animation -> camera). — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)
- Creator credits growth to: the daily format ("golden recipe"), being a niche with no precedent ("an animation a day"), a "gamified" daily-posting challenge, and cross-posting the same clip to YouTube/Snapchat/Instagram. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)
- Platform difference: TikTok grew faster; YouTube "requires more commitment from viewers to subscribe"; by 2022 Shorts growth was "roughly similar" to TikTok. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)
- What failed: an early TikTok test account (~100K followers) the creator "wasn't committed" to; the original weekly story-driven YouTube channel ("nothing I was committed to"); chasing trending audios (dropped in favour of comedic audio he could develop). — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)

**The Land of Boggs (BuzzFeed AnimationLab, 2D, two recurring friends)**
- Shorts crossposts Oct 2021-Jan 2022 got ~10K views each and <10K-30K subs; the team stopped posting. March 2022: views jumped "overnight" to ~1M per video; by the May 2022 article, nearly 800K subs and ~80M views/week. TikTok ~5.5M followers. — [Tubefilter, 11 May 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/)
- Live 18 Oct 2026: 9.88M subscribers, 751 videos, 14.34B views; channel joined 15 Apr 2021. Description: "an animated series about two best friends, Boggo and Boe". — [youtube.com/@TheLandofBoggs/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@TheLandofBoggs/about)
- Credits: TV/animation-studio production discipline, "simple, two-limbed character designs that make production fast", broad relatable friendship/family themes; three people on the show, target two videos/week; ~1 week of one person's time per video once script and voice exist; weekly compilations (~500K views each) built from the TikTok backlog. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/)
- Cast is a fixed pairing (introvert Boggo / extrovert Boe) plus a brother and "Tiffany (astrology)"; BuzzFeed's "Sanrio approach" tests new characters each year. Quote: "Now that YouTube has Shorts, focus on Shorts." — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/)

**Jake Fellman (3D animated Shorts, game-meme characters)** — closest 3D analogue
- First Among Us Short (24 s) >22M views; channel views ~24K in Sep 2020 -> >524M in Dec 2020 -> ~600M/month by mid-2021; subs 100K (Oct 2020) -> ~2M (end 2020) -> >6M (mid 2021). — [Tubefilter, 21 Jul 2021](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/creators-on-the-rise-jake-fellman/)
- ~90 Among Us shorts in ~3 months (about one a day), then ~70 Minecraft shorts in 4 months; ~8 h work + ~8 h render per short; 3D keyframe animation + After Effects. — [Tubefilter 2021](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/creators-on-the-rise-jake-fellman/)
- Credits: early adoption of Shorts, "a very distinct art style that no one else quite replicates", "Bite-sized content is perfect for animators because it allows for a faster production cycle", re-using models and rigs across series, riding the biggest game of the moment. — [Tubefilter 2021](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/creators-on-the-rise-jake-fellman/)

**DaFuq!?Boom! / Skibidi Toilet (Source Filmmaker 3D, serialized, no dialogue)**
- Channel took ~7 years to reach 5M subs, then gained ~12M in ~6 weeks (spring 2023); 2.8B views in May 2023 (top of Tubefilter's global chart); >7B total views by July 2023. — [Cartoon Brew, 3 Jul 2023](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/animators/dafuqboom-youtube-alexey-gerasimov-skibidi-toilet-230239.html)
- Industry source (Phil Ranta) credits serialization ("gave viewers a reason to return, unlike one-off shorts"), a recurring world fans theorize about on Reddit, and craft (pacing, camera, sound design); also says luck/timing played a role. Made by one person in Source Filmmaker plus one asset helper. — [Cartoon Brew 2023](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/animators/dafuqboom-youtube-alexey-gerasimov-skibidi-toilet-230239.html)

**GingerPale (2D, Shorts as funnel to long-form)**
- Monthly views ~600-900K before Shorts -> 4.5M in one month after; Shorts mostly 10-30 s, one per week, batched; ~1.5M subs at the time. **Cas van de Pol (2D "Ultimate Recap", long-ish shorts ~2 min)**

**Flashgitz (2D, serialized "Space King" universe)**
- Live 18 Oct 2026: 5.16M subs, 230 videos, 1.35B views; Shorts tab shows a run of Space King character clips at 243K-15M views ("Are WE the bad guys...?" 15M; "Captain Finds Out Space King is Coming Back in 20 Seconds..." 7M), i.e. shorts built from recurring characters of a long-form series. — [youtube.com/@Flashgitz/shorts, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@Flashgitz/shorts)

**Joel Haver (rotoscope skits; weekly cadence, then deliberately left the algorithm)**
- ~2M subs (Dec 2024); posted weekly live-action/animated sketches for ~12 years; breakout "Playing an RPG for the first time" (~9.4M views per an aggregator listing, approximate). — [Publish Press, 2 Dec 2024](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/can-this-creator-make-12-movies-in-12-months); [Plugged In review](https://www.pluggedin.com/youtube-reviews/joel-haver/); [Wikipedia: 2.12M subs, 314M views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Haver)
- Credits consistent weekly uploads: without them "I'd have about 1,000 subscribers"; calls the weekly commitment "the best way to get seen as a filmmaker online". In 2024 he felt "personally pigeon-holed by the algorithm and my weekly uploads" and switched to one feature film a month, funded by ~$100K from ~3,500 Patreon supporters. — [Publish Press 2024](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/can-this-creator-make-12-movies-in-12-months)
- Of his top-10 videos, 7 are the animated skits and 1 is the tutorial on how he makes them (fan count on a forum, unverified). — [Tildes thread](https://tildes.net/~movies/1d9s/youtuber_joel_haver_to_create_twelve_feature_length_films_in_twelve_months)

**Ice Cream Sandwich (2D, infrequent)**
- March 2022: >2M subs with <30 videos in five years; audience mostly 18-24; credits minimalist style and relatable adult stories; admits "I have little interaction with fans... not great" because he posts so little; advice: "being a perfectionist ends up working against you". Live 18 Oct 2026: 3.83M subs, 61 videos, 416M views. — [Passionfruit, 24 Mar 2022](https://passionfru.it/ice-cream-sandwich-youtube-1066/); [youtube.com/@icecreamsandwich/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@icecreamsandwich/about)

**Alan Becker (stick figures, no dialogue)**
- 27.5M subs Dec 2023 (Wikipedia); live 18 Oct 2026: 34.2M subs, 270 videos, 9.99B views; description: "There is no dialogue, but a lot of humor and emotion." Third-party estimate says ~92% of recent uploads are long-form, so Shorts are a small share. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Becker); [youtube.com/@alanbecker/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@alanbecker/about); [OutlierKit (estimate)](https://outlierkit.com/channel/alanbecker)

**YouTube's own Jan 2026 Shorts advice (skit creator "Andrea", Philippines)**
- Give each recurring character a distinct personality "so the audience can predict how a skit will play out"; fans suggest trends per character; mini-series "performed well, especially those featuring her character Agent Red"; pairing Agent Red with "the Faerie" became "one of the most requested pieces of content". Artist Kooleen's catchphrase ("juicy lips") became a community inside joke and merch; "never let your viewers know your next move". — [YouTube Official Blog, 15 Jan 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/cracking-the-code-to-youtube-shorts/)

### Inferences
- For a low-poly dev-skit channel the Fellman + Boggs pattern is the most transferable: 2-4 fixed characters with re-usable rigs, 20-45 s pieces, 3-7 uploads a week, same file to Shorts/TikTok/Reels. Skibidi Toilet and Flashgitz show that serialization (a running conflict between recurring factions) is what converts one-off meme views into a returning audience.
- Every success above had a visual signature nobody else copied (Fellman's style, Nutshell's look, Haver's rotoscope); low-poly is a usable signature only if it is consistent and recognisable in frame 0.
- Channels that post rarely (Ice Cream Sandwich) can still reach millions, but only with exceptional writing, and they admit weak community interaction; that is not a repeatable plan for a new channel.

### Gaps
- No verifiable low-poly 3D comedy skit channel with documented growth was found (search for "Blender 3D skit channel interview" returned nothing relevant). "@agentpoly" exists but has essentially no content or audience as of 18 Oct 2026.
- No numbers found for Sr Pelo, Lackadaisy shorts, "Oh No, Nina", Nutshell's Shorts-vs-TikTok split after 2022, or Alan Becker's Shorts specifically.
- Wayback has no `@handle` snapshots, so year-by-year subscriber curves (2023, 2024, 2025) could not be reconstructed; only press-dated points plus today's live counts.

## Key question 2: Developer / tech-humor skit channels: formats that got millions of views

### Takeaway
The best-documented dev-humor skit channel, "Programmers are also human" (400K subs, 113.5M views), grew on 2-3 minute "Interview with a Senior X Developer" sketches, got criticised for format fatigue by April 2024, and today its Shorts tab is a run of 20-60 s POV skits at 115K-2M views each ("Vibe Coded elevator" 419K, "AI CEO vs engineer" 480K, "Engineer leaving for Big Tech" 2M). Krazam, the critical darling of tech sketch comedy, is the cautionary case: 53 videos in nine years, 189K subs, and Shorts that mostly get 3-30K views.

### Inferences
- The one dev-skit channel with real scale moved from 2-3 minute interview sketches to <60 s POV character skits; its current Short titles are all "a type of person in a situation" (sysadmin, DBA, AI CEO, boss during your vacation), which is exactly the recurring-archetype format. Titles that name a current trope ("Vibe Coded", "AI CEO", "Tech Grifters in 2026") sit at 400-500K; the broadest relatable ones ("every corporate job", "leaving for Big Tech") hit 1.8-2M.
- Krazam shows that prestige writing and a Primeagen audience do not substitute for cadence: 53 uploads in nine years, Shorts in the low tens of thousands, and monetisation pushed to Patreon and a live show.
- Fireship's two recurring formats with hashtag names (#100SecondsOfCode, #TheCodeReport) and a fixed length are the dev-space proof that named, repeatable series build binge libraries.

## Key question 3: Workplace / relatable skit creators: what converts relatable skits to followers?

### Takeaway
Corporate Natalie, Laura Whaley, Corporate Erin and Corporate Brian all grew on the same mechanics: a fixed exaggerated persona, skits "based on personal experiences or conversations with friends and followers", a weekly batch-recording habit, and comment sections that argue (and that brands join). Whaley has gone furthest in characters: a multi-year episodic office comedy ("Project Poodles") whose fictional coworker Brenda now gets her own brand deals.

### Cited Findings
- Corporate Natalie (Natalie Marshall): began on TikTok in 2020 as a Deloitte consultant; hit 10K, 25K, 50K followers "within weeks"; Sept 2024: >1M Instagram, ~700K TikTok; audience mostly millennial women; records several videos twice a week and keeps a backlog; skits "all contain a kernel of truth"; quit her job mid-2022; one full-time employee; "I get a performance review every single day, 24 hours a day". — [SF Standard, 15 Sep 2024](https://sfstandard.com/2024/09/15/how-corporate-natalie-grew-tiktok-instagram-business-in-sf)
- April 2026: 1.4M Instagram, 827K TikTok, 276K LinkedIn; launched a creator-led agency. — [Fortune, 5 Apr 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/04/05/corporate-natalie-new-creator-led-agency-expand-co-lab-influencer-marketing/)
- YouTube is her weakest platform: 81.2K subs, 847 videos, 57.8M views (joined Feb 2022). — [youtube.com/@corporatenatalie/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@corporatenatalie/about)
- WorkLife (8 Jan 2024): Corporate Erin ~300K, Corporate Brian >100K, Corporate Natalie >500K followers; agency TikTok lead Malene Hold: "It's the relatability of it all and the fact there are so many small universal insights that we might not talk about"; each plays a consistent persona (Brian: "aloof, never gets it right"; Erin: head of HR with a "corporate accent" and the recurring line about making sure "all the deliverables get across"); Marshall: "almost all of my skits are based on personal experiences or conversations with friends and followers"; brand accounts (The Office, Indeed) reply in comments. Hashtag scale: #WorkLife 30.6B views, #CorporateTikTok 4.9B, #WorkTok 2.4B. — [WorkLife, 8 Jan 2024](https://www.worklife.news/culture/corporate-work-skits/)
- Laura Whaley (@loewhaley): started Nov 2020 with ~20 followers; >3.2M TikTok followers and >104M likes by April 2023; topics chosen by her community; audience ~70% female; "If people disagree, I love that; it creates good conversation". — [Fortune, 25 Apr 2023](https://www.fortune.com/2023/04/25/tiktoker-laura-whaley-career-advice-work-life-boundaries)
- Sept 2026: >5M followers across Instagram/TikTok/YouTube; "Project Poodles" is a multi-year episodic workplace comedy she scripts, voices and plays; a dating app asked to partner with the character Brenda rather than Laura; "some followers come only for Project Poodles, others follow her for everything except that series"; "How to Professionally Say" series and the "Work Besties" community are central; "I'm creating for the audience I have and not the audience that I necessarily am hoping to get". — [NetInfluencer, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.netinfluencer.com/how-laura-whaley-built-a-creator-business-where-brands-partner-with-her-fictional-characters/)
- YouTube: 908K subs, 1,067 videos, 929.9M views (joined Dec 2020); description "Hey Work Bestie... Toodaloo, Laura". — [youtube.com/@loewhaley/about, fetched 2026-10-18](https://www.youtube.com/@loewhaley/about)
- Emily Zugay (deadpan "bad logo redesign" format, Sept 2021): passed 1M TikTok followers within about a week; crowdsourced her next targets from her own comments; Amazon's "AmaSon" response got 46.4M views and a 30% follower lift in 48 h (brand self-reported); analysts credit deadpan delivery, "repulsive but believable" designs, and brands joining the bit. — [Shift Communications](https://www.shiftcomm.com/thinking/3-takeaways-from-the-viral-tiktok-brand-moment-of-emily-zugay); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/nascar-tinder-and-tampax-used-a-tiktok-creators-ugly-logo-redesigns-for-their-profile-pictures/); [Amazon award entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/amason-logo-606364)
- YouTube blog (Jan 2026) on skit creators: distinct recurring personalities make outcomes predictable, mini-series with a favourite character perform best, pairing two characters creates the most-requested content. — [YouTube Official Blog, 15 Jan 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/cracking-the-code-to-youtube-shorts/)

### Inferences
- The conversion engine in this genre is "I know this person": a persona with a fixed voice and tic (corporate accent, "Toodaloo", deliverables line) that viewers can quote back. Catchphrases become comment fodder and merch.
- All three top creators moved from relatable one-liners to named recurring series (Project Poodles, How to Professionally Say) once they had an audience; the series is what gives a reason to follow rather than just like.
- YouTube is consistently the laggard platform for these live-action skit creators (Natalie 81K YT vs 1.4M IG), which matches Nutshell's comment that YouTube "requires more commitment from viewers to subscribe".

### Gaps
- No analyst study quantifies conversion for relatable skits; the evidence is creator and agency quotes. Episode lengths and exact posting cadence for Whaley and Erin/Brian were not published.

## Key question 4: Numbers: trajectories, posting frequency, episode length, when series started

### Takeaway

## Key question 5: What went wrong for channels that plateaued; creator post-mortems

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: sporadic cadence (Krazam, Nutshell's abandoned first accounts), a 2-3 minute format that exhausted its premise (Programmers are also human's interview sketches, per HN), Shorts audiences that don't match the channel's core (Pat Flynn), a long posting pause that makes returning subscribers unsubscribe, unchanged packaging that viewers learn to skip, and "made for kids" mislabeling of cartoon styles.

### Cited Findings
- Format fatigue, dev skits: "the format gets kinda old after a few videos"; "would work better as a short reel than a 2-3 minute video... there's only so much material there"; "there's a difference between funny and relatable". — [Hacker News, Apr 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40129463)
- Sporadic cadence: Krazam is "sporadically-updated" and sits at 53 videos / 189K subs after nine years, with Shorts at 3-74K views. — [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WebVideo/KRAZAM); [live](https://www.youtube.com/@KRAZAM/shorts)
- Commitment: Nutshell's earlier TikTok (~100K followers) and weekly story channel both stalled because he "wasn't committed"; trending-audio chasing was dropped. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/08/11/youtube-millionaires-nutshell-animations/)
- Crossposting alone did not work: Boggs' TikTok reposts got ~10K views on YouTube for months and a TikTok hit "flopped on YouTube"; the team quit, then resumed daily posting and the channel jumped. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/)
- Wrong-audience virality: Pat Flynn (Deep Pocket Monster) says Shorts with >15M views "ended up hurting my channel": they reached people who mocked the niche, earned ~$400 total, and started replacing his long-form in recommendations; one 5M-view long video brought >40K subs and ~$20K. He stopped Shorts mid-2022. — [Smart Passive Income ep. 638, 16 Dec 2022](https://www.smartpassiveincome.com/session638)
- Subscriber conversion gap: long-form converted 22.7 subs per 10,000 views vs 16.9 for Shorts (reverses for >1M-sub channels, per Galloway's theory that they make better Shorts with stronger CTAs). — [Publish Press, 17 Apr 2023](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/are-shorts-worth-it)
- Posting pause churn: an entertainment channel with 142M views/month gained 42K and lost 21K subs a month; Shorts drove 72% of new subs but only 11% of unsubscribes; the real cause was a 10-month posting pause after which lapsed subscribers "didn't recognize the channel and left"; fixes were end screens and linking Shorts to full videos. — [AIR Media-Tech, updated 3 Sep 2026](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/every-youtube-channel-has-a-blocker-we-found-surprising-ones)
- Packaging fatigue: an education channel declined for two years because an unchanged face-forward thumbnail style "trained loyal viewers to recognize the format on sight" and assume they had already seen it; fix was varying position/size, not dropping the face. — [AIR Media-Tech 2026](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/every-youtube-channel-has-a-blocker-we-found-surprising-ones)
- "Dead subscribers" / passive feed: vendor guides describe Shorts subscribers who joined for one clip and never return, and "the Shorts audience is distinct from the long-form audience"; a surge-then-plateau pattern as the algorithm widens the audience and completion drops. (Vendor sources, no methodology.) — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/grow-channel-only-youtube-shorts); [Subscribr on sudden drops](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-suddenly-stop-getting-views)
- Algorithm pigeon-holing: Joel Haver left weekly uploads in 2024 because he felt "personally pigeon-holed by the algorithm and my weekly uploads". — [Publish Press 2024](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/can-this-creator-make-12-movies-in-12-months)
- Cartoon style mislabeled "made for kids" removes ~90% of ad revenue and comments (Cas van de Pol). — [Cartoon Brew 2021](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/how-to/how-cas-van-de-pol-gained-millions-of-youtube-followers-with-his-animated-recaps-206440.html)
- Low interaction: Ice Cream Sandwich calls his fan interaction "not great" because he posts so little. — [Passionfruit 2022](https://passionfru.it/ice-cream-sandwich-youtube-1066/)
- Weakly sourced but directionally consistent: an influencer-marketing site claims channels running structured multi-episode Shorts series see 2-3x higher subscriber conversion ("creator analytics platforms", unnamed), and recommends a consistent cold-open cue, episode numbering and a running question; a pinned comment linking to the full video reportedly converts at 1-3%. Treat as unverified. — [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/youtube-shorts-series-how-to-turn-viewers-into-subscribers/)

### Inferences
- For the low-poly dev channel the two most likely stall causes are (a) a single gag template (e.g. "Claude vs Codex argue") that runs dry after 10 episodes, the exact complaint levelled at the interview-sketch format, and (b) virality among non-developers who do not subscribe. Rotating the situation while keeping the cast fixed, and keeping jokes specific to dev work, address both.
- A low-poly cartoon look risks the same "made for kids" auto-label Cas describes; set audience to "not made for kids" explicitly and watch for mislabels.

### Gaps
- No first-person post-mortem from a dev-humor skit creator was found; the Krazam cadence reading is inferred from counts, not from the duo's own statement.
- No public data on Shorts subscriber conversion specifically for animated skits.
