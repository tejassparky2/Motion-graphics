## Fact check

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Hydrophobia (fear of water), aerophobia; once clinical signs appear, virtually 100% fatal | WHO, Rabies fact sheet (updated 17 Sep 2026) |
| Dogs cause 99% of human rabies; transmitted through saliva by bites, scratches, licks on broken skin | WHO |
| Incubation typically 2-3 months, from 1 week to 1 year | WHO; CDC averages 1-3 months |
| Incubation depends on how far the exposure site is from the brain (so a bite on the face reaches the brain sooner) | CDC, Clinical overview / Clinical features of rabies |
| The virus travels along the nerves to the brain | CDC; WHO |
| First signs: fever, and prickling/tingling/itching at the bite site; then spasms of the swallowing muscles | CDC; WHO |
| Washing the wound with soap and water for at least 15 minutes; PEP = wound washing, a course of vaccine, immunoglobulin when indicated; PEP works after the exposure | WHO |
| India: about 9.1 million animal bites a year, 76.8% (about 3 in 4) from dogs, an estimated 5,726 human rabies deaths a year; more than a fifth of dog-bite victims got no anti-rabies vaccine; nearly half of those who started did not finish | ICMR-NIE study, The Lancet Infectious Diseases, Jan 2025 (survey of 3.4 lakh people in 15 states, 2022-23) |
| The old nerve-tissue vaccine (14 injections around the stomach) is no longer used in India (stopped in 2004); modern vaccines are a few doses in the arm | Ministry of Health / NAPRE guidelines |

Left out:
- Whether the vaccine is free at government hospitals. It's a state matter, and stock-outs are reported.
- The older estimate of about 20,000 deaths a year. The script uses the newer Lancet estimate, said as "estimated".
- The 10-day dog observation rule. It's nuanced, and the script just says go to hospital the same day.
- No treatment advice beyond wash, go to hospital, finish the doses.
