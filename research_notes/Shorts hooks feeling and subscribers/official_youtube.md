# What YouTube itself has officially said (2022 to Oct 2026) about how Shorts are recommended and evaluated, and how Shorts viewers become subscribers

Scope note: this file covers official YouTube statements only: Help Center pages, the YouTube Official Blog, TeamYouTube Help Community posts, Creator Insider / Creator Liaison material, and YouTube staff quotes in reporting. Each finding carries a source tag:
- **[Official, primary]**: I read the wording on a YouTube-owned page (Help Center, blog.youtube, Help Community). Pages were pulled on 18 Oct 2026.
- **[Official, via secondary]**: YouTube staff wording transcribed by a third party (an article quoting a Creator Insider video, for example). I could not read the primary page.
- **[Third-party]**: someone else's interpretation or data, included only for contrast.

What is new here compared with `research_notes/Shorts retention for animated explainers/hooks_metrics_length.md`: the Feb 2027 YPP changes, the Oct 1 2026 "original content" Shorts ranking change, the full current wording of the inauthentic-content policy, AI disclosure, advertiser-friendly rules, hype, the related-video link, notifications, posting time, and subscriber data. Already established there and only summarised here: viewed vs swiped away, the 31 Mar 2025 view-count change, engaged views excluding loops, and 3-minute Shorts.

## Q1. What signals does YouTube say the Shorts feed uses?

### Takeaway
YouTube has never published a Shorts-specific list of signals or their weights. Officially, Shorts and long-form use the same core system, which is optimised for viewer satisfaction. The named inputs are clicks/views, watch time, survey responses ("valued watchtime"), shares, likes, dislikes, subscriptions, "not interested" style feedback, and (for Shorts) whether the viewer chose to view or swiped away. Staff keep repeating that no single metric decides reach.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, primary, Nov 2021 but still YouTube's fullest public description] Head of recommendations Cristos Goodrow: "A number of signals build on each other to help inform our system about what you find satisfying: clicks, watchtime, survey responses, sharing, likes, and dislikes." — [YouTube Blog, "On YouTube's recommendation system"](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [Official, primary, same post] On surveys: "we measure what we call 'valued watchtime'—the time spent watching a video that you consider valuable. We measure valued watchtime through user surveys that ask you to rate the video you watched from one to five stars... Only videos that you rate highly with four or five stars are counted as valued watchtime." Also: "Based on the responses we do get, we've trained a machine learning model to predict potential survey responses for everyone." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [Official, primary, same post] On shares and likes: "On average, people are more likely to be satisfied by videos that they share or like... If you dislike a video, that's a signal that it probably wasn't something you enjoyed watching." The post says the system "doesn't follow a set formula". It also says that when borderline/tabloid content was demoted, "watchtime actually increased by 0.5% percent over the course of 2.5 months". — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [Official, primary, undated "How YouTube Works" page] Recommendations use "likes, dislikes, subscriptions, and feedback, including from satisfaction surveys" and viewing behaviour, and consider "the reputation and the quality of a channel". The page says nothing Shorts-specific. — [How YouTube Works: Recommendations](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)
- [Official, primary, 1 Oct 2026] TeamYouTube (JJ), in the Help Community post "Prioritizing original content on Shorts": "We evaluate billions of signals—including watch history, topic interests, and engagement—to ensure the best possible experience for viewers. To improve the Shorts experience for everyone, we're updating our recommendation systems to further prioritize original content and reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own." (Posted 15:30 UTC, 1 Oct 2026, per the page's embedded timestamp.) — [YouTube Help Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, 11 Apr 2024] Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie: "at the core, both the Shorts and long-form algorithms are the same and focus on viewer satisfaction. With Shorts, you might get more variety in your recommendations... You might also get broader discovery... it's better to think 'Shorts audience' than 'Shorts algorithm'." In the same post: "If not, you swipe away, no worries!" — [YouTube Blog, Rene's Top Five, Apr 11 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-april-11-2024-edition/)
- [Official, via secondary, 23 Aug 2023] Todd Sherman (then Shorts product lead) on Creator Insider: "The audience is the algorithm." "At the core, we're trying to get videos to people they value, both in long form and short form." "What we try and do with a view is have it encode for your intent of watching that thing." — [Search Engine Journal, Aug 28 2023](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/); timestamps in [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/) (primary video: [youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU))
- [Official, via secondary, 1 Sep 2026] Todd Beaupré (Sr. Director, Growth & Discovery), Creator Insider "YouTube Algorithm 2026 - What Creators Need to Know", interviewed by Ritchie. As transcribed by ppc.land: "No metric on its own is a good indicator of value." "We don't want to just assume that all time is valuable." On signal weighting: "It actually doesn't even work such that it's the same for you at all times." On Shorts, his only remark was that viewers "sometimes feel overwhelmed with all the choices that they have" and a swipeable feed "eases the burden of decisionmaking." — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [Official, via secondary, 1 Sep 2026] Beaupré on repetition: "you wouldn't be recommended a video more than eight times" (describing a repetition experiment). — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [Official, primary, from prior research] Shorts analytics card "How many chose to view": "the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away." No threshold is published. — [YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)
- [Official, primary, current Help page, general analytics funnel] YouTube frames performance as Appeal (CTR), Engagement (views) and "Satisfaction: Did they watch for a significant duration, and will their experience encourage them to return?" It says retention "Spikes are moments in your video that were re-watched or shared." — [YouTube Help 12942217, video version](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] "Hype" does **not** apply to Shorts. The Hype Help page lists "YouTube Shorts" among content "not eligible for hype". Hype is for YPP channels with 500–500,000 subscribers, during a video's first 7 days, in listed countries. India and the US are on the list; the Philippines is not. — [YouTube Help 15509925](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15509925)
- [Official, via secondary, 2024] A YouTube Liaison TikTok titled "How the algorithm surveys for satisfaction w/ Todd Beaupré" confirms surveys are part of ranking (title only; transcript not read). — [TikTok @youtubeliaison](https://www.tiktok.com/@youtubeliaison/video/7397833528852892934)

### Inferences
- For Shorts, the officially named levers are: choosing to view rather than swipe, watch time and satisfaction (surveys plus a model predicting them), shares and likes, dislikes and negative feedback, and now originality. Comments are not named in any official signal list I found. Loops and rewatches have no stated ranking role (see Q2).
- The Oct 2026 originality change targets re-uploaders, not original animation. It favours an original-story channel like this one, provided each video is clearly its own work.
- Hype is irrelevant for this Shorts-only channel. Asking viewers to "hype" a Short would be wrong advice.

### Gaps
- No official statement gives weights, thresholds (e.g. a required "viewed %"), or whether comments or shares count more on Shorts than on long-form.
- I found no official statement that satisfaction surveys are shown inside the Shorts feed specifically, though the general system uses them.
- The 1 Sep 2026 Beaupré quotes come from ppc.land's transcription of auto-generated audio. I did not watch the video.

## Q2. Length (up to 3 minutes), loops and whether rewatches count

### Takeaway
Officially, Shorts can be up to 3 minutes (since 15 Oct 2024), and staff advice is "as long as the story needs." Every start or replay counts as a public Short view (since 31 Mar 2025). "Engaged views", which decide YPP qualification, exclude loops. YouTube's Aug 2026 explainer says the view change did not change recommendations. No official statement says loops boost ranking.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, primary, from prior research] Since 15 Oct 2024, square or vertical uploads up to 3 minutes are Shorts. YouTube said it would "improve recommendations for longer Shorts in the coming months". — [YouTube Blog, "Tall updates coming to Shorts", Oct 3 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/); [YouTube Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- [Official, via secondary, Aug 2023] Sherman on length: "To what length do you need to tell your story?" (t=183s) — [OutlierKit transcript](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- [Official, primary, from prior research] Engaged views = "How many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops." — [YouTube Help 12220281](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- [Official, primary, 19 Aug 2026] YouTube Blog "engaged views explained": for Shorts, "The moment it starts to play, it's counted as a view." An engaged view means watching past the opening. For long-form, from 24 Aug 2026 a view counts from the first frame (including Home autoplay), and engaged views are clicks or "watching past the initial seconds". The post states "there's no change to recommendations" and "no change to the YouTube Partner Program", which stays "based on qualified views". The post does not mention loops. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)
- [Official, via secondary, 14 Aug 2026] Ritchie on Creator Insider, defining an engaged/qualified view: "someone didn't just see the first frame and swipe or cancel out". — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/shorts-watched-as-ads-do-not-count-toward-youtubes-20-million-view-bar/) (video: [youtube.com/watch?v=Olq_R5oPnm8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Olq_R5oPnm8))
- [Official, via secondary, undated] Ritchie: a Short should be "as long as it needs to be, but no longer. As short as it can be, but no shorter. If it's padded… or cut to the point of being unsatisfying, people will lose interest or get frustrated and swipe out." I did not find the primary. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)
- [Official, primary] Retention "Spikes" are moments "re-watched or shared". This could mean the audience was interested, or that "your content wasn't clear and your audience had to rewatch a part." — [YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Loops raise average view duration and APV (watch time), but YouTube says nothing about how much that counts for Shorts ranking, and loops do not add engaged views. Treat "make it loop" as an unverified tactic, not official guidance.
- YouTube itself reads a rewatch spike as possibly a clarity problem. For paradox Shorts, a spike on a logic step can mean the step was unclear, not that it was good.
- Nothing official contradicts the channel's 40–50 s target. The official line is "length the story needs, no padding."

### Gaps
- No official data on whether 3-minute Shorts are recommended as widely as shorter ones. The promised "improved recommendations" were never quantified.
- No official word on whether APV above 100% is rewarded.

## Q3. How Shorts drive subscriptions, Shorts-to-long-form, related-video links, the subscribe button

### Takeaway
YouTube has given very few official numbers on Shorts-to-subscriber conversion. The only firm one is music-specific (2023: artists active on Shorts got "more than 50%" of new subscribers from Shorts). Officially: Shorts don't trigger upload notifications, subscribing is a weaker signal than creators think, and recommendations follow the same traffic source. The tool YouTube provides to bridge Shorts to other content is the "related video" link. Studio's "Viewers across formats" card shows audience overlap.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, via secondary, 30 Mar 2023] YouTube Music head Lyor Cohen (blog post, reported by TechCrunch): "artists who are active on Shorts saw more than 50% of their new channel subscribers coming directly from their Shorts posts on average." This applies to music artists only. — [TechCrunch, Mar 30 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/30/youtubes-new-metric-shows-an-artists-reach-across-all-formats-including-shorts)
- [Official, via secondary, Jul 2022] YouTube said official artist channels uploading both Shorts and long-form saw better overall watch time and subscriber growth than long-form-only channels (secondary report; original post not found). — [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/youtube-shorts-statistics)
- [Official, primary, 28 Mar 2024] Ritchie: "Shorts don't trigger notifications on upload". Also: "performance in one traffic source also tends to affect that specific traffic source (search affects search, home affects home)." He would not "encourage anyone to uncheck [the notify-subscribers box] in hopes of improving performance". — [YouTube Blog, Rene's Top Five, Mar 28 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-28-2024-edition/)
- [Official, primary, 18 Apr 2024] Asked whether Shorts subscribers who ignore long-form uploads hurt long-form, Ritchie said: "Great question! And no, not really... Homepage recommendations primarily focus on data from the same traffic source... so reactions from other viewers on the homepage, not whether someone ignored a notification or skipped a video on the sub feed." — [YouTube Blog, Rene's Top Five, Apr 18 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-april-18-2024-edition/)
- [Official, via secondary, 1 Sep 2026] Beaupré said subscriptions are "the number one place that people are watching videos from channels they're subscribed to" (traffic source). On the subscribers traffic source: "probably all your videos have about maybe a 10% or less click-through rate... even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch." He said the subscribe action "isn't as strong as a lot of people think" and that "It's normal for an audience that has been interested in your channel to not be interested in every upload." — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [Official, via secondary] Beaupré's oft-quoted channel-split heuristic: "Same audience? Same channel. Different audience? Different channel." (date and primary not verified) — [Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel)
- [Official, primary, current] Related video on Shorts: "You can add a related video after you upload your Short to help direct viewers to your other YouTube content." It can link "your Videos, Shorts, and Live in the Shorts player". Viewers see "a clickable link below your channel handle." Requirements: "only available with advanced feature access", and the target "has to be public or unlisted". The page says nothing about click analytics. — [YouTube Help 14075157](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, current] Studio's "Viewers across formats" card shows "The breakdown and overlap of returning viewers consuming your content by formats you publish (Videos, Shorts, and Live)". YouTube adds: "Audience behavior is different across formats, therefore success may look different. Different formats are meant to support your channel goals, whether it's meaningful community or broader audience reach." — [YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, Aug 2024] Ritchie: "Check your Audience tab in Analytics, see which videos are growing your audience. Make more videos like those..." — [Tubefilter, Aug 26 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- [Official, primary] YouTube's Shorts creator page says Shorts are "perfect for reaching new audiences, giving old content a fresh twist, and testing out new ideas." — [YouTube Creators: Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/creators/shorts/)
- [Official, primary, Mar 2024] Beaupré: "YouTube is focused on Shorts because audiences have led us there" (SEJ prints "let us there"). — [Search Engine Journal, Mar 4 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- [Third-party, contrast] Marketing blogs claim Shorts subscribers convert only with a deliberate bridge (a clear CTA, an open loop, the related-video link). Roberto Blake reports Shorts viewers often don't convert to long-form. These are opinions and anecdotes. — [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/convert-shorts-viewers-to-subscribers); [Roberto Blake, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/robertoblake_youtube-shorts-arent-there-to-grow-your-activity-7389263512117747712-yGhF)

### Inferences
- Officially, a Shorts subscriber is not a guaranteed returning viewer: Shorts don't notify, and even subscribers skip about 90% of uploads in the sub feed (Beaupré, general statement). Subscriber count matters less than whether each Short satisfies the feed audience.
- The Audience tab and "Viewers across formats" are where YouTube tells creators to look for which Shorts grow the audience. Compare subscribers gained per video in Studio rather than relying on outside benchmarks.
- A Shorts-only channel can use the related-video link to chain a Short to a companion Short (for example part 2, or the answer to a riddle), once advanced features are unlocked. Click results can't be measured from official analytics.

### Gaps
- No official general (non-music) number on the share of subscribers coming from Shorts, on Shorts-to-long-form conversion rates, or on subscribers per 1,000 Shorts views.
- No official statement on whether an in-video "subscribe" call to action helps or hurts Shorts. No official documentation on how the subscribe button in the Shorts player behaves.
- I did not locate an official Help page defining a "Subscribers from Shorts" metric. Third parties mention one, but it could not be confirmed.

## Q4. Posting time, frequency, and "test audience then expand" (official vs myth)

### Takeaway
Officially, posting time matters little for evergreen content: videos "end up in the same place" after weeks. YouTube says it does not optimise for upload hour, and there is no reward threshold for upload frequency. Early Shorts views are exploratory: the system tries to find viewers who will enjoy the video. Beaupré (Sep 2026) adds that a new video reaches people who have never watched the channel within the first hour, so subscribers are not a gating test group. Re-uploading flops risks being seen as spam.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, primary, 11 Apr 2024] Ritchie's tip of the week: "The absolute best time to post your video on YouTube is when it best fits your schedule and your wellbeing... after a couple of weeks or a month, videos tend to end up in the same place regardless of what exact hour of the day or night they were posted." For short-shelf-life content, "post as soon as you can". For evergreen content, "you can post at any time. Just be aware that if you post at off-hours, views might accumulate more slowly at first". — [YouTube Blog, Apr 11 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-april-11-2024-edition/)
- [Official, via secondary, Aug 2023] Sherman on posting time: "It's not something that we think about optimizing for." On frequency: "There is nothing in our systems that's like when you hit the five video or 10 short threshold per day". He encouraged better videos over many low-quality ones. — [OutlierKit transcript with timestamps](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- [Official, via secondary, Aug 2023] Sherman on testing: the system tries to "Find a set of people that may enjoy your video". Early views, often a few hundred to a few thousand, are exploratory before traffic may drop off (paraphrase in SEJ). On re-uploading: "There is a risk there that that gets seen as spam in our systems." And: "If that didn't go how you wanted to go, learn from it and move forward and try again." — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- [Official, via secondary, 1 Sep 2026] Beaupré on the first hour: "We see diverse audiences", "videos are reaching people who've never watched the channel before", "it's not only your subscribers who will see it". On early analytics: "We're not basing decisions just on those initial moments." — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [Official, via secondary, Mar 2024] Beaupré: "If your last video wasn't so great and your next video is great, we want to realize the potential of each video." And: "We aim to not overemphasize historical data if that data isn't particularly predictive of future video performance." — [Search Engine Journal, Mar 4 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- [Official, primary, current Help page] Average view duration "can help you spot high-performing videos, anticipate seasonal changes, and decide when to upload new videos." This is the only Help mention of upload timing found. — [YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, YPP activity rule from 1 Feb 2027] A YPP channel counts as "active" with "1,000 qualified watch hours in the past 365 days, or 1 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, or 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts uploaded every 90 days". This is the only official cadence number, and it is a minimum for staying active, not a growth recommendation. — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- [Third-party, contrast] Buffer and others publish "best times" from aggregate data that disagree with each other. YouTube's own line is to use your audience's analytics. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)

### Inferences
- The channel's 4:30 PM / 7:30 PM IST trial may change first-24-hour views (which the trial measures), but the official position says lifetime views converge after weeks. Compare 7-day or 28-day views as well, not only the first 24 hours.
- "Subscribers first, then expand" is not the official model. Officially, a Short is tried on a set of likely-interested viewers (including non-subscribers), and its reach grows or fades with their response.
- Two a day is fine officially: there is no frequency bonus or penalty, only the quality warning. Do not delete and re-upload flops.

### Gaps
- No official statement on how long Shorts keep being tested or revived (a "second wave" weeks later), or on whether posting two Shorts close together splits the test audience.
- No official country-specific timing guidance (India, Philippines, US).

## Q5. Official policies relevant to this channel

### Takeaway
(a) Since 15 Jul 2025, the "repetitious content" rule is called "inauthentic content". The current page (undated sections, live Oct 2026) explicitly bans "characters... put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome", "templated storylines", and template-made AI content. It allows a series with recurring characters only if "each video has a distinct storyline". (b) AI disclosure is needed only for realistic synthetic content. Fully animated or fantasy content and AI-assisted scripts are exempt. (c) Advertiser-friendly rules allow non-graphic dramatised discussion and educational debate, and religion is covered only under hate/protected groups. (d) YPP thresholds change on 1 Feb 2027: new entrants need 20M Shorts views in 90 days, and earning from the Shorts pool needs a rolling 10M.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Inauthentic / mass-produced content**
- [Official, primary] Change note: "July 15, 2025: We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced. We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.' This type of content has always been ineligible for monetization... There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos." — [YouTube channel monetization policies, Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, current text; sections undated] Under "Generic or Repetitive Content": content "that looks like it's made with a template, or that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel... What's important is that the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value."
  - Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different"; "Similar content, like a series following a set of characters across episodes or a channel that does product reviews, but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept."
  - Not allowed: "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome (i.e., using a highly similar storyline template across multiple videos)"; "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective".
  — [Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, current text] Under "Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content": content that "relies heavily on emotionally manipulative formulas, mimics existing formats or stories to a degree that the videos feel interchangeable, or appears designed to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views."
  - Allowed: "Content that expresses your unique creative voice, like using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented"; "using AI to edit your video scripts"; "a Short that puts your own personalized spin on a trend". The page adds: "If you use automated tools or templates to help create your content, the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision".
  - Not allowed: "Content that lacks a clear narrative arc or logical progression, such as videos that stitch together unrelated or inconsistent AI clips", and "Content that uses deceptive or misleading imagery or narratives".
  — [Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, current text] "AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics": not monetizable if "content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, finances, or politics." — [Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [Official, via secondary, 15 Jul 2025] TeamYouTube community manager Carlos called it "a minor update to our longstanding guideline on repetitious content". He said the content "includes repetitive or mass-produced content that users often consider spam" and "This content was never eligible for monetization". — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/). Ritchie also called it a "minor update" (per [GetCoai](https://getcoai.com/news/youtube-cracks-down-on-ai-generated-spam-with-new-monetization-rules)).
- [Official, primary, 1 Oct 2026] Ranking (not only monetization): "Channels identified as primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes will likely see less distribution within the Shorts feed... Rather than making minor technical edits or template-based changes simply to alter a video, focus on bringing your own voice, storytelling, or distinct point of view." — [YouTube Help Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en)
- [Third-party, contrast] Gulf News called the July 2025 change "one of the most aggressive pushes yet". Others said little changes for most creators. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [subsub.io](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2025)

**(b) AI disclosure (altered or synthetic content)**
- [Official, primary, current] Disclosure is required for realistic content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "Alters footage of a real event or place", or "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", and for AI music that is the main focus. Exempt: non-realistic content such as "Someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world", "AI-generated or altered animation of a missile in a fully animated video", and production help such as "using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script". Disclosing "won't limit a video's audience or affect its eligibility to earn money". Consistently failing to disclose can lead to a label being applied, removal, or YPP suspension. Labels can also be applied automatically (YouTube AI tools, C2PA metadata, detection). For non-photorealistic or animated AI content, a label "may appear in the expanded description". — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) (fetched summary; quotes as returned)

**(c) Advertiser-friendly guidelines: controversial issues, sensitive events, debate, religion**
- [Official, primary, current] "Controversial issues are topics associated with trauma, abuse, or that may be unsettling for viewers" (child abuse, sexual abuse, self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, domestic abuse, abortion). Full ads are allowed for "Non-graphic but descriptive or dramatized content". Limited ads for thumbnails with graphic depictions. No ads for graphic depictions. — [YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] Sensitive events: full ads are allowed for "Discussions involving the loss of life or tragedy that are not exploitative or dismissive". No ads for content "that profits from or exploits a sensitive event". — [Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] Debate: full ads are allowed for "Public debates on protected groups without inciting hatred and violent confrontation against them". Limited ads for "Debate that may include offensive language but is intended to educate". Religion appears only as a protected characteristic under hateful content. "Educational" means "informing or teaching about a subject without deliberately misinforming the audience", and "context is very important." — [Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] The monetization policies page logs "March 10, 2025: ... videos undergoing checks (including private videos) may receive an additional review that may be completed by a human... decisions may take up to 24 hours." — [Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**(d) YPP thresholds (Shorts)**
- [Official, primary, current until 31 Jan 2027] "Get 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". Qualified Shorts views exclude private, unlisted and deleted Shorts, ad-campaign views, and image posts. Watch hours from the Shorts feed don't count toward 4,000 hours. — [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- [Official, primary, announced Aug 2026, effective 1 Feb 2027] "Starting Feb. 1, 2027, YPP entry thresholds for new creators are changing to 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, in addition to still needing 1k subscribers. If you are already in YPP, your status is not impacted." Shorts earnings: "To earn each month from the Shorts Creator Pool, creators will now need to maintain 10M qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days. If you miss this threshold, you will not be removed from YPP". The lower tier (fan funding, Shopping, Creator Partnerships) is unchanged at "500 subscribers and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last year or 3M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". Other changes: Targeted Shorts Ads (a "direct 45% revenue share" when an ad targets five or fewer channels) and new Shorts incentive programs. Existing partners must accept the new terms by 31 Jan 2027 or stop earning from those features. — [YouTube Help 12843009, "Updates to the YouTube Partner Program"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- [Third-party] The 10 Aug 2026 announcement date and the 14 Aug 2026 Creator Insider explainer are reported by vidIQ and ppc.land. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/shorts-watched-as-ads-do-not-count-toward-youtubes-20-million-view-bar/)
- [Official, via secondary, Jun 2025] CEO Neal Mohan at Cannes Lions: Shorts are "now averaging over 200 billion daily views". This is not comparable with the earlier 70B figure, because of the Mar 2025 view-count change. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/youtube-shorts-200-billion-daily-views)

### Inferences
- **Biggest policy risk for this channel:** the genie-style formula (one rule, kid twists it, authority freezes, "Either way…", either/or close) matches the literal wording "characters... put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome (i.e., using a highly similar storyline template...)". Defences the policy names: a distinct storyline, focus or concept per video, varied outcomes, and the creator's own perspective. CLAUDE.md's "vary character and setting" rule is necessary but may not be sufficient: the *outcome* and story shape also need to vary.
- "Using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented" and AI script editing are explicitly allowed. Original invented stories with an AI voice are fine as long as they aren't templated.
- Animated, non-realistic stories most likely need no AI disclosure. Realistic depictions of real people or real events (e.g. "funny history" with realistic imagery) would. The label isn't a reach penalty.
- Religion and controversial topics are monetizable when discussed non-graphically and educationally, without disparaging protected groups. Debate framing is explicitly listed as acceptable.
- At India/Philippines-weighted RPMs, the Feb 2027 Shorts-route bar (20M views in 90 days for new entrants) is steep. If the channel isn't in YPP before 1 Feb 2027, the 10M bar under current rules is the window. Even once in YPP, Shorts-pool earnings need a rolling 10M per 90 days. The 500-subscriber / 3M-view lower tier stays open.
- If finance long-form ever happens, the "AI persona as human expert on finances" rule applies to an AI narrator presented as an advisor.

### Gaps
- The "Generic or Repetitive", "Unsatisfying or Off-putting" and "AI Personas" sections carry no date on the page. They are live as of 18 Oct 2026, but I couldn't confirm when each was added.
- The AI disclosure findings are based on a fetched summary of the page; exact quotes were not re-checked against raw HTML.
- No official guidance specifically on synthetic narrator voices (non-cloned stock TTS) beyond the "realistic" test.

## Q6. Official advice on hooks, the first seconds, captions and audio for Shorts

### Takeaway
YouTube publishes almost no Shorts craft advice. Official material says: tell the story at the length it needs, don't rely on thumbnails (most are never seen in the feed), make the first part strong because audiences drop over time, use built-in automatic captions and voiceover tools, and add your own voice and storytelling (originality). Hook-timing claims ("1 second", "3 seconds") come from creators, not YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, via secondary, Aug 2023] Sherman: "Most thumbnails are never seen because people are in the feed." (t=260s) — [OutlierKit transcript](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [Official, primary, current Help page, general retention advice] "If your top moments happen later in your video, consider introducing that content earlier in the video. Audience numbers tend to decrease as videos progress, so try to catch their attention earlier." On intros, YouTube suggests making the title or thumbnail better reflect the content and experimenting with the opening. (Written for long-form, with a 30 s "typical intro".) — [YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] The Shorts creator page lists tools: "Trim and edit clips together," "Add voiceovers and automatic captions," "Apply filters"; "Add sound" for music. It gives no performance claims. — [YouTube Creators: Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/creators/shorts/)
- [Official, primary, 1 Oct 2026] "focus on bringing your own voice, storytelling, or distinct point of view to the conversation." — [YouTube Help Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en)
- [Official blog, but creator's opinion, from prior research] Jenny Hoyos on the YouTube Blog: "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." — [YouTube Blog, Shorts deep dive](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- [Official, primary, from prior research] Music in Shorts up to 3 min: most songs usable for up to 90 s, some only 60 or 30 s, with Content ID implications. — [YouTube Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- [Official, primary] The "Unsatisfying or Off-putting" policy disallows content "designed to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views". It allows "a cohesive storyline that doesn't rely solely on shock value". — [Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

### Inferences
- The only official hook principle is "earn the view" (a view should encode intent; the viewer chose to view rather than swipe), plus "put your best moments early." The channel's hook rules (from its own analytics) are consistent with this. YouTube gives no specific timing.
- Hooks built on a contradiction or tension are fine. Hooks that shock for their own sake, with no narrative payoff, now carry monetization risk under the "Unsatisfying" policy.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube guidance found on burned-in captions vs auto-captions, narration pace, or audio mixing for Shorts.
- No official statement on how many seconds count as the "initial seconds" for an engaged view.
