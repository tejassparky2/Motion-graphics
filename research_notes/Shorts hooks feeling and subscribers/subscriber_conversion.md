# Shorts subscriber conversion: rates, drivers and channel strategies (as of Oct 2026)

Labels: **[Official]** = YouTube/Google statement; **[Data]** = dataset with stated sample; **[Anecdotal]** = single creator / vendor / blog claim without published method; **[Academic]** = peer-reviewed or thesis research.
Channel context: Interestingly Strange, 207 subs (15 Oct 2026), ~170K views / 28 days, target 1,000 subs before Feb 2027; 2D animated 40-50 s Shorts; recurring kid "The Last Bencher"; audience India 21%, Philippines 16%, US 14%.
Research budget note: ~18 searches/fetches; much of the web on this topic is vendor SEO content with recycled, unsourced numbers. Only one rigorous public dataset on subs-per-view (Galloway/Gileta 2023) was found.

## 1. Reported subscriber conversion rates: Shorts vs long-form (subs per 1,000 views)

### Takeaway
The only sourced dataset (Galloway & Gileta, 2023, 33 channels / 5,400 Shorts / 3.3B views) puts Shorts at ~1.7 subs per 1,000 views vs ~2.3 for long-form, with Shorts *beating* long-form on channels over 1M subs. Vendor blogs cluster around 0.5-5 subs per 1,000 Shorts views, but none publish methods.

### Cited Findings
- [Data] Paddy Galloway & Chris Gileta studied 3.3B views, 5,400 Shorts from 33 channels across niches (April 2023). Long-form converted **22.7 subs per 10,000 views (2.27/1,000)**, Shorts **16.9 per 10,000 (1.69/1,000)**. For channels **>1M subs, Shorts converted better than long-form**. Galloway calls it "early research"; averages were checked against medians — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); figures as reported by [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html) and [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315)
- [Data] Same study: most Shorts were 20-40 s; longer Shorts averaged far more views; Shorts with average view duration >50 s averaged 4.1M views; Shorts below 60% "viewed vs swiped away" rarely did well, best performers were 70-90%; likes/shares/comments showed no strong relationship with performance — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- [Data, causal caveat] Galloway's explanation of the big-channel reversal is a theory, not measured: "These channels are making better Shorts on average that reach more viewers, and are using better call-to-actions inside those Shorts." He had no CTA data — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] Benchmarks quoted by tool/blog sites (no methods): 0.1-0.3% (1-3 subs/1,000) "average" — [ToolGenieHub calculator](https://toolgeniehub.com/tools/youtube-shorts-conversion-calculator); 1M Shorts views → 500-5,000 subs (0.5-5/1,000), and below 0.3% "usually means the Short is not communicating who the channel is for" — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-to-subscribers-strategy-2026); aim >0.5% (5/1,000), >1% strong, >2% exceptional and usually tight-niche — [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-explained). One blog's "1-3% of viewers" claim is likely long-form or inflated — [Touhfa](https://touhfa.art/blog/growth/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-subscribers/)
- [Official, measurement caveat] Not every Shorts swipe counts as a view; YouTube requires "some intent to watch," a "meaningful threshold" it does not publish and adjusts over time (Todd Sherman, Shorts product lead, Aug 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/). A later change reportedly counts a Shorts view when it starts playing (with "engaged views" kept as the older measure), which makes subs-per-view ratios not comparable across time — [Substack summary](https://podcaststrategy.substack.com/p/youtube-shorts-200-billion-views) (secondary)
- [Official] For YouTube Music artists active on Shorts, on average **>50% of new channel subscribers came directly from their Shorts** (YouTube Music head's blog, reported 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/30/youtubes-new-metric-shows-an-artists-reach-across-all-formats-including-shorts)
- [Data, correlational] AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 English channels (Mar 2025-Mar 2026, 11 niches, each channel ≥40 videos incl. ≥10 long-form): mixing in Shorts (10-70% of uploads) correlated with higher median subs in fitness (+41%), business (+39%), education (+37% at 100K-1M); long-only led in gaming (+85%), beauty, kids' entertainment (+63% at 100K-1M), entertainment at larger sizes. Channels that shifted strategy by >30 pts mid-year (n=1,247) underperformed. Authors flag correlation-not-causation, small n in some cells, and AIR sells audits — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)

### Inferences
- A realistic planning range for a small story/education Shorts channel is ~1-3 subs per 1,000 views; 5+/1,000 is a strong result. Interestingly Strange's own figure should be computed in Studio (subs gained / views per Short and per 28 days) and compared against Galloway's 1.69/1,000.
- Arithmetic: at ~170K views / 28 days, 1.69 subs/1,000 would yield ~290 subs per 28 days, i.e. the ~793 subs still needed would take about 2.7 months at constant views. Raising conversion to 3/1,000 roughly halves that. This is an extrapolation, not a forecast.
- Because view-counting rules changed, compare the channel to itself (same period definition), not to old benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No vidIQ, TubeBuddy or Social Blade dataset with Shorts subs-per-1,000 figures was found; no YouTube official benchmark exists.
- Galloway's later (claimed 2026, 500+ channels, "2.8x faster sub growth") study appears only in a marketing blog and could not be verified — [MarketMaker](https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-your-channel).
- No public breakdown by niche/format (animated story vs talking-head) or by viewer country.
- No Reddit r/NewTubers / r/PartneredYoutube threads with Studio numbers surfaced in searches.

## 2. Do "subscribe" CTAs help or hurt? Placement, A/B tests, "follow for part 2"

### Takeaway
No controlled public A/B test of Shorts subscribe CTAs was found. The best evidence is indirect: Galloway's theory that top channels use better CTAs, YouTube's own advice to put a verbal + visual CTA in the last 5 seconds for related-video links, and consistent practitioner advice that a *specific reason* ("subscribe for one riddle a day") beats a generic "subscribe."

### Cited Findings
- [Official] YouTube's July 2026 Related Videos guide: in the **final 5 seconds**, give a verbal prompt plus visual cues (pointing to the link, flashing the long-form thumbnail); make clips stand alone; match the linked video to the Short's intent — [YouTube Blog, 14 Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- [Data, theory only] Galloway attributes >1M-sub channels' better Shorts conversion partly to "better call-to-actions," without measuring it — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] Generic subscribe prompts reportedly "convert poorly no matter how well the video performed"; recommendation is a CTA with a reason; some suggest a static end frame in the last ~2 s since Shorts have no end screens; others suggest testing mid-video vs end CTAs and checking the retention curve for drops right at the CTA — summarized from [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-to-subscribers-strategy-2026), [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/best-youtube-shorts-call-to-actions), [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-explained), [DriveEditor](https://driveeditor.com/blog/best-cta-formats-for-short-videos)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] Claims such as "380% conversion lift" or "270% gain from a three-placement strategy" appear on single vendor pages without methodology; treat as unverified — (seen in search results for [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/best-youtube-shorts-call-to-actions) / [StoryShort](https://storyshort.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-ctas-for-youtube-shorts))
- [Anecdotal/vendor] A series guide argues "Part 1 / Part 2" sequencing is not serialization; a real series needs a repeating structure (episode number in title/on-screen, recurring host or character). It claims series see 2-3x higher sub conversion than standalone Shorts, citing unnamed "creator analytics platforms" — unverified — [Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/youtube-shorts-series-how-to-turn-viewers-into-subscribers/)
- [Data] Galloway: Shorts below 60% viewed-vs-swiped rarely performed; the top performers were 70-90%. So any CTA that costs early retention (e.g. a "subscribe" ask at the start) affects distribution, which matters more than the CTA — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)

### Inferences
- Never put the CTA in the first seconds (it competes with the hook, which the channel's own rules protect). Put a short, specific, in-world CTA after the payoff: e.g. the Last Bencher line "New riddle tomorrow. Subscribe so you don't miss it." Keep it ≤2 s so it doesn't hurt the end of the retention curve or the loop.
- "Follow for part 2" cliffhangers withhold the payoff; viewers who feel cheated may swipe or dislike, and the Short may underperform on completion. Safer: close each story fully, then tease the *next* story ("Tomorrow, the Last Bencher meets a genie who only grants wrong wishes").
- The channel can run its own test: alternate CTA / no-CTA versions over 10+ Shorts of similar format and compare subs per 1,000 views and the retention drop over the last 3 s.

### Gaps
- No public A/B test with sample size on Shorts CTAs (verbal vs on-screen, start vs end) was found.
- No data on "Part 2" cliffhanger Shorts vs complete stories.

## 3. Recurring character / series / consistent identity and subscription

### Takeaway
No direct dataset links a recurring Shorts character to higher subscribe rates, but theory and adjacent research support it: parasocial relationships (PSR) predict subscription intention in several YouTube/Twitch studies, and mere exposure raises liking up to roughly 10-20 exposures before over-exposure lowers it. Practitioner consensus is that viewers subscribe when they can predict "more of the same value."

### Cited Findings
- [Academic] Horton & Wohl (1956, *Psychiatry*, "Mass Communication and Para-Social Interaction: Observations on Intimacy at a Distance") introduced parasocial interaction: one-sided felt intimacy with media personae, based on observation of radio/TV personalities — [Wikipedia: Parasocial interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasocial_interaction); [SIUE theory summary](https://aura-lab.siue.edu/theories/parasocial-interaction/)
- [Academic] Uğurhan & Yaşar (2022), n=449 YouTube users (Turkey): PSR plus information, escape and entertainment motives positively predicted intention to become a (paid) subscriber; PSR partly mediated the motives — [Dicle Univ. repository](https://acikerisim.dicle.edu.tr/items/1b3c9c9b-e08f-43c1-b29c-b2f52e6f7b28)
- [Academic, thesis] Twitch (Brazil, 2023): friendship-type PSR, exposure time and prior interaction correlated with subscription purchases; romantic-type PSR did not — [UnB thesis PDF](https://bdm.unb.br/bitstream/10483/38874/1/2023_LucaAndradeDaMata_tcc.pdf)
- [Academic, counter-evidence] Esports live-stream study (Primadana & Windasari): only perceived expertise significantly predicted subscribe intention; PSR did not — [JBME](https://ejournal.upi.edu/index.php/JBME/article/view/64950)
- [Academic] Korean study (n=322): PSI higher for live than recorded content; PSI predicted fandom formation but had no direct effect on viewing behaviour — [KSCAP](https://accesson.kr/kscap/v.25/1/87/40223)
- [Academic] Mere exposure (Zajonc 1968): repeated exposure increases liking. Bornstein's 1989 meta-analysis of 208 experiments found the effect robust, strongest for briefly presented unfamiliar stimuli, typically peaking within 10-20 presentations, with liking possibly declining after longer series; Montoya et al. (2017) meta-analysis found an inverted-U — [Wikipedia: Mere-exposure effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mere-exposure_effect)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] Real series = repeating structure + episode markers + recurring host/character — [Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/youtube-shorts-series-how-to-turn-viewers-into-subscribers/)
- [Anecdotal] Practitioners warn of a "revolving door of subscribers who come for one thing and leave once they realize that's not what you actually do" — [Simon Owens, "The shallow branding of shortform video"](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/the-shallow-branding-of-shortform)
- [Official] The Shorts feed deliberately favours variety because viewers flip through hundreds of Shorts — so a viewer may see a given channel rarely unless they subscribe or visit the channel (Todd Sherman) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- [Data] Example of an animated recurring-format channel: Bored Shorts TV, 508K subs / 236M upload views (June 2024); growth drivers not isolated — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bored_Shorts_TV)

### Inferences
- Because the feed rarely re-shows the same channel, recognizability within the first 1-2 s (same character design, title card, "The Last Bencher" label, colour palette) is what lets a second or third exposure register as "that channel again" — the mere-exposure and PSR mechanisms only work if the viewer recognizes the repeat.
- Friendship-type PSR (a likeable, clever kid the viewer roots for) matches the Twitch finding better than a faceless narrator. Giving the Last Bencher a consistent voice, catchphrase and personality likely helps; this is theory-based, not measured on Shorts.
- Inverted-U: rotate stories/settings (already a channel rule for the "inauthentic content" policy) while keeping the identity constant; identical formats repeated too often risk fatigue.
- Tension to manage: the channel mixes Last Bencher riddles, genie-style stories, paradoxes and animals. A viewer who subscribes for one may not want the others; a common identity ("clever twist in 45 seconds") and the narrator's voice can be the unifying "promise."

### Gaps
- No Shorts-specific study comparing subscribe rates for series/recurring characters vs one-offs.
- PSR studies are mostly about live-streamers/vloggers (human hosts), not animated characters; no study found on PSR with animated Shorts characters.

## 4. What makes a viewer subscribe after a Short (expectation of more, channel page, name/avatar, pinned comment, playlists, related link)

### Takeaway
Documented levers are thin: YouTube officially offers the Related Video link (one per Short) and advises a last-5-second CTA; practitioners agree conversion depends on the viewer understanding "who the channel is for" and expecting more of the same. Most Shorts viewers never leave the feed, so the subscribe decision mostly happens on the Short itself.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Related Video link: clickable link on a Short that routes to a long-form video, livestream or another Short on the channel; set in Studio > Content > Short > "Related video." YouTube describes viewer types: Shorts-only, context-dependent, exploratory — [YouTube Blog, Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- [Official] YouTube introduced Shorts-to-long-form linking (Sept 2023) after removing clickable links in Shorts descriptions/comments as an anti-spam measure — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-related-links-connect-shorts-long-videos/)
- [Anecdotal] Related-link click-through is often low (sometimes <1%); a Sept 2026 piece says most Shorts viewers never leave the Shorts feed, so the link "helps a little, but not much" — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-not-converting-viewers); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-to-long-form/)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] Low conversion (<0.3%) "usually means the Short is not communicating who the channel is for" — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-to-subscribers-strategy-2026)
- [Anecdotal] A 30-day Shorts experiment gained ~46 subs, mostly from watch pages rather than the Shorts feed — single anecdote (via [Goodreads blog of Andrew Macarthy](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/6456695.Andrew_Macarthy/blog?page=9))
- [Official] YouTube advises skipping custom Shorts thumbnails because most aren't seen in the feed — but they appear on the channel page Shorts tab (Todd Sherman) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)

### Inferences
- The channel name "Interestingly Strange" and the avatar shown on every Short are the only identity cues a feed viewer gets; a clear, readable avatar of the Last Bencher (or the channel's mascot) and a one-line channel description ("Clever twist stories, riddles and paradoxes in 45 seconds") tell "who it's for."
- Link each Short (Related Video) to the next/previous episode of the same series (Short-to-Short is allowed), which suits a channel without long-form. Add playlists per series ("The Last Bencher", "Genie stories", "Paradoxes") so a channel-page visitor sees a back catalogue that promises more of the same.
- Pinned comment with the debate question (already a channel practice) gives returning reasons; clickable links in Shorts comments are not reliable (removed in 2023), so use the Related Video field instead.

### Gaps
- No data found on how channel-page visits after a Short affect subscribe rates, or on avatar/name clarity effects.
- No official YouTube data on Related Video link CTR.

## 5. Comments, replies and debates → subscriptions or returning viewers

### Takeaway
No credible data shows that replying to comments raises subscriptions; claims like "2x sub growth for channels replying to >50% of comments" are unsourced. Galloway found engagement metrics weakly related to Shorts performance. The defensible metric to watch is Studio's returning viewers.

### Cited Findings
- [Data] Galloway: likes, shares and comments showed no strong relationship with Shorts performance (33 channels) — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- [Anecdotal] "Channels replying to >50% of comments see 2x subscriber growth" — no source or method given; treat as unverified — [JustTalk Studios](https://www.justtalkstudios.com/post/why-responding-to-comments-can-double-your-growth-on-youtube)
- [Anecdotal] Reply-to-return link is plausible via ordinary human behaviour, but recommendation impact is "small and unproven"; watch returning viewers in the Audience tab, expect movement over weeks — [ReplySpark](https://replyspark.ai/blog/youtube-comment-engagement-ranking-signal)
- [Anecdotal] Replies aren't a major ranking factor compared with CTR, watch time, retention, satisfaction — [BlackHatWorld thread](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/does-replying-to-youtube-comments-help-a-channel-grow.1844383/)
- [Academic] 2025 study of ~15,000 comments on one YouTube video identifies drivers of replies to initial comments; does not measure subs/returning viewers — [RePEc/HAL](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-04931878.html)
- [Anecdotal/vendor] vidIQ recommends replying quickly and asking follow-up questions to keep threads going — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/responding-youtube-comments/)

### Inferences
- Debate replies are cheap and consistent with PSR theory (prior interaction correlated with subscribing on Twitch), but the channel should judge them by its own returning-viewer and subs data, not expect a measured lift.

### Gaps
- No controlled or large-sample data on comment replies → subscribers for Shorts.

## 6. Audience geography: India and the Philippines

### Takeaway
India is reported as the largest Shorts audience (~24% of global Shorts viewers, secondary source), and Indian viewers heavily favour Indic-language content; English story content competes in a smaller slice. Reliable Philippines Shorts data was not found. Note: YouTube's Shorts lead said posting time generally doesn't matter except for news.

### Cited Findings
- [Anecdotal/secondary] India ≈24% of global Shorts audience, US ≈12% (marketing roundup citing Market.biz) — [inBeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/youtube-shorts-statistics) / [Loopex](https://www.loopexdigital.com/blog/youtube-shorts-statistics)
- [Data, third-party 2022] Hindi accounted for 71.88% of Shorts viewership in India (Good Creator Co.) — via [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2023/03/youtube-shorts-longer-than-15-seconds-gets-maximum-share-of-viewership-report/)
- [Official, 2021] ~93% of Indian YouTube viewers prefer Indic-language content — via search result summary of [TechRadar/Google](https://www.techradar.com/news/1-in-3-indians-watch-online-videos-usually-in-hindi-says-google) (older)
- [Official, 2025] YouTube India year-end analysis: 77% of Gen Z viewers in India watched content translated from another language; MrBeast's multi-language dubbing drew 47M+ new Indian subscribers — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/77gen-z-india-watched-dubbed-content-10892203)
- [Anecdotal] Precise Philippines Shorts vs long-form data is "tricky to get"; claims of more interactive Filipino viewers lack method — [RichestPH, May 2025](https://richestph.com/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-which-is-king-in-the-philippine-market/)
- [Official] Posting time "generally doesn't matter" for Shorts except news (Todd Sherman, 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)

### Inferences
- The separate Hinglish channel matches the Indic-language preference; the English channel's Indian viewers are likely English-comfortable students — the "last bencher" school setting is culturally familiar in India (the term is Indian English), which may raise relatability there.
- Multi-language audio tracks (dubbing) worked at scale for MrBeast in India; YouTube's auto-dubbing could in principle add Hindi audio to the English Shorts, but whether it is available for this channel and helps Shorts was not researched.
- Shorts views from India/Philippines are worth less in RPM but count fully toward subscribers; a 1,000-sub goal is indifferent to geography.

### Gaps
- No Think with Google India/APAC report on Shorts specifically found; no reliable Philippines Shorts data; no country-level subs-per-view data.

## 7. Risks: churn from inconsistency, "Shorts subscribers don't watch," effect on long-form

### Takeaway
No public data quantifies unsubscribe/churn rates for Shorts-acquired subscribers. YouTube says Shorts and long-form recommendations are separate and that it doesn't see Shorts as cannibalizing long-form; practitioners report Shorts subs rarely watch long-form and churn when content changes. The 18,000-channel AIR data shows channels that change strategy sharply mid-year underperform (correlational).

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Shorts and long-form algorithms work differently; YouTube says it doesn't see Shorts cannibalizing long-form (Todd Sherman, 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- [Anecdotal] Agency claim: long-form views fell as creators posted more Shorts; <0.2% of Shorts viewers convert to long-form — [Roberto Blake, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/robertoblake_youtube-shorts-arent-there-to-grow-your-activity-7389263512117747712-yGhF)
- [Anecdotal] Creator reports new subs wanted more Shorts, not long-form — [bbautista Substack](https://bbautista.substack.com/p/my-failed-experiment-with-youtube); "revolving door" of subs who leave when content changes — [Simon Owens](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/the-shallow-branding-of-shortform)
- [Anecdotal] Shorts subs "subscribed on a 30-second experience" and engage less with long-form — [TubeCore](https://tubecore.xpanddigital.io/blog/youtube-shorts-vs-longform)
- [Data, correlational] Channels that shifted Shorts share by >30 points mid-year underperformed; above ~55% Shorts share long-form performance reportedly dropped — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- [Anecdotal/unverified] Marketing blog cites a 500+ channel analysis claiming hybrid channels grew subs 2.8x faster with no negative long-form impact — [MarketMaker](https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-your-channel)

### Inferences
- For a Shorts-only channel, the "Shorts subs don't watch long-form" risk mainly matters if the owner later launches long-form ("what if," money, funny history ideas). Building that audience would need explicit bridges (related links, a long-form version of a popular Short's topic).
- Consistency risk is relevant: subscribers who join for the Last Bencher may churn if the feed shifts to unrelated formats; keep a stable core identity even while varying stories.
- The YPP Shorts route depends on subs plus Shorts views; subscriber counts can fall through churn and YouTube's spam/closed-account cleanups, so a small buffer above 1,000 is prudent (inference; not researched).

### Gaps
- No public dataset on unsubscribe rates by acquisition source (Shorts vs long-form).
- Current YPP thresholds (e.g. 1,000 subs + 10M valid Shorts views in 90 days) were not re-verified in this research pass; check YouTube Help before relying on them.
