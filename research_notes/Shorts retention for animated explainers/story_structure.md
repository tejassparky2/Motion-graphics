# Story & Script Structure for 30-90s Vertical Explainer Shorts (finance parables / "how the rich trick you")

Tag legend: [Official] = YouTube/Google statement; [Academic] = peer-reviewed; [Data study] = analysis of a disclosed dataset; [Anecdotal/creator] = creator/educator advice or vendor blog without a disclosed dataset.
Research date: 2026-09-29.

## 1. What structures do top short-form scriptwriters and creator educators teach, and what is the academic basis (curiosity gap, open loops, but/therefore)?

### Takeaway
The dominant creator-taught short-form structure is: 1-second hook phrased as an open question/unexpected twist → foreshadow the payoff → causal "but/therefore" escalation (no "and then") → payoff delivered late → abrupt end. This maps onto well-replicated academic findings: curiosity is a deprivation state caused by a perceived knowledge gap (Loewenstein 1994), strongest at *moderate* prior knowledge (Kang et al. 2009), and it improves memory for the answer (Gruber et al. 2014); narrative absorption ("transportation") increases belief change (Green & Brock 2000) — relevant to finance-parable persuasion.

### Cited Findings
**Academic basis**
- [Academic] Loewenstein (1994), "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation," *Psychological Bulletin* 116, 75-98: curiosity is "a form of cognitively induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge or understanding" (information-gap theory). — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232440476_The_Psychology_of_Curiosity_A_Review_and_Reinterpretation); [SciSpace record](https://scispace.com/papers/the-psychology-of-curiosity-a-review-and-reinterpretation-2bj8cmzgxx)
- [Academic] Information-gap theory implies curiosity is low when a person knows too little *or* too much; it peaks with a small amount of knowledge (i.e., the gap must be salient and feel closable). — [Psychology Fanatic summary of Loewenstein](https://psychologyfanatic.com/information-gap-theory/) (secondary summary)
- [Academic] Kang, Hsu, Krajbich, Loewenstein, McClure, Wang & Camerer (2009), "The Wick in the Candle of Learning," *Psychological Science*: fMRI with 19 participants / 40 trivia questions; inverted-U relation between confidence and curiosity (curiosity highest at moderate confidence); curiosity correlated with caudate (anticipated-reward) activity; higher curiosity predicted better recall of surprising answers ~10 days later. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1308286)
- [Academic] Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014), *Neuron* 84(2):486-496: better memory for information people were curious about, and also for incidental material encountered during high-curiosity states; enhanced midbrain and nucleus accumbens activity. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25284006/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627314008046)
- [Academic] Green & Brock (2000), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 79, 701-721: "transportation" (absorption combining attention, imagery, feelings) increased story-consistent beliefs and favorable evaluations of protagonists; highly transported readers found fewer "false notes" in the story. — [PDF](http://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/the_role_of_transportation_in_the_persuasiveness_of_public_narratives.pdf); [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_theory_(psychology))
- [Academic] Curiosity-gap mechanics are also the basis of clickbait-detection modeling research (i.e., the same mechanism exploited by headlines). — [arXiv 1806.04212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.04212)

**But/Therefore (South Park)**
- [Anecdotal/creator] Trey Parker & Matt Stone (mtvU "Stand In," NYU Tisch "Storytelling Strategies" class, 2011): if "and then" belongs between beats "you're in trouble"; between every beat there should be "therefore" or "but"; they write out episode beats to check for "causation between each beat." — [Speakola transcript](https://speakola.com/arts/matt-stone-trey-parker-nyu-writing-class-2014); [Aerogramme](https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2014/03/06/writing-advice-from-south-parks-trey-parker-and-matt-stone/); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGUNqq3jVLg)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Jenny Hoyos (Shorts creator) explicitly applies but/therefore to Shorts: "I went on a walk, but it started raining. Therefore I started running back home" beats a sequential list. Her 4-step structure (per Jay Clouse interview breakdown): (1) very short hook, (2) foreshadowing of what's coming, (3) smooth transitions avoiding pace-breakers like "let's get started," (4) but/therefore storytelling; limit upfront detail. — [Jay Clouse LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jayclouse_jenny-hoyos-gave-me-her-exact-script-that-activity-7262878750684459010-NIZu)

**Hooks as open loops / expectation-breaks**
- [Official] YouTube Blog (Jan 28, 2025), Shorts product lead Todd Sherman with Jenny Hoyos: Hoyos: "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts"; she uses a "shock, intrigue, and satisfy" formula; Sherman notes creators crafting 15-second "moments." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Hoyos: "An easy way to think about an open loop is to think about it as a question rather than just making a statement" (e.g., "Can I eat this foot-long sub in six bites?" vs "I'm eating…"); check that viewers cannot guess the ending from title/thumbnail; "start with the setting your viewers already expect… then change one thing"; record multiple hook versions to test. — [KDCC summary of Hoyos talk](https://blog.kdcc.social/make-anything-go-viral-how-to-hook-and-reel-in-your-viewer/)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Hoyos writes the hook and the last line first, then fills the middle; hook/foreshadow lines kept to ≤3 s; endings abrupt. — [Medium write-up](https://medium.com/@10xquality/600m-viral-youtube-shorts-views-in-only-1-year-jenny-cracked-the-code-bcb43708f298) (secondary)
- [Anecdotal/creator] George Blackman (former scriptwriter for Ali Abdaal) teaches tension as: show viewer's current (wrong) behavior → explain the mechanism of why it fails → reveal what to do instead, with the specific payoff held to the end; hook = "context lean-in + scroll stop + contrarian snapback" opening one curiosity loop. — [Write With AI summary](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/write-a-killer-youtube-script-like); [georgeblackman.com](https://www.georgeblackman.com/)

### Inferences
- A finance parable ("the rich guy did X, but the bank did Y, therefore Z…") is naturally a but/therefore chain; the "trick" reveal is the payoff and should be withheld until near the end (Blackman, Hoyos).
- Kang et al.'s inverted-U suggests the hook should reference something the viewer partly knows (EMI, FD, credit card, SIP) and then assert a counter-intuitive gap — not a topic they know nothing about, and not one they think they fully understand.
- Gruber 2014 implies incidental facts placed *during* the open loop (between hook and payoff) are better remembered — a good place for the educational core.
- Green & Brock supports framing explainers as a character story (a named protagonist) rather than a list of facts when the goal is belief/behavior change.

### Gaps
- No academic study found that directly tests open-loop hooks on short-form video retention; application is inferred from curiosity/narrative research.
- The Parker/Stone lecture is widely quoted but primary transcript is via secondary sites; the original mtvU video is linked.

## 2. Where do Shorts retention graphs typically drop, and what fixes are evidenced?

### Takeaway
The only large disclosed dataset (Paddy Galloway, 3.3B views, 2023) shows the hook-level metric "viewed vs. swiped away" (VVSA) and average view duration drive distribution: top Shorts had 70-90% VVSA; below 60% "typically did not perform well." Everything more granular (drop at 1-3 s = hook, drop at ~15 s = content didn't deliver, drop at end = payoff delivered) comes from creator/vendor guides without disclosed data.

### Cited Findings
- [Data study] Paddy Galloway & analyst Chris Gileta, published Apr 2023: 5,400 Shorts across 33 channels, 3.3B views. VVSA of top performers 70-90%; ≥60% as minimum threshold, below 60% "typically did not perform well." Shorts with AVD >50 s averaged 4.1M views vs 1.8M for AVD 40-50 s. Most creators made 20-40 s Shorts, but 50-60 s Shorts performed best. Average Shorts RPM ~$0.06/1,000 views; no meaningful RPM difference by length. Advice: treat the first second like a thumbnail. — [BAM summary](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315); [length distribution tweet](https://twitter.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898359808974851)
- [Official] YouTube product lead Todd Sherman / Jenny Hoyos: one second to hook. — [YouTube Blog, Jan 2025](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- [Anecdotal/creator — vendor blog] VVSA is found in YouTube Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts; vendors recommend aiming for 70-80%+. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics/); [ReelRise](https://reelrise.app/guide/viewed-vs-swiped-away-the-only-youtube-shorts-metric-that-matters/)
- [Anecdotal/creator — vendor blog] Diagnostic heuristics: sharp drop in first 1-3 s = hook/first frame problem; holding past 5 s but dropping at ~15 s = hook worked but content didn't deliver; gradual decline = pacing drift. Intro retention (past 3 s) "should ideally be above 70%." — [TubeAnalytics](https://www.tubeanalytics.net/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-guide); [SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-complete-guide); [AdShortsAI](https://adshortsai.com/en/low-retention-on-youtube-shorts/)
- [Anecdotal/creator — vendor blog] Three curve shapes: "cliff" (30-50% drop in first 3 s), "hump" (>60% through middle), "plateau" (flat >70% with loop-friendly end). No dataset disclosed. — [Aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)
- [Anecdotal/creator] End-of-video drop happens "when viewers have seen the main payoff and don't have anything they need to stick around for"; in 45-60 s videos few reach an end-placed subscribe CTA. — [George Blackman Retention Review #2](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core); [Retention Review #4](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-4-second-payoff)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Mid-video fix: visible progress markers ("3 steps," timers, attempt counters) because "the viewer can always see how close they are to the payoff"; no "separation" before the hook ("I'm not going to waste your time"). — [KDCC / Hoyos](https://blog.kdcc.social/make-anything-go-viral-how-to-hook-and-reel-in-your-viewer/)
- [Official — press report] Since Mar 31, 2025 a public Shorts "view" counts every start or replay with no minimum watch time; the stricter "engaged views" metric remains in Analytics and is used for YPP/revenue. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Sprout Social support note](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/35874991211533-YouTube-Shorts-View-Count-Update-March-2025)

### Inferences
- For a 2D-animated explainer, the first frame must already show the stakes visually (the "thumbnail" rule) — no logo sting or title card.
- Mid-video lull fix for explainers: re-open a second loop before closing the first ("but here's the part the bank doesn't tell you") and use on-screen step counters.
- Because post-2025 public view counts include replays, VVSA and engaged views/AVD are the better metrics for judging scripts.

### Gaps
- Galloway's dataset is from early 2023 (pre-view-count change); no newer disclosed-dataset study found. A claim of a "2026 Galloway analysis of 500 channels (2.8x subscriber growth)" appears only on a vendor blog ([marketmakermgmt](https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-your-channel)) and could not be verified — do not use.
- Could not retrieve Reddit threads (r/NewTubers etc.) with actual retention-graph screenshots in this pass; no Film Booth / Colin and Samir / vidIQ Shorts-specific retention data was verified.
- No evidence specific to animated or finance Shorts.

## 3. Script length/word counts; stating the payoff early vs late; numbers, "you," stakes

### Takeaway
Open-source AI Shorts pipelines encode ~120-140 words for a <50 s spoken Short; Galloway's data favors 50-60 s runtimes with high AVD. Creator advice consistently says withhold the payoff to the end but foreshadow it early (promise the number/result, reveal the specifics late). No controlled evidence was found on "you" address or numeric specificity in Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- [Anecdotal/creator — open-source prompt] ShortGPT facts prompt: "less than 50 seconds verbally (around 140 words maximum)." — [ShortGPT facts_generator.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/facts_generator.yaml)
- [Anecdotal/creator — open-source prompt] ShortGPT Reddit-story prompt: "The story must be between 120 and 140 words MAXIMUM"; narrated "as if you're a friend of the viewer." — [ShortGPT reddit_generate_script.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/reddit_generate_script.yaml)
- [Data study] 50-60 s Shorts performed best; AVD >50 s → 4.1M avg views (2023 dataset). — [BAM summary of Galloway](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Hoyos's videos typically run ~34 s. — [Medium write-up](https://medium.com/@10xquality/600m-viral-youtube-shorts-views-in-only-1-year-jenny-cracked-the-code-bcb43708f298) (secondary)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Payoff timing: Blackman's formula reveals "the specific payoff only at the end"; Hoyos foreshadows the payoff up front and uses progress markers toward it. — [Write With AI](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/write-a-killer-youtube-script-like); [Jay Clouse](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jayclouse_jenny-hoyos-gave-me-her-exact-script-that-activity-7262878750684459010-NIZu)
- [Academic] Curiosity is highest with partial knowledge (Kang 2009) — supports teasing the *existence* of a number/result early while hiding its value. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x)

### Inferences
- Applying the ~140 words/<50 s ratio, a 45-60 s English voiceover is roughly 130-170 words; Hinglish delivery speed varies by narrator, so time the VO rather than rely on word count.
- "Promise early, deliver late": state the stakes in the hook ("this one line in your loan agreement costs you lakhs") and reveal the exact figure/mechanism in the final seconds.
- The ShortGPT "friend telling a story" instruction is effectively a direct-address rule.

### Gaps
- No source found giving Hindi/Hinglish words-per-minute for VO.
- No controlled study found on the effect of second-person address, specific numbers, or explicit stakes on Shorts retention; recommendations here are creator consensus only.

## 4. Endings: loops, CTA placement, whether "subscribe" CTAs hurt, ending right after the payoff

### Takeaway
Creator consensus: end abruptly right after the payoff, ideally with a last line that flows into the first line (loop), and put CTAs in the caption rather than a spoken "subscribe" outro. Evidence is anecdotal; no controlled test of subscribe CTAs was found.

### Cited Findings
- [Anecdotal/creator] Hoyos writes the last line first and ends abruptly. — [Medium](https://medium.com/@10xquality/600m-viral-youtube-shorts-views-in-only-1-year-jenny-cracked-the-code-bcb43708f298); related Short: [“Why your Short's ending is destroying your retention”](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/95JNCi489gA)
- [Anecdotal/creator] ShortGPT story prompt: "DO NOT end the story with a moral conclusion or any sort of conclusion that elongates the personal story. Just stop it when it makes sense." — [ShortGPT reddit_generate_script.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/reddit_generate_script.yaml)
- [Anecdotal/creator] End drop occurs once the payoff has landed and nothing remains to stay for; end-placed subscribe CTAs in 45-60 s videos reach few viewers. — [George Blackman RR#2](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core)
- [Anecdotal/creator — vendor blog] A 3-second "follow for more" at the end of a 20 s Short is 15% of runtime and "breaks the loop"; put CTAs in the caption. Loop endings that feed back into the opening encourage replays (retention >100%). No dataset disclosed. — [PrePublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention); [Virvid looping](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026); [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- [Official — press report] Replays now count as public Shorts views (from Mar 31, 2025), while engaged views stay separate. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)

### Inferences
- Loop pattern for a finance parable: end on a half-sentence that completes with the opening line (e.g., last line "…and that's exactly why" → first line "the rich never pay full price for anything").
- Since replays count as views, loop endings raise public view counts; judge them on engaged views and AVD rather than raw views.

### Gaps
- No A/B or controlled data found on spoken subscribe CTAs vs none in Shorts; the "CTAs hurt" claim is creator/vendor opinion.
- No official YouTube statement found that loops/rewatches are a ranking signal.

## 5. Language/localization: Hinglish/Hindi vs English for Indian audiences on Shorts

### Takeaway
Official figures confirm India is a very large Shorts market (650M+ monthly logged-in Shorts viewers, June 2025; Shorts "first launched in India"; >1 trillion views there by Aug 2024) and that translated/multilingual content is widely consumed. Academic Twitter data show Hinglish is the preferred register among Hindi-speaking users. No source directly compares Hinglish vs English Shorts retention.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Brandcast 2025 (India): YouTube Shorts has "over 650 million monthly logged-in viewers" in India (June 2025). — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/brandcast-2025-ctv-shorts-and-youtube-as-indias-new-tv/)
- [Official] Neal Mohan, Brandcast India Aug 2024: "Shorts, which we first launched in India, now has trillions of views here"; Tubefilter reports >1 trillion views in India and 11,000 Indian channels with ≥1M subscribers. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/08/india-youtube-shorts-one-trillion-views/)
- [Official] YouTube India 2025 trends: "77% of Gen Z in India watched content or creators that have been translated from another language"; "68% of Gen Z in India use language or slang they've picked up from videos or memes"; MrBeast gained "47 Million+ subscribers from India" using seven audio tracks. — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/2025s-biggest-trends-on-youtube-that-gave-india-a-new-shared-vernacular/)
- [Academic] Twitter-based study (*Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 2024): proportion of users preferring Hinglish rose from 44.9% (2014) to 56.3% after 2020 (~1.2%/yr); users preferring monolingual English fell from 23.3% to 11.2%; Hinglish is the most popular choice, Hindi second. — [Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03058-6)
- [Anecdotal/industry report — unverified] Mindshare-e4m report: "93% YouTube viewers watch Indian language content"; Indic preference higher in non-metros (53%) than metros (39%). Page returned 403; figures from search snippet only. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/93-youtube-viewers-watch-indian-language-content-mindshare-e4m-content-marketing-report-134060.html)

### Inferences
- Hinglish narration matches the register most Hindi-speaking internet users prefer (Twitter proxy), and offers broader reach than pure English in India; English captions/on-screen numerals can serve both audiences.
- The 77% translated-content stat suggests dubbed/multi-audio versions are a viable reach extension.

### Gaps
- Figures like "India contributes 30% of Shorts views" and "regional content >55% of consumption" appeared only on aggregator/vendor sites with no primary source — excluded.

## 6. GitHub/AI-pipeline prompt templates for short scripts: what structure they encode

### Takeaway
Popular open-source pipelines encode minimal structure: hook first, no intro/"welcome," 120-140 words, casual first-person/friend tone, no moral/outro. None encode open loops, but/therefore, payoff timing, or loop endings, so those need to be added to prompts for this project.

### Cited Findings
- [Anecdotal/creator — open source] MoneyPrinterTurbo (harry0703) `DEFAULT_SCRIPT_SYSTEM_PROMPT` rules: "get straight to the point, don't start with unnecessary things like, 'welcome to this video'"; no markdown/titles; no "voiceover"/"narrator" labels; respond in the subject's language; paragraph count set by user. No hook/payoff/loop structure. — [llm.py](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo/blob/main/app/services/llm.py)
- [Anecdotal/creator — open source] ShortGPT facts template: one short hook line (e.g., "Weird facts you don't know.") then a run of facts; <50 s / ~140 words. — [facts_generator.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/facts_generator.yaml)
- [Anecdotal/creator — open source] ShortGPT Reddit-story template: 120-140 words; start with "I"; casual/"youthful" language; "narrated as if you're a friend of the viewer"; "very SPICY, very unusual"; must directly answer the title; no moral conclusion — "just stop it when it makes sense." — [reddit_generate_script.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/reddit_generate_script.yaml)
- [Anecdotal/creator] Extended MoneyPrinterTurbo forks use longer "expert YouTube scriptwriter" prompts aimed at 5-minute videos, not Shorts. — [MoneyPrinterTurbo-Extended](https://github.com/thangckt/MoneyPrinterTurbo-Extended)

### Inferences
- A project prompt should add: (1) a ≤1-2 s question-form hook with a salient gap, (2) a foreshadow line, (3) 3-5 beats joined by "but/therefore," (4) payoff in the last ~5 s, (5) a last line that loops into the first, (6) no spoken subscribe CTA, (7) a word budget timed to VO speed (~140 words per 50 s in English per ShortGPT).

### Gaps
- Did not audit ShortGPT's full repository (GitHub API access blocked in this session); only the two templates above were read directly.
- No open-source template found for Hinglish finance explainers specifically.
