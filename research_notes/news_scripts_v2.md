# Rewritten scripts, version 2 (5 Oct 2026)

Owner's feedback: sentences were fragments, joined badly, too few full stops, not clearly explained. Rules now:
- Every line is one complete, grammatical sentence. No fragments ("Over four months." on its own is not allowed).
- Explain one step per sentence, in order: what happened, why it matters to you, why it happened, what is being
  done, what it means, the catch. No jumps.
- Longer pauses at every full stop (pacing set in motion/storykit.py: news_pacing()).
- Global coverage, US as the main audience: lead with the American angle when there is one, but cover world stories too.

## 1. Diesel (US NEWS)
1. Diesel fuel just hit its highest price ever in the United States.
2. On September twenty-second, the average gallon cost six dollars and fifty-two cents.
3. That matters even if you never buy diesel.
4. The trucks that bring food and goods to your stores run on diesel.
5. So when diesel costs more, almost everything in the store can cost more too.
6. Why did the price jump so much?
7. The war with Iran has stopped most oil tankers from passing through the Strait of Hormuz.
8. That narrow sea route normally carries about one fifth of the world's oil.
9. With less oil getting through, diesel became scarce all around the world.
10. On October second, the G7, a group of seven of the world's biggest economies, agreed on a plan.
11. Together, they will release up to one hundred million barrels of oil and diesel from their emergency reserves.
12. The release will take four months, and most of the diesel will come in the first twenty days.
13. Energy experts say diesel could fall by about twenty-five cents a gallon within a few weeks.
14. But there is a catch.
15. Emergency reserves are a short-term fix, and they will have to be refilled later.
16. So prices may drop soon, but they may not stay low for long.
17. Have you noticed higher prices at the store? Tell me in the comments.

## 2. Jobs (US NEWS)
1. Economists expected America to add about ninety thousand jobs in September.
2. The official report showed only twenty-nine thousand.
3. The Bureau of Labor Statistics released those numbers on October second.
4. The unemployment rate also rose, from four point one to four point two percent.
5. The government also lowered its numbers for July and August, by sixty thousand jobs in total.
6. Pay is growing slowly too.
7. Average hourly pay rose three percent over the past year, the slowest rise since twenty twenty-one.
8. So where are the new jobs?
9. Most of them were in health care, with a few more in construction and in factories.
10. Economists call this a low hire, low fire job market.
11. Companies are not laying many people off, but they are not hiring many people either.
12. That is tough news for anyone who is looking for work.
13. Is it harder to find a job where you live? Tell me in the comments.

## 3. Tesla Cybercab (TECH)
1. In Austin, Texas, you can now pay for a taxi ride with no driver.
2. The car is Tesla's Cybercab, and it has no steering wheel and no pedals.
3. Paid rides began in September, so the first month is now over.
4. It was a bumpy start.
5. Passengers complained about long waits and about cars picking them up in the wrong spots.
6. Some also had problems with the doors and the trunk.
7. So far, no major crashes have been reported.
8. But federal safety regulators have opened a review of the car.
9. Austin firefighters have also asked for clear rules on how to handle a car with no driver controls.
10. Even so, Tesla is not slowing down.
11. The number of Cybercabs registered in Texas has grown from forty-five to one hundred sixty-nine.
12. Elon Musk says the company is being extremely careful with safety.
13. Would you ride in a car with no steering wheel? Tell me in the comments.

## 4. Apple (TECH)
1. If you use a Mac, an important setting is about to change.
2. On October second, Apple warned app makers about a setting called Full Disk Access.
3. When you turn it on, an app can see almost everything on your Mac.
4. That includes your files, your emails, your messages and even your browsing history.
5. Many new AI helpers ask for this access, so they can work for you in the background.
6. Apple says this could expose everything on your computer without your full knowledge and understanding.
7. So Apple plans to make this setting harder to turn on.
8. You will need to take a very clear and deliberate step before an app gets that access.
9. Apple has not said when this change will arrive.
10. Would you let an AI read everything on your computer? Tell me in the comments.

## 5. Plague (WORLD)
1. A laboratory worker in Siberia has died, and doctors suspect the plague.
2. The plague is caused by the same germ that caused the Black Death in the fourteenth century.
3. The worker was a twenty-eight-year-old technician at an institute in Irkutsk, Russia, that studies the plague.
4. She went to hospital on September twenty-ninth and died a few days later.
5. Russian officials say the situation is stable.
6. Still, one hundred ninety-seven people who may have had contact with her were isolated.
7. Several hospitals also went into a three-week quarantine.
8. The plague has not been confirmed.
9. Russia's health agency says she died of pneumonia with an unknown cause.
10. The US State Department says it is monitoring the situation closely.
11. Here is something many Americans do not know.
12. The plague still exists in the United States, with about seven human cases a year, mostly in the West.
13. The good news is that it can be treated with antibiotics when it is caught early.
14. Did you know the plague still exists in America? Tell me in the comments.

## 6. Japan (WORLD)
1. If you dream of moving to Japan for good, it just got a lot more expensive.
2. On October first, Japan raised the fee for permanent residency twenty times over.
3. The fee used to be ten thousand yen, which is about sixty-three dollars.
4. Now it is two hundred thousand yen, which is about twelve hundred and seventy dollars.
5. Permanent residency lets a foreigner stay in Japan with no time limit.
6. But it does not make them a citizen, and it does not come with a Japanese passport.
7. Japan now has more than four million foreign residents, which is a record.
8. The government says the new fee covers the real cost of handling applications.
9. It also says the fee is now closer to what other countries charge.
10. Applicants now also need a higher income to qualify.
11. Would you pay twelve hundred dollars to live in Japan? Tell me in the comments.
