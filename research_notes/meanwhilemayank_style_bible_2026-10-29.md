# Meanwhile Mayank: how the reels are made (frame-by-frame study of all 12, 29 Oct 2026)

**Method:** every second of all 12 reels was viewed at one frame per second, 1,800+ frames in all. Openings,
transitions and moving parts were also checked at 6 fps, and key frames at full 1080x1920 resolution. Sound was
measured (loudness, spectrogram, transients), and every post's caption and description was searched for credits.

The per-reel shot logs stay on the owner's laptop (`shorts research\mayank\study\*.md`), next to the private
downloads. **Observed** means seen in the frames; *inference* is marked as such.

## 1. Stock images or animation? Both: real images composited and animated in 2D
- **No live-action footage**, except one 1-second shot (a hand smearing gobar, radiation reel, 137-138 s). All 12
  reels are made of still images moved in 2D: slides, drops with a bounce, scale pops, rotations, page tear-offs,
  parallax layers, zooms and camera pans across a layout.
- **Many images are real open-licence or archive images, credited in the YouTube descriptions** (observed, quoted):
  - "Imagery: NASA (Blue Marble, Black Marble, LRO Moon, SDO Sun…)"
  - "Images: Gray's Anatomy (1918, public domain)"
  - a Wikimedia Commons photo credited by photographer
  - "Photo: PIB, Government of India (GODL-India)"
  - "Kalam photo: Govt of India (GODL-India)"
  
  On-screen footnotes add "ELECTRON MICROGRAPH · NIAID · CC BY 2.0 (MODIFIED)", "RABIES PATIENT, 1959 · CDC",
  "EDWARD JENNER · WELLCOME COLLECTION · CC BY 4.0" and "ORTELIUS, 1570 · UTA LIBRARIES".
- **Everyday objects carry no credit:** dogs, hands, taps, turmeric, stopwatches, mannequins. That fits free stock
  (Pexels/Unsplash/Pixabay don't require credit) or generated images. Which one could not be proven: a search of
  Pixabay, Unsplash and Commons did not find his exact dog. Similar free stock shots exist.
- **Compositing is visible:**
  - 16 identical syringes copied and rotated in a ring round a mannequin
  - flat vector water, soap bubbles and a line-drawn glass placed over real photos of a tap, an arm and a hand
  - vial labels set in his own headline font ("Rx / RABIES / VACCINE")
- **One printed texture over every layer** (observed at 1:1): a regular halftone dot screen on photos, speckled paper
  grain, faint ruled lines, a vertical crease, and a column of binder-hole dots down the left edge. This is what
  makes mixed sources read as one poster.
- No AI disclosure is shown on his YouTube Short, and no tool is named in any caption.

## 3. The look (observed)
- **Backgrounds** follow the subject:
  - beige creased poster paper for facts, objects and data
  - dark slate or charcoal for inside the body: x-ray diagrams, a circular microscope view, anatomy engravings
  - sky-blue cut-paper dioramas or flat Indian city scenes for streets and everyday life
  - space black for space
- **Headline system:** a small dark line, then a big red line on the key word. The small line appears first, often
  with the red line as a grey "ghost" that turns red when spoken. Sometimes the red line stands alone ("THE BITE").
  On dark grounds the red goes coral or orange. A typewriter-style mono font is used for labels, tags and footnotes.
- **Header strip:** a thin rule with a small mono label on the left and "FIG. n" on the right. The label may be a
  section name (rabies, tardigrade), a template tag (wasp, fungus), or absent.
- **Asset families mixed in one reel:**
  - photo-real cut-out objects with soft shadows
  - vintage engravings (Gray's Anatomy) and old charts
  - flat mid-century textbook illustrations (cells, virions, antibodies)
  - cut-paper dioramas
  - real archive photos and micrographs with credits
  - data graphics
- **Data devices:**
  - flip counters with Indian digit grouping (56,00,000), sometimes inside the headline
  - icon arrays (10x10; 1 in 5; 1 in 10)
  - a stopwatch with a red wedge
  - tear-off calendars flipping fast (? → 1 WEEK → 1 MONTH → 3 MONTHS → 6 MONTHS → 1 YEAR)
  - progress boxes, thermometer, ruler or scale bar, gauges, luggage tags, YES/NO cards
- **Metaphor objects:**
  - WANTED poster (immune training), castle walls (blood-brain barrier), bullet (virus shape)
  - tenant with TO LET / OCCUPIED signs (chickenpox)
  - marionette strings (fungus), dhol (dried tardigrade), pause button (no metabolism)
  - golden rope snapping (gravity gone), quilt on ice (ice is a blanket)
  - stamps: HIJACKED, MYTH, NOT A MOVIE, IMPOSSIBLE, MADE IN INDIA
- **Every picture is the literal noun or number of its sentence.** Recurring base figures (brain + spine, forearm
  engraving, mannequin, the hero animal) come back in a new state every time. Nothing returns unchanged.

## 4. Motion (observed at 6 fps)
- **Arrivals are physical:** the vial drops in from the top with a bounce by 0.33 s; the next scene knocks the old
  object over (the vial tips as the dog bursts through torn paper); cards and stamps thump in; tags type in.
- **Text** fades in with opacity, never slides. The red word lands about 0.2 s after its cue.
- **Marks are drawn on as strokes:** ticks, circles, strikes, X's.
- **Counters** spin with blurred digits and ease out on the final value.
- **Dioramas never stand still:** vehicles at different speeds, birds, walking dogs.
- **Magnifier circles** zoom from a base figure to the micro level and back.

## 5. Sound (measured on the rabies reel)
- **Loudness:** −14.2 LUFS integrated with a loudness range of only 2.4 LU, so it is heavily levelled.
- **The bed never stops.** It sits ~17 dB under the voice in pauses: sustained bass notes (64-128 Hz) plus pad tones.
- **The bed dips briefly before section changes or reveals** (8.5 s, 18.5 s, 25.8 s).
- **Sharp hits land on visual actions:** the vial landing 0.1 s, TOO LATE 19.6 s, the HIJACKED stamp ~64 s, the red X
  163 s, the dog bite 170.6 s.

## 6. Openings and endings (observed)
- **The hits put a contradiction or threat on screen by ~2-3 s:**
  - "RABIES HAS A VACCINE ✓" → "YET IT KILLS"
  - "AN INSECT" → "IT WON'T ~~KILL~~"
  - "YOUR LEGS ARE WALKING" → "NOT YOU."
  - "HAD CHICKENPOX?"
  - "EVER WONDERED WHY SOME PALMS… FOR NO REASON"
- **The weak three don't:** STRESS on a busy street; a dim phone with blank grey notification bars and no number
  until ~6 s; a "2001 / $33 A DAY" price card.
- **Endings are one of three kinds,** and none has a logo or outro:
  - a practical action (rabies: wash 15 min, vaccine today, TODAY circled)
  - a comment card with ☐YES ☐NO or a typed "YES, ME TOO" (chickenpox, palms, radiation, stress)
  - an awe line (wasp "HOW STRANGE", fungus "IT CAN ASTONISH YOU", sun "ALONE")

## 7. What our channel takes from this
1. **One new specific picture per sentence** (every ~3 s), with something changing every 1-2 s. A base figure may
   return only in a new state.
2. **Real images first.** Archives credited like his (NASA, Gray's Anatomy, CDC/NIH, Commons, GODL-India), plus free
   stock (Pixabay, Unsplash, Pexels) for everyday objects, all cut out. Designed labels go on top in our own fonts;
   flat drawn overlays add water, marks, cells and arrows.
3. **One printed texture over the finished frame:** halftone dots, paper grain, ruled lines, crease, binder holes.
4. **Section backgrounds:** paper, dark slate, Indian diorama.
5. **Physical motion vocabulary:** drop + bounce, knock-over hand-offs, stamps, drawn marks, spinning counters,
   tear-off calendars, magnifier zooms, living dioramas.
6. **A continuous music bed** with brief dips before reveals and hits on actions, at −14 LUFS.
7. **Credits** in the description, plus footnotes on screen for archive or science images and data sources.
