## Script structure (the same skeleton in all 9)

1. **Hook, 0-15 s: an impossible-sounding claim in "you" form.** "Imagine...", "There's a virus living in you and
   you don't know", "Have you ever had chickenpox? If yes, it's still in your body", "Rabies has a vaccine. Yet it
   kills 100 out of 100." Three short punches: the claim, the twist, the stakes ("by the time you know, it's too late").
2. **Proof it's real + one big number** (deaths, crore/lakh counts, prices), often local (India).
3. **Name it** ("Its name is...") plus a friendly nickname or pop-culture hook (water bear; The Last of Us).
4. **The turn:** "So first, let's understand..." / "Now the question is, how...?"
5. **Mechanism as a story, one step per sentence**, carried by ONE metaphor: body = country, immune system = army,
   CD4 = commanders, infected cell = virus factory; stress = an alarm that forgot to switch off; the fungus = a
   puppeteer; a dried tardigrade = life on pause; rabies hijacks the nerve's transport system like a highway.
6. **A second "wow" fact with a study behind it** (2017 3D scans of ants; 394 volunteers given a cold virus;
   tardigrades sent to space in 2007; radioactive tracking of the wasp's sting).
7. **Myth-busting / what people get wrong** (HIV doesn't spread by hugs; chilli and turmeric on a bite; "14 injections
   in the stomach" is the old vaccine; "it's the goddess", so no doctor; "bathing is fine, never aspirin for kids").
8. **Hope or a clear action** (one pill a day; wash a bite for 15 min and get the vaccine the same day; meditation).
9. **Close:** a big world number, a thought ("Nature is not only beautiful..."), or a comment question
   ("Did you get this vaccine? Tell us in the comments").

Sentences are short; many are fragments ("One day off." "Twice its size."). Numbers are always concrete.

## Look

- **Paper, always:** aged cream/khaki paper with vertical fold creases, grain, and a column of punch-hole dots at the
  left edge, like a page from an old notebook or file. Section palettes swap the paper colour: khaki, mint-green,
  deep teal-green (cells), dark charcoal-brown (anatomy, microscope, world), pink-red (blood), blue-patterned cloth.
- **Header on every frame:** a thin rule near the top with a tiny monospaced section label on the left ("HOOK",
  "THE BITE", "THE BRAIN", "INDIA") and "FIG. N" on the right, like a figure in a textbook.
- **Headline, top-left:** a small dark condensed line + a big RED condensed line, 2-4 words each ("YET IT / KILLS",
  "BY THE TIME / YOU KNOW...", "TOO / HEAVY"). It changes on every phrase. The words that aren't spoken yet sit faded
  and fill in as they're said. **There are no subtitles:** the headline is the only text tied to the voice.
- **Images:** mostly photo-real cut-out objects with soft shadows on the paper (vaccine vials, a stopwatch, a mercury
  thermometer, a wooden artist's mannequin as "you", an angry dog bursting through torn paper, insects), plus
  - old anatomy engravings in white on a dark board ("GRAY'S ANATOMY, 1918": spinal cord, head section, body
    outline with dermatomes), with one red glowing element on them;
  - circular microscope or magnifier views (cells, viruses as red spiky discs, bacteria);
  - flat paper-craft scenes (cotton clouds, Indian streets with shop signs, autos and buses, a house with a "TO LET"
    sign for the virus, a window with a calendar showing "DAY 14");
  - paper props: flip counters on brass plates (-273 °C, 0,000 deaths, 30,213 crore), torn paper labels, red rubber
    stamps ("ZOMBIE", "ALMOST IMPOSSIBLE", "NO PROTECTION"), a checklist card ("KILL IT? / CARRY IT?"), a cinema
    ticket ("HORROR / NOT A MOVIE"), a health-report card with NORMAL stamps, bar charts, price tags, Polaroid photos
    on a string with green ticks.
- **Motion:** gentle push-ins, objects sliding or popping in, counters rolling, stamps slamming, red lines and arrows
  drawing on, a stopwatch's red sector growing. The background scene changes every 4-9 s; something (headline, prop,
  label) changes every 1-2 s.
- **Sound:** one calm, warm narrator, no character voices, a soft music bed, small foley (stamp, tick, counter).

## Why it works

- The first sentence is a mystery about YOU, so the viewer can't scroll away without the answer.
- One metaphor turns hard biology into a story a child can follow, and every step has a picture.
- Concrete numbers on physical counters feel like evidence; "FIG. N" and the old-textbook look feel trustworthy.
- Local stakes (India's numbers, local myths) make it personal; myth-busting gives viewers something to share.
- It ends useful (what to do) or with a question, which drives comments.

## How we'd do it (our own version, English, doctor channel)

- Same skeleton and pacing, our own topics and metaphors; never one of the 9 topics told the same way.
- Our own art: drawn in `motion.engine` (paper texture with creases, flat cut-out objects with shadows, headline +
  FIG header, flip counter, stamps, microscope circle) plus genuinely public-domain anatomy plates (Gray's Anatomy,
  1918) where they fit. No stock or AI images copied from anyone.
- Every number checked against WHO/CDC/NHS/peer-reviewed sources and cited in the upload sheet; no treatment
  advice beyond "see a doctor" and official first aid.
