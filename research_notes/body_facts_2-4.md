# Body Facts 2-4: research notes

Three Shorts: awake brain surgery (`videos/brain_awake.py`), the nasal cycle (`videos/nose_cycle.py`) and liver
regrowth (`videos/liver_regrow.py`). Every medical line in the scripts traces back to a source below. Where sources
disagree, the script uses the wording that all of them support ("every few hours", "a few months", "most people").

## How the topics were picked

What wins on Shorts (see `reports/Shorts retention for animated explainers.md` for the full evidence): the hook
decides the swipe, the payoff has to be held back, and the topic should open a gap in something the viewer *partly*
knows (Loewenstein 1994; Kang et al. 2009: curiosity peaks at moderate confidence). Organ-character Shorts that
regularly reach millions of views are organs reacting to everyday things, and organs arguing with each other
(aituber.app write-up of the talking-organs format; Organ-ized Health on YouTube).

Shortlist, scored on four questions: is it about the viewer's own body? does it sound impossible? can the viewer
test it right now? does it make a fight between organs?

| Topic | Own body | Sounds impossible | Testable now | Organ conflict | Picked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brain can't feel pain (awake surgery) | yes | **very** | no | brain vs scalpel | **yes** |
| Nasal cycle (one nostril at a time) | **yes** | yes | **yes** | two sides fight over shifts | **yes** |
| Liver grows back after donation | yes | yes | no | lobes split up; sequel to the kidney video | **yes** |
| Stomach lining renews every few days | yes | yes | no | weak | backup |
| Taller in the morning (discs compress) | yes | medium | sort of | weak | backup |
| Babies' kneecaps are cartilage | no | medium | no | weak | no |
| Funny bone is a nerve | yes | low | yes | weak | backup |

On "sensitive topics": the owner allowed any topic. These three were still kept advertiser-safe, because YouTube
limits ads on graphic or shocking medical content. That is revenue lost on a channel that's still new. No blood,
no open wounds: the scalpel only "boops" the brain.

| Technique | Where | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Talk to "you" and make it about the viewer's own body | nose: "Hey, you. Yes, you. | Impossible-sounding claim on frame 1 (headline at t=0) | "AWAKE brain surgery?!", "Only ONE nostril works?", "HALF a liver?!" | Key message in the first 3 s (TikTok ad research) **[Data, ads]** |
| Open loop: organs panic about something the viewer can't explain yet; the doctor closes it in the second half | all three | Curiosity is an information gap (Loewenstein 1994); payoff held to the end (Blackman) **[Data + Anecdotal]** |

## Facts and sources

### Brain (`brain_awake`)
- "There are no pain receptors in the brain itself." "Your scalp will be anesthetized, so you will not feel the
  operation or any pain." Patients count, read, or name objects in pictures during mapping; typically sedated at the
  beginning and end, awake for mapping. UCSF Brain Tumor Center, Awake Brain Mapping FAQ:
  https://braintumorcenter.ucsf.edu/about-brain-tumors/treatment/surgery/awake-brain-mapping-faq
- Awake craniotomy is used when a tumour is near speech, language or movement areas, so the team can find
  (map) those areas: St George's NHS leaflet, https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NEU_ACBT.pdf
- Headache pain: nociceptors in the meninges and their blood vessels (trigeminal afferents), not brain tissue.
  Levy et al. 2019, *Cephalalgia*, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0333102418771350 ; NINDS "Headache: Hope
  Through Research", https://www.ninds.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/headache-hope-through-research.pdf ; Live
  Science explainer, https://www.livescience.com/if-the-brain-doesnt-feel-pain-why-do-headaches-hurt
- Patients have played the violin and guitar during awake surgery (NPR 2020; CBS). Not used in the script, but it's
  a good pinned-comment reply.

Script wording: "The brain itself has no pain sensors." / "We numbed your scalp. After that, the brain felt
nothing." / "you stayed awake, so you could talk while we worked. That told us which parts to leave alone." /
"That pain comes from the layers and blood vessels around the brain."

### Nose (`nose_cycle`)
- 24-hour recordings: all 33 subjects cycled; mean cycle 2.15 h (awake 2.02 h, asleep 4.5 h), range 15 min to
  10.35 h. Engorgement of erectile tissue in the septum and inferior turbinate on one side; autonomic control
  (sympathetic on one side, parasympathetic on the other). Kahana-Zweig et al. 2016, PMC5053491:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5053491/
- Cleveland Clinic: one side congests and the other decongests, switching "every four to six hours"; it's normal:
  https://health.clevelandclinic.org/why-do-i-sometimes-get-stuffy-in-one-nostril
- Reviews put it at 70-80% of healthy adults (summarised in the Wikipedia "Nasal cycle" article and in ENT clinic
  explainers).

Script wording: "Most people have it." / "Tissue inside one side swells up, so most of the air goes through the
other side." / "Every few hours, your nervous system swaps the sides." The patient's line was changed from "always
blocked" to "keeps getting blocked", because a side that's blocked *all* the time is not the nasal cycle. The
description says to see a doctor in that case.

### Liver (`liver_regrow`)
- UPMC: donors give "anywhere from 25% to 65%" of the liver; "about eight to 10 weeks after the living-donor liver
  transplant, the liver will regrow to full size and function in both the donor and the recipient"; back to
  pre-donation health "in a few months":
  https://www.upmc.com/services/transplant/services/procedures/living-liver-donor-surgery
- Other centres: function normal in 2-4 weeks, at least 80% of original size in 2-3 months (Lahey; Columbia FAQ).
- The removed lobe does not come back: the remaining liver enlarges, so the mass is restored but not the original
  shape (Wikipedia "Liver regeneration"; ScienceDirect topic overview).

Script wording: "Your liver grows back." / "In a few months, it's almost full size again. Just a different shape."
/ "And the piece your brother got grows too." / "Pretty much, yes." (both end up with a working, near-full-size
liver). The script deliberately doesn't say "the only organ that regrows" (that claim is disputed and imprecise), and it
doesn't answer the lungs joke.

## Voices restyled on the owner's reference (second pass)
The owner sent a reference Short (a cracked kneecap, wires and a doctor) and asked for organ voices "like this,
don't copy". Measured with resemblyzer speaker embeddings and pYIN pitch on the reference's character lines:
- Two character voices. The kneecap (the one things happen to) sounds like a young male voice, very expressive
  (pitch SD 7-8.5 semitones), ~4.6-5 syllables/s. The tools (helpers) are brighter and higher, ~3.9 syllables/s.
- 28 Kokoro voices x pitch 0..+9 x formant shifted/preserved were synthesized and compared. Closest to the kneecap:
  `bm_george` +2 (0.71), `bm_daniel` +2 (0.69). Closest to the tools: `bf_alice` +4 (0.80), `af_jessica` +4 (0.79),
  `af_river` +4 (0.78). The old +5..+7 cast was more cartoonish than the reference.
- These are different stock voices with a similar style, not a copy of the reference's voices.

## Third pass: the owner's two new reference Shorts (house style)
Measured from the files the owner sent (frames at 1 fps, Whisper medium transcripts, pYIN pitch, resemblyzer):
- **Reference A** (57 s, esophageal varices): the esophagus with googly eyes and swollen purple veins bursts and
  bleeds bright red; the stomach (deeper voice) drinks the blood thinking it's wine; an endoscope character bands
  the veins. Then the doctor (masked) names the condition and the procedure ("esophageal varices", "endoscopic band
  ligation") in one calm 24 s explanation. Organ part ~32 s, doctor part ~24 s.
- **Reference B** (57 s, fetal growth restriction): the fetus is too small, a scale shows it, a gloved hand with
  eyes takes the baby out to a NICU incubator, then the doctor explains to the father.
- **Look:** clean flat vector, thin even outlines, realistic medical colours, googly eyes straight on the organ,
  white anatomy tags ("Esophagus", "VAIN"), bright red blood, tools as characters. One word of caption at a time,
  uppercase, white with a dark outline, ~75% down the frame. Doctor's room: plain wall, masked doctor, patient in a
  blue shirt in bed, a static medium shot. A diagonal channel watermark.
- **Voices:** doctor ~120 Hz at 4.7-5.0 syllables/s (calm, slower than our clone's 5.5). Organs: a scared high
  voice (290-480 Hz), a grumpy natural one (~150 Hz; `am_eric` matches it at 0.89, so the references very likely
  use the same stock voices we do), tools/helpers mid-high.
- **What we changed:** clean style, one-word captions, anatomy tags, masked-doctor room, doctor at 4.9 syl/s, the
  doctor naming the medical term ("awake craniotomy", "living donor liver transplant", "nasal cycle"), organ voices
  re-cast (scared `af_heart`/`af_jessica` +6, grumpy `am_eric`, tool `af_nova` +2, helper `af_river` +4). Every
  character line re-checked with Whisper medium; four were reworded ("his"→"this", "A break?"→"Unbreak",
  "Shift change!"→"15", "which half"→"what have").
