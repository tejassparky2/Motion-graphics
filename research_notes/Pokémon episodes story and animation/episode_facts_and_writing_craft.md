# Pokémon anime: facts for three Sun & Moon episodes, and how the episodes are written (researched 30 Oct 2026)

Access notes: Bulbapedia, the Japanese Pokémon Wiki (wiki.pokemonwiki.com), Dogasu's Backpack (dogasu.bulbagarden.net) and the Anime News Network feature returned HTTP 403 or would not resolve from this session, so they were not opened. Where a fact rests only on a search-engine excerpt of one of those pages, it says so. The main sources actually opened were Wikidex, PocketMonsters.net episode and staff pages, English and Japanese Wikipedia, anime.eiga.com, the Nerima interview, Looper and GoNintendo. No episode video copies were looked up or linked.

## Q1. How the owner's "S22E14 / S22E22 / S20E43" map to SM codes

### Takeaway
International season 20 is *Sun & Moon* (43 episodes, SM001–SM043) and season 22 is *Sun & Moon – Ultra Legends* (54 episodes, the last 54 of 146). So S20E43 = **SM043**, S22E14 = **SM106** and S22E22 = **SM114**. Wikidex and PocketMonsters.net confirm all three SM codes by title and air date.

### Cited Findings
- Season 20 is "Twentieth season of the *Pokémon* animated television series", with 43 episodes. Its episode 43 is "When Regions Collide!" (JP air 21 Sep 2017, US 9 Dec 2017). — [Wikipedia: Sun & Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon)
- Season 22 (*Ultra Legends*) runs season episodes 1–54. Its episode 14 is "Evolving Research!" (JP 27 Jan 2019, US 1 Jun 2019) and its episode 22 is "Beauty is Only Crystal Deep!" (JP 24 Mar 2019, US 27 Jul 2019). — [Wikipedia: Ultra Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon_%E2%80%93_Ultra_Legends)
- The Japanese Sun & Moon series has 146 episodes in total (「全146話」). — [Japanese Wikipedia: ポケットモンスター サン&ムーン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3)
- Wikidex: "Evolving Research!" is the 14th episode of the 22nd season, linked as SM106. — [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049)
- Wikidex: "Beauty is Only Crystal Deep!" is the 22nd episode of the 22nd season. Its own list counts it as 114 in the series. — [Wikidex EP1057](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1057)
- Wikidex: "When Regions Collide!" is episode 43 of the 20th season and the season finale. — [Wikidex EP986](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP986)
- PocketMonsters.net lists the same three episodes as "SM 106", "SM 114" and "SM 43", with the same Japanese titles and dates. — [PM.net SM106](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2286/Staff), [PM.net SM114](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2302/Staff), [PM.net SM43](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2142/Staff)
- **Sources number the episodes differently overall (these are not SM codes):**
  - English Wikipedia gives Japanese overall #1047 for S22E14 and #984 for S20E43, and English overall #1040 and #978. — [Wikipedia: Ultra Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon_%E2%80%93_Ultra_Legends), [Wikipedia: Sun & Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon)
  - Wikidex's page codes are EP1049, EP1057 and EP986. — [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049)
  - The TV listing site thetv.jp files SM106 under index "104". This looks like a site index, not an episode number. — [thetv.jp](https://thetv.jp/program/0000913720/104/)

### Inferences
- How the mapping works: SM codes run continuously across the three international seasons. Season 22 has 54 episodes and the series ends at SM146, so season 22 starts at SM093 (146 − 54 + 1). That gives S22E14 = SM093 + 13 = SM106 and S22E22 = SM093 + 21 = SM114. Season 20 starts at SM001, so S20E43 = SM043. Season 21 (*Ultra Adventures*) must then be SM044–SM092, which is 49 episodes. That count is derived from the numbers above, not read from a source.
- Use the SM code or the title when filing clips. The "overall" episode numbers differ by up to two between sources.

### Gaps
- The Netflix/Pokémon TV per-season numbering on pokemon.com was not checked. All the sources above agree, so no discrepancy is expected.

## Q2. S22E14 = SM106 "Evolving Research!"

### Takeaway
SM106 is a Poni Island research episode. In the A plot, Sophocles' Charjabug evolves into Vikavolt in Poni Canyon; in the B plot, Lana's Popplio evolves into Brionne. Hapu appears, which matches the owner's frame. The sources don't confirm a Pokémon School classroom scene.

### Cited Findings
- **English title:** "Evolving Research!" — [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049); [PM.net](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2286/Staff)
- **Japanese title:** 海あり谷あり！ポケモン進化大特訓！！ (*Umi ari tani ari! Pokémon shinka dai tokkun!!*)
  - Wikipedia translates it as "We Have a Sea and We Have a Valley! Great Intensive Pokémon Evolution Training!!" — [Wikipedia: Ultra Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon_%E2%80%93_Ultra_Legends)
  - PocketMonsters.net translates it as "A Roller Coaster Ride with Seas and Valleys! Pokémon Evolution Training!!" — [PM.net](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2286/Staff)
- **Air dates:** Japan 27 Jan 2019; South Korea 7 May 2019; United States 1 Jun 2019; Netherlands 4 Aug 2019. — [PM.net](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2286/Staff). Wikidex gives the same Japan and US dates, plus Spain 30 Jun 2019 and Latin America 23 Jul 2019. — [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049)
- **Staff:** PocketMonsters.net, Wikidex and the Japanese Wikipedia episode table agree on every credit below. — [PM.net staff](https://pocketmonsters.net/episodes/viewep/2286/Staff); [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049); [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3)

- **Official synopses:**

  - Japanese TV listing: 「デンヂムシをクワガノンに進化させたいマーマネは、不思議な力があると言われるポニ島の大峡谷に行くことに」 ("Sophocles, who wants to evolve Charjabug into Vikavolt, heads to Poni Island's great canyon, said to hold a mysterious power"). Seen in a search excerpt of the listing. — [thetv.jp](https://thetv.jp/program/0000913720/104/)
- **Plot, from Wikidex:**
  - On Poni Island, Hapu brings Poni radishes to share and confirms that Poni Canyon holds a mysterious power. Ash keeps asking her for a battle.
  - Sophocles, Ash, Kiawe and Hapu go into the canyon. They fight wild Golduck and Golbat, and meet the rival Horacio, who plans to evolve his Charjabug with an evolution stone.

  - In the parallel plot, Lana trains all day with Ida, whose Primarina performs a Z-Move.
  - At a night party, Lana is inspired. The next morning Popplio evolves into Brionne, and Ida gives Lana a Primarium Z.

### Inferences
- The owner's frame of "the class with Hapu" probably shows the group update scene during the Poni Island trip (for example at the Pokémon Center where they stay), not the Melemele Pokémon School. This is inferred, not confirmed.
- The episode is a clean A/B-plot example. A (Sophocles and Charjabug in the canyon) and B (Lana and Popplio training at sea) run in parallel and meet at the night party. Each pays off with an evolution. The title's 海 (sea) and 谷 (valley) name the two plots, and appear to pun on the idiom 山あり谷あり ("ups and downs"). The pun reading is inferred.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether any shot is in the actual Pokémon School classroom, and Hapu's "purple bonnet" description. No opened source describes either.
- Bulbapedia's SM106 page (403) could not be checked directly. A search excerpt said it agrees with the storyboard and director credits and also credits Akemi Omode with the screenplay.

## Q3. S22E22 = SM114 "Beauty is Only Crystal Deep!"

### Takeaway
SM114 is the Pheromosa episode. At night an Ultra Beast steals Hala's Z-Crystal, the Ultra Guardians (with Kiawe's Charizard) hunt it, Meowth falls for it, and Bewear defeats it. The white, slender Ultra Beast in the owner's frame is almost certainly **Pheromosa**, not Kartana, Blacephalon or Nihilego, which none of the opened sources mention.

## Q4. S20E43 = SM043 "When Regions Collide!"

### Takeaway
SM043 is the season 20 finale. On the Kanto trip, Professor Kukui sets up gym battles for the Alola class against Brock and Misty. The episode builds to Z-Move vs. Mega Evolution, ending in Pikachu's Gigavolt Havoc beating Misty's Mega Gyarados. The head series writer Atsuhiro Tomioka wrote it.

### Gaps

## Q5. Who writes Pokémon episodes: series composition and the writing team

### Takeaway
Each TV series has one **series composition** (シリーズ構成) writer who sets the overall direction, with a rotating team of episode screenwriters. For Sun & Moon it was **Aya Matsui** (松井亜弥). Atsuhiro Tomioka led the DP, BW, XY and XY&Z series (and *Mezase Pokémon Master*) but was one of the rotating episode writers on Sun & Moon. Storyboard artists, episode directors and animation directors also rotate per episode.

### Cited Findings
- **Sun & Moon main staff:** 【総監督】湯山邦彦 (chief director Kunihiko Yuyama), 【監督】冨安大貴 (director Daiki Tomiyasu), 【シリーズ構成】松井亜弥 (series composition Aya Matsui), 【キャラクターデザイン】中野悟史、安田周平 (character design Satoshi Nakano and Shūhei Yasuda), music 宮崎慎二 (Shinji Miyazaki). — [anime.eiga.com](https://anime.eiga.com/program/105875/)
- Japanese Wikipedia credits 「シリーズ構成・イーブイどこいくの？構成 - 松井亜弥」, citing TV Tokyo's staff page. The TV listing names "松井亜弥(構成)". — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3); [thetv.jp](https://thetv.jp/program/0000913720/104/)
- **Tomioka:** his profile says he worked on the TV anime as series composition and scriptwriter. It lists series composition for *Diamond & Pearl* (2006–10), *Best Wishes* (2010–13), *XY* (2013–15) and *XY&Z* (2015–16, 47 episodes + 2 specials), and series construction on *Mezase Pokémon Master* (2023). — [Nerima interview vol. 29, 2024-08-01](https://nerima-eizobunka.com/interview/vol29/)
- **Rotating writers on Sun & Moon** (from search excerpts of the Japanese Pokémon Wiki, which returned 403 when opened):
  - SM16: 冨岡淳広 (Tomioka)
  - SM20, SM67, SM116: 松井亜弥 (Matsui)
  - SM28, SM64, SM117: 藤咲淳一 (Junichi Fujisaku)
  - SM50, SM56, SM69: 米村正二 (Shōji Yonemura)

  - Akemi Omode wrote both SM106 and SM114, and Tomioka wrote SM043 (see Q2–Q4).
- **On team writing**, Tomioka says: 「チームの方が面白いんですよ。1人で書くのはつまらない。」 ("A team is more fun. Writing alone is boring."). He adds that another writer's proposal "widens the range" of a lead character. ### Inferences

## Q6. The episode formula, and what Sun & Moon changed

### Takeaway
No official or staff document laying out a fixed "Pokémon formula" was found. The sources that were opened support:
- Sun & Moon centred the show on **school life**,
- it added more **gag-style face distortions**,
- it was drawn in **simpler, looser lines**.

Team Rocket's recurring "blast off" ending is documented only second-hand, from fan translations of Shudo's blog. The three episodes above show the usual shape: cold open, school or briefing set-up, A/B plot, battle as the climax, epilogue gag.

### Cited Findings
- **Japanese Wikipedia on Sun & Moon:**
  - 「サトシのポケモンスクールでの学校生活が主軸となっている」 ("Satoshi's school life at the Pokémon School is the main axis").
  - 「キャラクターの顔が崩れたり鼻水を垂らすなどのギャグ的な演出も増えている」 ("gag direction such as characters' faces collapsing or noses running has increased").
  - It is drawn 「ややシンプルなゆるっとした線で」 ("in somewhat simpler, looser lines").
  - — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3)
- English Wikipedia: Ash "attends the Pokémon School in the Alola region". — [Wikipedia: Sun & Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series:_Sun_%26_Moon)
- **Art style, via Looper citing ANN:** "the new style gave animators the freedom to create a wider range of comical expressions", and the style "allows for more movement and exaggerated expressions". Looper (Brooke Knappenberger, 1 Jul 2022) paraphrases ANN here and quotes no staff. — [Looper](https://www.looper.com/914079/why-pokemon-sun-moon-stood-out-so-much-to-anime-fans), citing [ANN feature, 9 Dec 2016](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2016-12-09/why-does-pokemon-look-so-different-now/.109646)
- **The ANN feature itself** ("Why Does Pokemon Look So Different Now?", Callum May, 9 Dec 2016) was not opened (403 / DNS block). Search excerpts say it quotes animation producer Hiroyuki Kato on the XY look growing out of 3D experiments in *Black & White*, and says Sun & Moon moved to Toon Boom while much animation was still drawn on paper. Treat this as **UNVERIFIED** until the article is read. — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2016-12-09/why-does-pokemon-look-so-different-now/.109646)
- **Broadcast changes:**
  - Sun & Moon began in TV Tokyo's Thursday 18:55–19:25 slot and moved to Sunday 18:00–18:30 from October 2018.
  - From episode 3 a data-broadcast quiz corner, 「ポケもんだい」, ran before the avant (cold open) and after the ending.
  - Episodes 93–98 had a mini-corner, 「イーブイどこいくの？」.
  - The next-episode preview starts directly over story footage with a 「次回予告」 caption.
  - — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3)
- **Team Rocket formula, second-hand only:** a Bulbagarden fan translation of Shudo's 2005–2010 blog reportedly says a formulaic format with Team Rocket "blasting off" at the end of the day was easier for the writers than coming up with more original ideas. It also reportedly says the Rocket voice actors were the ones who wanted their motto in every episode. This was seen only in a search excerpt (403 when opened), so it is **UNVERIFIED**. — [Bulbanews: Translating the Blog Entries of Mr. Shudo](https://bulbanews.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbagraphic:Translating_the_Blog_Entries_of_Mr._Shudo)
- **The three studied episodes show the structure:**
  - **SM114**: night cold open with a threat (Pheromosa robs Hala) → morning school or briefing set-up → Team Rocket subplot that crosses the main plot (Meowth's crush) → failed first battle → trap plan → climax fight (Bewear) → capture and resolution at sunset → two epilogue gags. — [Wikidex EP1057](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1057)
  - **SM106**: two parallel plots (Sophocles' canyon trip; Lana's sea training), each paying off with an evolution, joined at a night party. Team Rocket is not in the summary. — [Wikidex EP1049](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP1049)
  - **SM043**: a run of rising battles with the hero's battle last, then a farewell and a post-credits gag. — [Wikidex EP986](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP986)

### Inferences
- Under Sun & Moon the formula bends rather than breaks:
  - Team Rocket is optional (SM106) or a comic sub-plot that turns a villain's story into an emotional one (Meowth's crush in SM114) rather than a weekly villain.
  - Battles are placed as the climax.
  - The "lesson" is usually tied to a Pokémon's growth (evolution, a new Z-Crystal) more than a spoken moral.
  - Episodes often end on a one-shot gag after the resolution (Faba stuck in glue, Jigglypuff on the plane).

### Gaps
- No primary staff source (interview, scenario guide or TV Tokyo document) states a cold-open / A-plot / B-plot / Team Rocket / battle / lesson template for any Pokémon series. The formula above is inferred from episode summaries.
- No primary staff statement explaining the Sun & Moon art-style change was found. Only ANN's analysis (not opened) and Looper's paraphrase of it were available.

## Q7. What staff say about storytelling goals (emotion, humour, Pokémon personalities, bonding)

### Takeaway
The clearest staff statements come from chief director **Kunihiko Yuyama** in *Animedia*, reported by GoNintendo in March 2023:
- communication between people and Pokémon is "one of the main themes";
- each Pokémon is a "unique individual" with its own reasons;
- Pikachu combines "cuteness, strength and the ability to bring a few laughs";
- stories start from imagining what a Pokémon would really do in a given place.

### Cited Findings
- **Yuyama** (*Animedia*, via GoNintendo, 16 Mar 2023):
  - "I think of Pikachu as a combination of cuteness, strength and the ability to bring a few laughs."
  - "Pokemon aren't always what humans expect them to be like." … "They're unique individuals that each have their own individual reasons for how they interact with humans."
  - "Communication is one of the main themes of the show, and Satoshi's first Pokemon, Pikachu, puts it into practice."
  - "we try to start by imagining what would happen if these Pokémon actually did exist in a given place, and what those Pokémon would be doing in that case, then let the story develop from there."
  - On why Ash got Pikachu rather than a game starter: "I figured that might make some of the viewers sad."
  - No issue number or translator is given. — [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/17625-pokemon-anime-director-explains-why-ash-was-paired-with-pikachu)
- **Yuyama on Ash's final season** (*Animedia*, via GoNintendo, 14 Mar 2023): "It started with the idea of tracing [Ash] Satoshi and Pikachu's journey one more time", depicting the world champion "just living the life of a 10 year old boy again." — [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/contents/17493-pokemon-anime-director-details-the-goal-for-ash-s-final-season)
- **Tomioka** writes first for small children, aiming for what they find "cool" and want to act out in pretend play. — [Nerima interview vol. 29](https://nerima-eizobunka.com/interview/vol29/)
- When asked whether he likes human-and-partner pairs like Satoshi and Pikachu, Tomioka said it's simply the setup the job requires: 「それが好きなわけではないんです」 ("it's not that I like it"). — [Nerima interview vol. 29](https://nerima-eizobunka.com/interview/vol29/)

### Inferences
- For short-form storytelling that borrows from this, the staff's stated recipe is:
  - give each creature its own motive;
  - let humour and emotion come from miscommunication that becomes understanding;
  - build the plot from "what would this creature really do here?";
  - keep a younger audience's "cool / want to imitate" test in mind.

### Gaps
- Daiki Tomiyasu's *Animedia* June 2020 interview has an English translation on Dogasu's Backpack, but it returned 403 and could not be quoted. — [Dogasu translation](https://dogasu.bulbagarden.net/features/translations/animedia_june2020_daiki_tomiyasu.html)
- No interview with Aya Matsui or the Sun & Moon producers on comedy goals was found.

## Q8. The runtime split of a ~22-minute episode

### Takeaway
The order of parts is documented, but not their exact lengths. A Japanese TV episode runs:
1. (recap)
2. avant-title / cold open
3. opening
4. A part
5. commercial break, framed by eyecatches
6. B part
7. ending
8. optional C part
9. next-episode preview

Sun & Moon filled the 30-minute TV Tokyo slot this way, with its quiz corner added. No source gave per-segment minute counts for Pokémon.

### Cited Findings
- **General anime episode order**: recap (前回までのあらすじ) → cold open (アバンタイトル) → OP → A part → commercial break → B part → ED → optional C part → preview. From a CC BY-SA diagram on Japanese with Anime, seen only via a search excerpt; the article page returned 404. — [Japanese with Anime diagram](https://media.japanesewithanime.com/images/1738/parts-of-an-anime-episode.png/)
- **Sun & Moon slot:** 30 minutes, first Thursday 18:55–19:25 and then Sunday 18:00–18:30. The ポケもんだい quiz ran before the avant and after the ED, and the preview opens over story footage. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC_%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3&%E3%83%A0%E3%83%BC%E3%83%B3)
- **Who's That Pokémon?** is a segment that can differ by region: in SM043 the Japanese answer was Mega Steelix and the Western answer was Psyduck. — [Wikidex EP986](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/EP986)
- **Eyecatch timing:** a Pokémon wiki excerpt says "the 12 seconds available are used for two 6-second eyecatches or a single 12-second eyecatch". Seen only in a search excerpt of Bulbapedia's Eyecatch page (403), so it is **UNVERIFIED**. — [Bulbapedia: Eyecatch](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eyecatch)

### Inferences
- Within a 30-minute slot, the programme (including OP and ED) is typically about 22–24 minutes, the rest being ads. This is general industry knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** for Pokémon specifically.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** exact lengths of the OP, A part, B part, ED and preview in Sun & Moon episodes. No official or staff source was found, and the episodes were not timed. Measuring a legal copy (Pokémon TV, Netflix or a licensed DVD) would answer it.
