# Hooks, value all the way through, the comment question, clear understanding, and coming back (5 Oct 2026)

Owner's ask: hooks so viewers don't swipe away, value information all the way through, a question at the end so
they comment, clear understanding so they come back. Deep research, no guesses.

This builds on `research_notes/news_pacing.md` (speed and pauses) and on the 29 Sep notes in
`research_notes/Shorts retention for animated explainers/`. Those notes cover curiosity-gap research
(Loewenstein 1994; Kang et al. 2009), "but/therefore" storytelling and Jenny Hoyos's Shorts structure.

**Access note.** Reddit refuses connections from this environment (HTTP 403 on reddit.com and old.reddit.com), so
Reddit threads could not be read directly. On GitHub I found only small personal analyses of Shorts (for example,
75 Shorts from one channel), which are too small to rely on. The evidence below is peer-reviewed research, YouTube's
own statements, and large surveys.

**[Creator]** a creator or vendor claim with no published method.

## 1. The hook: state the surprising fact. Don't open with a question.

- **[Academic/field]** Scacco & Muddiman (2016), *Investigating the Influence of "Clickbait" News Headlines*,
  Engaging News Project. Experiment with 2,057 US adults.
  - **Question headlines** led to more negative reactions, more negative expectations of the story, and slightly
    less intent to engage than plain headlines.
  - **Forward-reference headlines** ("Here is how…") were no worse than plain ones.
  - https://mediaengagement.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/ENP-Investigating-the-Influence-of-Clickbait-News-Headlines.pdf
- **[Academic]** Molyneux & Coddington (2020): a clickbait headline lowered the perceived credibility of the news,
  and there may be a wear-out effect over time.
  https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b07bc3ca25119d65a499c29cba702ae1fa3728ee
- **[Official]** YouTube's recommendation system measures "valued watchtime" through viewer surveys (1-5 stars).
  Demoting sensational "borderline" content raised overall watch time.
  https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/
- **[Academic]** Curiosity is the feeling of a gap in knowledge, and it is strongest when the gap feels closable
  (Loewenstein 1994; Kang et al. 2009). See the 29 Sep notes.
- **Rule for our hooks.**
  1. Sentence 1 states the most surprising true fact, with the key word in the first ~1.5 seconds.
  2. Keep it short (10-15 words).
  3. Make it a statement, not a question.
  4. A forward reference is allowed only when the video pays it off.

## 2. "Why this matters to you", early

- **[Academic]** Symons & Johnson (1997), *The self-reference effect in memory: a meta-analysis*, Psychological
  Bulletin (62 studies, over 3,000 people). Information people relate to themselves is remembered better.
  https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a59cefe5401fa58c0e65bdac7e9f9607f5fe51f
- **[Survey]** Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025:
  - 40% of people sometimes or often avoid the news.
  - Under-35s are much more likely to say the news is too hard to follow or understand.
  - People ask for better explanations, more relevance to their lives, and more constructive reporting.
  - 65% now watch social video news, up from 52% in 2020.
  - https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary
- **Rule.** Sentence 2 or 3 says why it matters to the viewer ("Even if you never buy diesel, you are probably paying
  for it.").

## 3. Clear understanding (the reason people come back)

- **[Academic]** Mayer & Fiorella, *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*, ch. 12. Each principle below held in
  almost every test:
  - **Temporal contiguity:** pictures shown at the same moment as the words. 9 of 9 tests, median d = 1.22.
    *We do this:* every picture pops on its spoken word.
  - **Coherence:** cut extra material. 23 of 23 tests, median d = 0.86. *Rule:* one idea per sentence, no side facts.
  - **Signaling:** cues that show the structure. 24 of 28 tests, median d = 0.41. *We do this* with headlines; the
    scripts add short signposts ("Why did the price jump so much?", "But there is a catch.", "And there is one more
    change.").
  - **Redundancy:** narration plus identical full on-screen text can hurt. 16 of 16 tests, d = 0.86. But it doesn't
    apply when the on-screen text is short. *We do this:* 2-3-word captions and short headlines, not full sentences.
  - https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf
- **[Academic]** Pre-training: explain a key term **before** using it (reported d ≈ 0.46-0.78).
  - *Rule:* "Permanent residency lets a foreigner stay in Japan with no time limit." comes before the details.
  - "The G7, a group of seven of the world's biggest economies" keeps its explanation.
- **[Academic]** Montero Perez, Van Den Noortgate & Desmet (2013), *System* 41(3), meta-analysis: captions
  improved second-language listening comprehension a lot (g = 0.99). This matters because we are a global channel.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X13001012
- **[Academic]** Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995): counts like "1 in 5" are understood far better than percentages or
  probabilities (correct reasoning went from 16% to 46%).
  - *Rule:* "one in every five barrels", "about four in every hundred people", "almost four times as many".
- **[Academic]** Explanatory video reporting gave better understanding of an issue than conventional news
  (*Explanatory reporting in video format*, Journalism Practice 2021; abstract via search, full text paywalled).
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17512786.2021.1966644

## 4. Value all the way through, without empty teasers

- **[Academic]** Ghibellini & Meier (2025), meta-analysis of the "Zeigarnik effect" (37 studies, *Humanities &
  Social Sciences Communications*).
  - **No general memory advantage** for unfinished tasks.
  - People do tend to **go back to** interrupted things (the Ovsiankina effect).
  - "Open loops" are not magic. What keeps people watching is a real payoff each step.
  - https://colab.ws/articles/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w
- **[Creator / YouTube Blog]** Hoyos: hook, foreshadow, then "but/therefore" steps with no filler.
  https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/
- **[Survey]** Reuters 2025: people want constructive reporting ("what happens next / what can I do"), and
  powerlessness is a reason they avoid news.
- **Rules.**
  - Every sentence adds a new fact or a step of explanation. One signpost per video in the middle sets up a real
    payoff.
  - Near the end comes one **"what happens next / what you can do"** line. Examples:
    - "The next jobs report comes out on November sixth"
    - "see which apps already have it in System Settings, under Privacy and Security"
    - "if you fall sick after contact with wild animals in the West, see a doctor quickly"

## 5. The question at the end, so people comment

- **[Academic]** de Vries, Gensler & Leeflang (2012), *Journal of Interactive Marketing* 26(2), 355 brand posts:
  **asking a question raised the number of comments.**
  https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:joinma:v:26:y:2012:i:2:p:83-91
- **[Survey methodology]** Pew Research: open questions go unanswered about 18% of the time, against 1-2% for closed
  (pick-one) questions. Easy questions get answered.
  https://www.pewresearch.org/decoded/2021/10/14/why-do-some-open-ended-survey-questions-result-in-higher-item-nonresponse-rates-than-others/
- **Rule.**
  1. End on one easy, personal question that anyone can answer in a word ("Would you…?", "Have you noticed…?",
     "Is it harder… where you live?").
  2. Follow it with "Tell me in the comments." and show the YES/NO buttons on screen.
  3. The pinned comment repeats the question.
  4. Don't ask for likes or subscribes in the voice: it adds seconds where people drop off.

## 6. Coming back (returning viewers)

- **[Official]** YouTube weighs satisfaction, through surveys and likes and shares, not just watch time. Videos
  people rate 4-5 stars count as "valued watchtime". See section 1.
- **[Official]** Since July 2025, YouTube Studio splits viewers into **new**, **casual** and **regular**. Watch the
  "regular" line to see whether people come back.
- **[Academic]** Viewers keep watching creators they have a parasocial bond with. That bond is stronger when the
  creator is reliable: consistent in character, predictable, and posting on a set schedule. (SPU honors thesis
  review; Akron thesis.)
  https://digitalcommons.spu.edu/context/honorsprojects/article/1229/viewcontent/_I_m_not_your_friend_Content_type_influence_on_parasocial_relationship__formation_on_YouTube.pdf
- **Rules.**
  - Same narrator voice, same look (LATEST badge, globe, handwritten headlines) and the same story structure every
    time.
  - Post at the same two times every day (9:30 PM and 4:30 AM IST).
  - When a story has a known next step, say when it comes. We follow up, which gives people a reason to come back
    ("The next jobs report comes out on November sixth, and we will tell you what it shows").

## 7. The structure every news script now follows

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **HOOK** | sentence 1 | The most surprising true fact, key word first, a statement |
| **YOU** | sentence 2-3 | Why it matters to the viewer |
| **CTX** | before the term is used | Define the term, translate numbers ("1 in 5", "$1,270") |
| **STEP** | the body | One fact or step per sentence, linked by "so", "but", "because" |
| **SIGN** | about halfway | One signpost ("Why did…?", "But there is a catch.") with a real payoff |
| **NEXT** | last but one | What happens next, or what you can do |
| **ASK** | last | One easy question, then "Tell me in the comments." |
