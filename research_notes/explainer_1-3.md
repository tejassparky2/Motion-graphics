# Explainers 1-3: scripts for the owner's approval (24 Oct 2026)

New format (see `explainer_study.md`): one narrator (Chatterbox Turbo stock female, tags), 75-90 s, aged paper with
fold creases, a "SECTION ... FIG. N" header, a two-line headline (small dark line + big red word) on every beat,
no subtitles, our own drawn objects + public-domain Gray's Anatomy (1918) plates. Each row: narration | header
label | headline (small / BIG) | what's on screen. Turbo tags are in square brackets; they only shade the delivery.

---

## Explainer 1: Snakebite antivenom (about 90 s)

**Title:** The Cure for a Snakebite Is Made Inside a Horse
Alt titles: Why Snakebite Medicine Comes From Horses / India's Deadliest Animal Isn't a Tiger / How Antivenom Is Made

| # | Narration | Label | Headline | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [dramatic] If a cobra bites you tonight, the medicine that saves you was made inside a horse. | HOOK | MADE INSIDE / A HORSE | Cobra cut-out strikes from torn paper; a paper horse slides in; Rx vial drops beside it |
| 2 | Not a lab. Not a factory. A horse. | HOOK | NOT A LAB. / A HORSE. | Lab flask and factory crossed out with red X; horse stamped "TRUE" |
| 3 | A huge study found that snakebites killed about fifty-eight thousand Indians a year, for twenty years. | INDIA | EVERY YEAR / 58,000 | Paper map of India, glowing dots; flip counter rolls to 58,000 |
| 4 | That's close to half of all snakebite deaths in the world. | THE WORLD | CLOSE TO / HALF | Globe; a pie chart fills nearly half in red |
| 5 | So how does a horse fix a snakebite? First, let's see what venom does. | THE VENOM | FIRST, / THE VENOM | Venom drop falls into a microscope circle |
| 6 | Your body runs on messages. | THE VENOM | YOUR BODY / RUNS ON MESSAGES | Gray's body outline (white on dark board) with nerves lighting up |
| 7 | Your nerves tell your muscles when to move, and when to breathe. | THE VENOM | MOVE. / BREATHE. | Signal sparks run down a nerve to the arm, then to the diaphragm |
| 8 | Cobra and krait venom jams that signal. | THE VENOM | IT JAMS / THE SIGNAL | Red venom blobs block the spark at the nerve-muscle junction; spark fizzles |
| 9 | The eyelids droop first. Then the breathing muscles can stop. | THE VENOM | EYELIDS DROOP / THEN BREATHING | Paper face, eyelids drooping; lungs slow, stopwatch appears |
| 10 | Viper venom does something else. It stops your blood from making clots. | THE VENOM | BLOOD / WON'T CLOT | Russell's viper card; blood drop that won't form a clot (red cells scatter) |
| 11 | Now, the horse. | THE HORSE | NOW, / THE HORSE | Horse cut-out on khaki paper |
| 12 | Scientists collect venom from the four snakes behind most deaths in India. | THE HORSE | THE / BIG FOUR | Four snake cards pinned up: cobra, krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper |
| 13 | They give a horse tiny doses. Too small to hurt it. | THE HORSE | TINY / DOSES | Syringe; a dose gauge with the needle in a green "SAFE" zone |
| 14 | The horse's immune army learns the enemy, and builds antibodies. | THE HORSE | ITS ARMY / LEARNS | Microscope circle: Y-shaped antibodies appear and grab venom blobs |
| 15 | Doctors take some of its blood, and purify those antibodies. | THE HORSE | PURIFIED / ANTIBODIES | Blood bag -> filter -> clear vial labelled ANTIVENOM |
| 16 | That's antivenom. Borrowed bodyguards that grab the venom before it does more damage. | THE CURE | BORROWED / BODYGUARDS | Antibodies swarm venom in the blood; venom neutralised (greyed out) |
| 17 | But many people don't die from the snake. They die from the delay. | THE DELAY | NOT THE SNAKE / THE DELAY | Stopwatch, red sector growing |
| 18 | A krait often bites at night, while people sleep on the floor. | THE DELAY | AT NIGHT / ON THE FLOOR | Paper room at night, moon in window, mat on floor, krait sliding past |
| 19 | The bite can be almost painless, so people go back to sleep. | THE DELAY | ALMOST / PAINLESS | Tiny two-dot bite; "PAIN" meter barely moves |
| 20 | Others lose hours on cutting, sucking, tight ropes or herbs. | MYTHS | HOURS / WASTED | Four Polaroids on a string: knife, mouth, rope, leaves; each gets a red X |
| 21 | None of these remove the venom. | MYTHS | NONE / WORK | Stamp: "DOESN'T WORK" |
| 22 | Doctors say: stay calm, keep the limb still, and get to a hospital fast. | WHAT TO DO | STAY CALM / GET TO HOSPITAL | Checklist card ticks: Calm / Still / Hospital |
| 23 | Did you know your life could depend on a horse? Tell me in the comments. | END | A HORSE / SAVED YOU? | Horse with a tiny red cross badge; comment bubble |

Facts and sources:
- 1.2 million snakebite deaths in India 2000-2019, average 58,000/year: Suraweera et al., eLife 2020;9:e54076
  (Million Death Study), https://elifesciences.org/articles/54076; also in WHO's fact sheet.
- World: 81,410-137,880 deaths/year (WHO fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/snakebite-envenoming).
  58,000 / 81,410-137,880 = 42-71%, so "close to half".
- Paralysis that may stop breathing, bleeding disorders: WHO fact sheet.
- Big Four (cobra, common krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper); Indian polyvalent antivenom made by immunising
  horses with sub-lethal venom doses, antibodies purified from their blood: Think Global Health / ICMR-related
  reporting (https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/antivenom-plan-india), Indian Pediatrics (Big Four).
- Krait: bites at night to people sleeping on the floor, often painless, ptosis then respiratory paralysis:
  Indian Pediatrics krait case series (https://www.indianpediatrics.net/nov2007/nov-852-853.htm) and Sri Lankan series.
- Antivenom from horse serum, Calmette, 1890s: NIH Fogarty "Antivenom: a brief history"
  (https://fic.nih.gov/News/GlobalHealthMatters/september-october-2022/Pages/antivenom-brief-history.aspx).
- First aid: India's protocol "Do it R.I.G.H.T." (Reassure, Immobilise, Get to Hospital, Tell the doctor); no
  tourniquet, cutting, suction or herbal remedies (Indian Pediatrics 2007 https://indianpediatrics.net/mar2007/mar-173-176.htm;
  IJMR 2023 table https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438420/). Re-check against the MoHFW National
  Snakebite Management Protocol text before upload.

## Explainer 2: Superbugs (about 85 s)

**Title:** Bacteria Are Learning to Beat Our Medicine
Alt titles: Why Antibiotics Are Stopping Working / The Superbug Problem in 80 Seconds / Your Body Isn't Resistant. They Are.

| # | Narration | Label | Headline | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [dramatic] A tiny cut on your finger used to be able to kill you. | HOOK | A TINY CUT / COULD KILL | Paper finger with a small cut; red lines spreading |
| 2 | Then in 1928, a mould was found that kills bacteria. | 1928 | THEN CAME / PENICILLIN | Petri dish: mould spot, clear ring where bacteria died |
| 3 | Now the bacteria are fighting back. | HOOK | THEY'RE / FIGHTING BACK | The ring fills back in with bacteria |
| 4 | In 2021, drug-resistant bacteria directly killed more than a million people. | THE WORLD | 2021 / 1,140,000 | Globe; flip counter 1,140,000 |
| 5 | And they played a part in nearly five million deaths. | THE WORLD | NEARLY / 5 MILLION | Counter rolls on to 4,710,000 |
| 6 | These germs are called superbugs. So how does a germ beat a medicine? | SUPERBUGS | HOW DO GERMS / BEAT MEDICINE? | Bacterium cut-out wearing a little shield |
| 7 | An infection can be billions of bacteria. | INSIDE | BILLIONS / OF THEM | Microscope circle packed with bacteria |
| 8 | Their copies aren't perfect, so by chance, a few are slightly different. | INSIDE | A FEW ARE / DIFFERENT | Three bacteria tinted a different colour, circled in red |
| 9 | Now the antibiotic hits. | THE DRUG | THE DRUG / HITS | Capsule drops in; a wave sweeps across |
| 10 | It kills almost all of them. But not the different ones. | THE DRUG | ALMOST / ALL | Bacteria pop; the three tinted ones remain |
| 11 | Those survivors multiply, and now the whole infection carries their shield. | SURVIVORS | THE SURVIVORS / TAKE OVER | Survivors divide 1-2-4-8; circle refills with shielded bacteria |
| 12 | Worse, bacteria can hand the shield to their neighbours, in tiny loops of DNA. | SURVIVORS | THEY / SHARE IT | A small DNA ring hops from one bacterium to another |
| 13 | Even to completely different kinds of bacteria. | SURVIVORS | EVEN TO / STRANGERS | Rod-shaped and round bacteria swap the ring |
| 14 | The man who found penicillin saw this coming. | 1945 | HE / WARNED US | Paper portrait silhouette, Nobel medal |
| 15 | In 1945, he warned that taking too little would teach the microbes to resist. | 1945 | TOO LITTLE / TEACHES THEM | Typed quote card, red underline |
| 16 | Here's what most people get wrong. Your body doesn't become resistant. The bacteria do. | MYTHS | NOT YOU. / THE BACTERIA. | Paper body with a red X; bacterium with a tick |
| 17 | And antibiotics do nothing against a cold or the flu. Those are viruses. | MYTHS | COLD? FLU? / VIRUSES. | Tissue box and thermometer; capsule bounces off a virus |
| 18 | Every dose you didn't need is free training for the bacteria. | MYTHS | FREE / TRAINING | Bacteria lifting tiny dumbbells |
| 19 | So take antibiotics only when a doctor prescribes them, exactly as prescribed. | WHAT TO DO | ONLY WHEN / PRESCRIBED | Prescription card with a tick |
| 20 | Never share them, and never use leftovers. | WHAT TO DO | NO SHARING / NO LEFTOVERS | Half-used strip with a red X; two hands passing pills crossed out |
| 21 | Experts estimate superbugs could directly kill thirty-nine million people between now and 2050. | 2050 | BY 2050 / 39 MILLION | Calendar flips 2025 -> 2050; counter 39,000,000 |
| 22 | Have you ever taken antibiotics for a cold? Be honest in the comments. | END | BE / HONEST | Comment bubble |

Facts and sources:
- 2021: 1.14 million deaths attributable to bacterial AMR, 4.71 million associated; 39 million attributable deaths
  forecast 2025-2050: GBD 2021 AMR Collaborators, The Lancet 2024;404:1199-1226 (GRAM), summary at
  https://www.ndm.ox.ac.uk/news/antibiotic-resistance-could-cause-39-million-deaths-between-now-and-2050.
- Penicillin discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928; his 1945 Nobel lecture warned that underdosing would make
  microbes resistant: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1945/fleming/questions-and-answers/.
- "Bacteria, not the body, develop the ability to defeat the antibiotics": CDC
  (https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/AU-AR-Bacteria-infographic-P.pdf).
- Antibiotics don't work on viruses such as colds and flu: CDC Antibiotic Use pages / WHO AMR fact sheet.
- Resistance genes spread between bacteria (including different species) on plasmids: American Society for
  Microbiology, https://asm.org/articles/2023/january/plasmids-and-the-spread-of-antibiotic-resistance-g.
- "Only when prescribed; never share or use leftover antibiotics": WHO antimicrobial resistance fact sheet.
- Checked: CDC (antibiotics don't help viruses such as colds and flu; don't take someone else's antibiotics; don't
  keep leftovers) and WHO AMR fact sheet (misuse and overuse drive resistance).

## Explainer 3: Malaria (about 90 s)

**Title:** This Parasite Hides in Your Liver Before It Attacks
Alt titles: What a Mosquito Really Puts Inside You / The Parasite That Sleeps in Your Liver / How Malaria Actually Works

| # | Narration | Label | Headline | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [dramatic] One mosquito bite. And something moves into your liver. | HOOK | ONE / BITE | Mosquito lands on a paper arm; Gray's liver plate glows red |
| 2 | For days, you feel completely fine. | HOOK | YOU FEEL / FINE | Paper room, calendar flipping DAY 3, DAY 7; health card "NORMAL" stamps |
| 3 | Then it breaks out into your blood, again and again. | HOOK | AGAIN / AND AGAIN | Red cells bursting in a microscope circle |
| 4 | It's malaria. In 2024, it made about two hundred and eighty million people sick. | THE WORLD | 2024 / 282 MILLION | Globe, flip counter 282,000,000 |
| 5 | And it killed about six hundred thousand. Most of them young children in Africa. | THE WORLD | 610,000 / MOSTLY CHILDREN | Counter 610,000; Africa highlighted |
| 6 | And it isn't a virus or a bacterium. It's a parasite. So let's follow its journey. | THE PARASITE | IT'S A / PARASITE | Microscope circle: crescent-shaped parasite, label "PLASMODIUM" |
| 7 | Only female mosquitoes drink blood. They need it to make eggs. | THE BITE | ONLY / FEMALES | Female mosquito card with egg raft; male crossed out |
| 8 | When an infected one bites, tiny parasites slip into your blood. | THE BITE | INTO / YOUR BLOOD | Close-up: proboscis, tiny parasites streaming in |
| 9 | Within hours, they reach your liver and hide inside its cells. | THE LIVER | THEY / HIDE | Paper body map: dotted route arm -> liver; liver cells with parasites inside |
| 10 | There, quietly, each one multiplies into thousands. | THE LIVER | ONE BECOMES / THOUSANDS | Liver cell swelling, counter rolling up |
| 11 | Then the liver cells burst, and the parasites flood your blood. | THE BLOOD | THEN / THEY BURST | Liver cell pops; flood into a red blood vessel |
| 12 | Each one breaks into a red blood cell, multiplies again, and bursts it open. | THE BLOOD | INVADE / AND BURST | Red cell fills with parasites and splits |
| 13 | That's when you get sick, usually ten to fifteen days after the bite. Fever, chills, headache. | THE BLOOD | DAY 10-15 / FEVER | Calendar DAY 12; thermometer rising; blanket |
| 14 | And one type, called vivax, can leave sleeping parasites in the liver. They can wake up weeks or even years later. | THE LIVER | IT CAN / SLEEP | Liver plate with a tiny sleeping parasite and an alarm clock; calendar pages flying |
| 15 | We know mosquitoes spread it because of a discovery in India. | INDIA | DISCOVERED / IN INDIA | Paper map of India, pin on Secunderabad |
| 16 | In 1897, in Secunderabad, Ronald Ross found the parasite inside a mosquito. It won him a Nobel Prize. | 1897 | SECUNDERABAD / 1897 | Old microscope, sepia notebook page, mosquito specimen; a medal drops onto the notebook |
| 17 | Malaria doesn't spread from person to person. It needs the mosquito. | MYTHS | NOT PERSON / TO PERSON | Two paper people with a tick between them; mosquito circled |
| 18 | And it can be treated. If you get a fever in a malaria area, see a doctor. A simple blood test can find it. | WHAT TO DO | SEE A / DOCTOR | Blood test slide; "MALARIA: DETECTED" card, Rx bottle |
| 19 | Bed nets and repellent stop the very first step. | WHAT TO DO | STOP / THE BITE | Bed net drops over a sleeping figure; mosquito bounces off |
| 20 | Have you or someone you know had malaria? Tell me in the comments. | END | HAVE YOU / HAD IT? | Comment bubble |

Facts and sources:
- 2024: about 282 million cases and 610,000 deaths; African Region about 95% of deaths; children under 5 about 75%
  of the African Region's deaths; symptoms 10-15 days after the bite; fever, headache, chills; not spread person to
  person (except blood transfusion/needles); test every suspected case; nets and repellents: WHO malaria fact sheet
  (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria). 95% x 75% = about 71%, so "most of them young
  children in Africa".
- Female Anopheles feed on blood for egg production: CDC (https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/about/life-cycles/anopheles.html).
- Life cycle (sporozoites -> liver cells -> schizonts burst -> merozoites invade red cells; blood stage causes the
  illness; P. vivax and P. ovale hypnozoites relapse weeks or years later): CDC DPDx
  (https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/malaria/index.html).
- Synchronised bursting gives periodic fever (every second day for vivax/ovale; less regular in falciparum):
  standard parasitology; worded as "in some types".
- "Thousands" from one liver-stage parasite: Prudêncio, Rodriguez & Mota, "The silent path to thousands of
  merozoites: the Plasmodium liver stage", Nature Reviews Microbiology 2006.
- Ronald Ross, 20 Aug 1897, Secunderabad, parasite in an Anopheles mosquito; Nobel Prize 1902: LSHTM
  (https://www.lstmed.ac.uk/node/1777), nobelprize.org.
