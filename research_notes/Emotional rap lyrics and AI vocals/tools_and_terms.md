# AI music tools for emotional sung/rapped vocals: quality, commercial terms, YouTube policy, copyright (as of 24 Oct 2026)

Scope: a monetized YouTube Shorts rap/lyric-video channel run by an owner in India. Not legal advice. Every policy
statement is dated with the date shown on the source page (or the date it was opened, 24 Oct 2026, where the page
gives none). Sources marked "(search summary)" were seen only as search-result summaries, not opened in full; treat
them as lower confidence. Pages marked "(opened)" were fetched and read.

## Q1. Which tools give the most human, emotional vocals (incl. rap), and what are their limits and prices?

### Takeaway
Suno (v6, launched 9 Sep 2026; its previous v5.5 is #1 for vocals on the Artificial Analysis blind-vote music arena)
and Mureka V9 (#2 vocals) are the vocal-quality leaders; Google Lyria 3 Pro / 3.5 and ElevenLabs Music rank lower on
vocals but have cleaner commercial positions. Udio cannot be used for publishing (downloads disabled since 29 Oct
2025). No rap-specific blind test was found: the owner must A/B test rap delivery himself.

## Q2. Commercial terms: ownership, free-tier use, YouTube monetization, attribution, Content ID, SynthID

### Takeaway
Suno: paid plans only, and only for songs downloaded through an approved channel within the monthly cap; Suno assigns
ownership but warrants neither copyright nor non-infringement. ElevenLabs: user retains output; streaming distribution
only from Creator ($22/mo) up; Free needs attribution. Google: "won't claim ownership", SynthID always embedded, no
explicit consumer commercial-use clause found. Content ID registration of AI songs is the riskiest area and is
poorly documented by primary sources.

### Cited Findings
**Suno Terms (last revised 10 Aug 2026, effective 3 Sep 2026) (opened)** — [suno.com/terms](https://suno.com/terms)
- Pro/Premier: "Suno hereby assigns to you all of its right, title and interest in and to any Output owned by Suno."
- Free/Basic: Outputs only "for your lawful, personal and non-commercial purposes."
- "You may not commercially exploit Output that has not been downloaded by you through an approved channel"; "The
  number of Downloads you are permitted per month depends on your service tier"; stream ripping prohibited.
- Remixes: "may only be used for lawful, personal and non-commercial purposes."
- No attribution requirement; must not "remove, alter, obscure or circumvent any fingerprint, watermark or metadata."
- No warranty that use "will not infringe the rights of any third party"; "Suno makes no representation or warranty
  to you that any copyright will vest in any Output"; "the Service may generate the same or similar output for a
  third party."
- Off-platform use "is also subject to the terms, policies" of the third-party platform; "Applicable rights holders
  may also have the right to collect revenue" from third-party distribution.
- Broad licence to Suno over your content, incl. "likeness, voice rights," to improve models "without compensation."
- Suno blog (10 Aug 2026): paid-plan downloads carry "commercial rights to the music"; trial downloads are personal
  use only — [Suno blog (opened)](https://suno.com/blog/suno-updates-tos).

**ElevenLabs Music Terms (last updated 9 Oct 2026) (opened)** — [elevenlabs.io/music-terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)
- "you retain any and all rights in and to your Input and Output."
- Free: individual use, downloads not permitted, attribution required ("Created in collaboration with ElevenLabs" or
  similar); commercial use allowed except film, TV, radio, studio games. (Internal tension: commercial use allowed but
  no downloads; the marketing page says Free is "personal use only" — [elevenlabs.io/music (opened)](https://elevenlabs.io/music).)
- Music streaming distribution prohibited on Free and Starter, permitted from Creator up (s.4.4); must comply with
  platform AI-disclosure/labelling rules.
- No prompts using artist names, song/album titles or substantial lyrics (s.2.1); no output mimicking a recording
  artist's voice (s.3.3); user must clear output before use (s.3.1); no warranty the output carries a watermark (s.3.4).

**Google (Gemini app / Lyria)**
- Google Terms of Service (effective 30 Jul 2026): "Some of our services allow you to generate original content.
  Google won't claim ownership over that content." — [policies.google.com/terms (opened)](https://policies.google.com/terms).
- The old Generative AI Additional Terms (last modified 9 Aug 2023) no longer apply since 22 May 2024 except for
  business partners — [policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai (opened)](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai).
- Neither the Lyria 3 blog, Gemini music page nor help pages make an explicit commercial-use statement; they mention
  download and sharing "across your favorite platforms" and point to the ToS and Prohibited Use Policy —
  [Google blog (opened)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/lyria-3/); [Gemini music page (opened)](https://gemini.google/overview/music-generation/).
- Google may generate similar content for others; every output carries SynthID — [TeamDay (opened)](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-music-models-2026).
  SynthID does not establish ownership — [itechguides (search summary)](https://www.itechguides.com/googles-lyria-3-brings-ai-powered-music-generation-to-the-gemini-app/).
  Vertex AI (Cloud) docs say the preview may be used for production/commercial purposes, but that applies to the
  Cloud product, not the consumer app — [Google Cloud docs (search summary)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/lyria/lyria-3).

**Content ID and distributors**
- YouTube Content ID requires that owners "own exclusive rights to a substantial body of original material" —
  [YouTube Help 2797370 (opened)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370). Because Suno says it may give
  similar output to others and Google may generate similar content, exclusivity is doubtful (inference).
- Secondary claims (unverified against primary sources): DistroKid registers AI tracks for Content ID as an opt-in;
  TuneCore with a DDEX AI disclosure; CD Baby as standard; Amuse disabled Content ID for new AI submissions in 2026;
  AI tracks can be claimed if someone else registered a similar output, and self-claims happen —
  [Undetectr, 20 Apr 2026 (opened)](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-youtube-copyright-claims).
  Conflicting claim that fully AI audio has not been Content ID-eligible since July 2025 —
  [dynamoi (search summary)](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/does-distrokid-accept-ai-music).
- Suno partnered with Believe/TuneCore (8 Sep 2026) and signed BMG (12 Aug 2026) — [Mubert/aimusicpreneur (search summary)](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-tools-news/suno-download-caps-free-pro-premier-september-2026/).

### Inferences
- Minimum safe setup on Suno: Pro plan, generate AND download on Pro (20 songs/month is enough for ~1 Short/day only if
  re-generations aren't downloaded), keep download receipts, never use free or trial downloads or remixes commercially.
- Don't register the channel's AI songs for Content ID: it risks claiming your own Shorts or other creators' similar
  outputs, and exclusivity is doubtful. Monetizing the Shorts themselves does not require Content ID.
- Never remove SynthID or Suno watermarks/metadata (channel rule and both tools' terms agree).

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube or DistroKid page found that states Content ID eligibility for AI-generated music specifically.
- Gemini consumer-app commercial-use wording not found beyond the ToS ownership line; owner should verify.

## Q3. YouTube policies: AI disclosure, inauthentic content, AI music on Shorts, label deals

### Takeaway
YouTube monetizes AI-assisted content but (a) requires the "AI use" disclosure for realistic AI content, which the
help page applies to AI music that is the main focus, and (b) demonetizes templated, mass-produced output under the
"inauthentic content" rule (renamed 15 Jul 2025). The UMG/WMG settlements did not add rights for creators; they
mostly cut downloads and introduced caps.

### Cited Findings
- YPP policy (opened 24 Oct 2026): on 15 Jul 2025 "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content". Not
  monetizable: "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation
  across videos"; "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary,
  or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass
  production." Reused content bans "Collections of songs from different artists (even if you have their permission)"
  and pitch/speed-changed songs — [YouTube Help 1311392 (opened)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- YouTube's creator liaison Rene Ritchie called the July 2025 change a "minor update" —
  [Plagiarism Today, 8 Jul 2025 (search summary)](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/).
- Disclosure (opened 24 Oct 2026): creators must disclose realistic AI-generated/altered content; the page lists AI
  music that is the video's main focus among items needing disclosure; animation/fantasy, AI-assisted scripts, and
  cloning your own voice don't. Set "AI use" in YouTube Studio > Attributes. Labels appear in the player for AI music;
  YouTube may auto-label content made with its own GenAI tools, with C2PA metadata, or detected as AI. "Disclosing
  alone doesn't limit a video's audience or affect monetization eligibility"; repeated non-disclosure can mean removal
  or YPP suspension — [YouTube Help 14328491 (opened)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
  Caveat: this summary came through a fetch tool; the owner should read the page's exact music wording.
- Label deals: WMG settled with Suno 25 Nov 2025; Suno committed to licensed models in 2026 and retiring current
  models; free-tier songs not downloadable; paid downloads capped; artists opt in for names/voices/likeness; no
  statement on user ownership — [Music Business Worldwide, 25 Nov 2025 (opened)](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno-strikes-first-of-its-kind-deal-with-ai-song-generator/).
  UMG settled with Udio Oct 2025; WMG with Udio Nov 2025; GEMA and Koda claims against Suno continue (same source).
- UMG and Sony remain in litigation with Suno (impasse reported April 2026) — [OpusClip blog, Apr 2026 (search summary)](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-music-news-april-2026);
  confirmed as of Sep 2026 v6 launch coverage — [ProPakistani (search summary)](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/10/suno-launches-licensed-ai-music-models-after-warner-copyright-settlement/).
- Creator-side reports (secondary, not Reddit): YPP rejections for Suno channels usually trace to raw audio over a
  static image; the same audio with original visuals got approved — [Medium (search summary)](https://medium.com/illumination/can-suno-ai-music-be-monetized-on-youtube-79cd8b303b23).

### Inferences
- A rap/lyric-video Shorts channel is at real risk under "scrolling text with minimal narrative" and "templated
  storylines" if every Short is AI song + scrolling lyrics. Original visuals, the owner's own lyrics/ideas, story
  framing and varied formats are the defence (matches CLAUDE.md's inauthentic-content rules).
- Tick "AI use: Yes" on every AI-music Short; it costs nothing for monetization per YouTube's page.
- Using a licensed v6 model doesn't remove UMG/Sony litigation exposure for Suno, but there is no reported case of
  users being sued for Suno outputs (no source found either way; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No r/SunoAI or r/udiomusic threads surfaced; first-hand monetization reports are from blogs only.
- The YPP threshold change "effective 1 Feb 2027" was seen only on one blog (search summary) — unverified.
- Whether YouTube applies any AI-music-specific Shorts RPM or eligibility rule: no primary source found.

## Q4. Copyright: can AI songs with human-written lyrics be protected (US and India)?

### Takeaway
In the US, human-written lyrics are protectable; purely AI-generated music and vocals are not, and registration must
disclose and disclaim the AI parts (USCO Part 2, 29 Jan 2025). India's Copyright Act names "the person who causes the
work to be created" as author of computer-generated works, but a DPIIT committee is reviewing AI authorship; no final
position found.

### Cited Findings
- USCO AI reports: Part 1 Digital Replicas (31 Jul 2024); Part 2 Copyrightability (29 Jan 2025); Part 3 Generative AI
  Training, pre-publication (9 May 2025) — [copyright.gov/ai (opened)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/).
- Part 2: copyright protects human original expression in a work that includes AI material, not purely AI-generated
  material or material without sufficient human control; human contributions count if expressive, if they modify or
  arrange AI output, or if human content is incorporated; applicants must disclose and disclaim AI-generated elements —
  [Rimon Law (search summary)](https://www.rimonlaw.com/how-copyright-office-guidance-applies-to-music-that-includes-ai-generated-material/);
  [Library of Congress blog, May 2025 (search summary)](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2025/05/a-i-art-and-copyright-the-human-element-that-makes-all-the-difference/).
- Reported: US Supreme Court denied certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter on 2 Mar 2026 (single blog source, unverified) —
  [search summary via musicologize](https://www.musicologize.com/?p=49735).
- India: DPIIT committee formed 28 Apr 2025; Working Paper Part 1 (8 Dec 2025) proposed a hybrid/blanket licence for
  AI training with statutory remuneration (proposal, not law); Part 2 on authorship pending/unconfirmed; Copyright Act
  1957 s.2(d)(vi): author of a computer-generated work is "the person who causes the work to be created" —
  [S.S. Rana (search summary)](https://ssrana.in/articles/who-owns-what-the-machine-makes-authorship-and-ownership-of-ai-generated-works-under-indian-law/);
  [MediaNama, May 2025 (search summary)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-india-ai-copyright-law-committee/);
  [letsdatascience (search summary)](https://letsdatascience.com/news/dpiit-committee-prepares-copyrightability-working-paper-22494988).

### Inferences
- The owner's own written lyrics are his copyright in both countries (ordinary literary work). The AI melody/beat/
  vocal performance is unlikely to be protectable in the US and uncertain in India, so anyone could reuse it; another
  reason not to rely on Content ID.
- Keep drafts of lyrics with dates as authorship evidence.

### Gaps
- USCO Part 2 PDF itself not opened; conclusions come from secondary legal summaries.
- No India-specific court ruling or registration practice on AI music found; DPIIT Part 2 paper status unverified.
- The owner should verify all terms on the official pages before monetizing; this is not legal advice.
