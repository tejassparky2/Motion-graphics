# Cut words, widen melodies, hide the idea

Our songs fall short of the owner's references for three reasons, in this order. **The melody is too flat**, **the lyric says too much**, and **the vocal model is a mid-ranked one**. The pacing gap is already fixed. In this session's audio measurements, "Soft Hands" sings at about **1.4-2.8 words a second** and holds notes about **89%** of voiced time, the same as the references (about 2-3 words/s and 83-90%). Its melody, though, spans only about **8.5 semitones**, against **12-18** for the references. The prompt asked for exactly that: a "half-spoken, half-sung" voice "telling a secret" with a "simple" melody, while the chorus lines are crowded and end on short, closed vowels ("in", "been"). On the lyric side, the references build 15-30 seconds on **one hidden idea and one reversal**. Pattison's craft rules explain why that works: show an object before naming a feeling, keep a stable surface and crack it on one line, and put key words on strong beats ([Pattison](https://www.patpattison.com/rusty-s-collar)). Our drafts are full three-minute songs with many images, and the Short only gets the intro and chorus. The tool question is real but secondary. In blind listener votes, Suno ranks **#1 for vocals** and Google's Lyria 3 Pro and 3.5 rank **#7 and #9** ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-music-models-2026)), so a same-lyric A/B test of Suno Pro ($8/month, explicit commercial rights) against Gemini is worth one afternoon. Gemini/Lyria has no pronunciation or pause controls and no way to edit one section, so "cry" sung as "lie" can only be fixed by rewriting the line and re-rolling. YouTube monetizes AI music, but it demands the "AI use" disclosure and demonetizes "scrolling text with minimal or no narrative". The channel's defence is the owner's own ideas, original visuals and varied formats, not the tool ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

**What this research could and could not see.** Reddit, Medium and GitHub were blocked from this environment, so every "community" tip about Suno, Udio and Lyria comes second-hand, through blogs that quote those forums. The YouTube watch pages, TikTok sound pages, Spotify and Genius for the reference songs could not be opened, so view counts and sound-usage numbers are missing. No controlled study exists of syllable density, pronunciation or emotional expressiveness in any AI singer. The pitch and pace figures come from a rough autocorrelation pitch estimator run in this session. They show the direction of the gap, not exact values.

## The gap is melody range and idea density, not pace

The measurements narrow the problem. "Still Breathing", the first song, was a rap at about 4-5 words a second in AABB couplets, with only about 74% of voiced time on held notes. The README in `channels/rap/` measured the references at 86-90% held, so that song was simply a different genre from what the owner was pointing at (First-party). "Soft Hands" corrected the pace and the sustain, and Gemini sang every line correctly. Its remaining measurable gap is melodic: about 8.5 semitones of range against 12-18 in the references. A singer who moves across an octave and a half sounds like they are feeling something. One who stays inside two-thirds of an octave sounds like they are reciting it.

The flat melody has three likely causes, and all three are under our control (Inference). The first is the prompt. The "Soft Hands" prompt asked for a voice "half-spoken half-sung like someone telling a secret" and a chorus with "a simple haunting melody". A model asked for near-speech and simplicity will keep pitch movement small. Google's own guide treats range as something you name ("commanding baritone", "clear and high soprano range") and dynamics as something you script ("start out confident but get calmer and quieter") ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-lyria-3-pro)). The second cause is the lyric's vowels. Melodic-rap practice says the end-word of each bar is the longest-held note, so it should carry an open vowel (OH, AY, AH, EE). Closed vowels (IH, EH, UH) "cut notes short" ([RhymeFlux](https://rhymeflux.com/blog/how-to-write-melodic-rap/), Practitioner). The "Soft Hands" chorus resolves on "in" and "been", both short IH sounds, and its key line runs to about 13 syllables, above the 8-10 that a sung bar holds comfortably. The third cause is the model. Lyria 3 Pro and 3.5 rank below Suno and Mureka on blind vocal votes ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-music-models-2026)). Prompt and lyric fixes cannot fully close a gap that sits in the model.

The lyric gap is harder to measure, and it deserves a steelman. Read against the craft rules below, "Soft Hands" is not a weak lyric. It personifies one idea without naming it, opens on a reversal (expected knife, actual blanket) and shows objects (a key, tea, a dimmed lamp). A real share of "not as good" may therefore be voice and audience rather than words. One reference comes from an artist who built a TikTok and YouTube following before the song ([Songfacts](https://dev-www.songfacts.com/facts/marino/devil-in-disguise)). Where our lyrics do fall short is economy. The references deliver **one conceit with one turn in about 20 seconds**. Our drafts carry a whole arc (setup, verse, father's eyes in the mirror, escape, ominous outro) and then cut a Short from the front of it. The clip the viewer hears was never written to stand alone.

## Craft that makes a 20-second song land: one hidden face, a stable surface, one crack

**Prosody is the master rule.** Pattison reduces it to one test: every element (stress placement, line length, line count, rhyme scheme, rhyme type) must support what the line means ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4927197), Craft). Stressed syllables go on strong beats, which in 4/4 are beats 1 and 3, and the important words belong there. Content words take stress; function words take it only when they carry a contrast ([Pattison, "Perfect Match"](https://www.patpattison.com/perfect-match)). There are two ways to get it wrong. A "greedy" setting puts stressed syllables on weak beats and sounds hurried, and an "overwrought" one forces unnatural stresses and loses the listener. When "you" is set against "I", both take stress, and "you" should land on a downbeat ([Pattison, POV](https://www.patpattison.com/pov)). Structure carries feeling too. Matched line lengths, counted in stressed syllables, feel settled, while a shortened or extra line "tell[s] us to keep going" ([Pattison, "Structure Creates Expectations"](https://www.patpattison.com/structure-creates-expectations)). Rhyme types run from most to least stable: perfect, family, additive, subtractive, assonance, consonance. Perfect rhyme "slams the gate shut", and the weaker types open it toward "uncertainty and longing" ([Song Cage, quoting Pattison](https://songcage.com/blog/rhyme-scheme/)). An unrhymed line tells the ear that a match is still coming, which throws weight onto the title when it lands ([Pattison](https://www.patpattison.com/structure-creates-expectations)).

Applied to the three reference shapes, these rules suggest a design for each (Inference, not a documented analysis of those songs). **The charming-villain story** gets its menace from a polite, perfectly rhymed surface over sinister content, then one line, shortened, unrhymed or consonance-rhymed, where the mask slips. **The confession to God or to a lost person** sits mostly in unstable territory (assonance rhymes, a short last line, an unanswered question) and earns a perfect rhyme only on the line where acceptance arrives. **The struggle rap** moves from crowded, unstable verses into a sparse, perfectly rhymed hook that shuts the gate on the resolve. AI singers set every stress the melody gives them, so mis-stresses are amplified. Speak each line over a 4/4 click before generating.

**Show first, then tell once.** "You can't tell unless you show first": a dog's collar carries a whole childhood more efficiently than any statement of grief ([Pattison, "Rusty's Collar"](https://www.patpattison.com/rusty-s-collar)). Sensory detail turns listeners into participants ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4927197)). Object writing (ten minutes on one object across seven senses) and Stolpe's destination writing (one place, all senses, no rhyme) are the standard drills for finding the detail that only this story could have ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/object-writing-your-secret-to-vivid-song-lyrics.html); [Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/small-moments-write-the-song/)). Personifying evil as a polite man is show-don't-tell applied to a villain: the listener deduces the evil from a held door and a combed parting. A flat lyric would announce it. The common amateur failures are the reverse: naming emotions, stock images ("hearts that beat as one"), rhymes that bend the syntax, unanswered rhetorical questions and drifting pronouns ([Wisseloord](https://wisseloord.org/?p=33953); [BMI](https://bmi.com/news/entry/5-common-lyric-mistakesand-how-to-avoid-them), both seen as search snippets). Our drafts mostly avoid these. "The Quiet Kind" still leans on abstract statements in places ("the strong one", "I'm not okay"), and those are the lines to swap for images.

**Choose who is talking to whom, then twist with grammar.** Pattison's first question is "Who are you talking to, and why?". Direct address is the most common and most intimate point of view, a close-up rather than a middle shot ([Pattison, POV](https://www.patpattison.com/pov); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4927197)). His cheapest twist device is "productive repetition": repeat a hook fragment as a new grammatical type. Turned into a question it adds doubt; with the subject dropped it becomes a command ([Pattison, "Productive Repetition"](https://www.patpattison.com/productive-repetition)). Our final-chorus flips already do this. The upgrade is to make the flip land inside the 20-second clip, not two minutes later.

**Emotional rap trades density for space.** Melodic-rap writers budget about **8-10 syllables for a bar to be sung**, up to about 12 for rap-leaning bars. At 16 there is "no room to sustain". They write tight AABB rhyme on held notes and save multisyllabic and internal rhyme for the verses, not the hook ([RhymeFlux](https://rhymeflux.com/blog/how-to-write-melodic-rap/), Practitioner; [Musosoup](https://musosoup.com/blog/how-to-make-a-rap)). A plain line about loss can beat a clever scheme, and a bar that is clever but doesn't feel right should go ([JBZ Beats](https://jbzbeats.com/how-to-write-emotional-rap-lyrics-that-resonate-with-fans/), low authority). At 70 BPM a bar lasts about 3.4 seconds, so 8-10 syllables comes to roughly 2-3 words a second, which matches the reference measurements (Inference). Some of our lines run past the budget. One "Quiet Kind" verse line runs to about 16 syllables, and the "Soft Hands" chorus key line to about 13.

**Write the Short first.** The industry voices agree that the unit is one line that grabs "in two seconds", which is what a songwriter at Music Ally Connect 2026 said virality-chasing sessions want ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/02/13/lyrics-and-the-pressure-to-go-viral-you-have-to-get-somebody-in-two-seconds/)). Users hunt for "4-16 bars that make an immediate impression", quotable or act-out-able ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/what-music-goes-viral-tiktok/), 2019). Viral TikTok songs skew toward lyrics about "common human problems" ([Sawicka 2025](https://journals.us.edu.pl/index.php/SEIA/article/view/22879)). Berklee's songwriting chair now has students state "your main idea for the song that would normally be three minutes" and write mini songs first ([Berklee Now](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-now/news/tiktok-is-changing-the-dna-of-hit-songs-and-artists-are-taking-note)). For this channel that means drafting the 15-30-second core first: a zero-context first line, one caption-worthy line, and a turn that resolves inside the clip. Any longer song is grown outward from it. Musixmatch's Rio Caraeff adds the honest limit: audiences can tell genuine pathos from contrived work ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/02/13/lyrics-and-the-pressure-to-go-viral-you-have-to-get-somebody-in-two-seconds/)). That is one more reason the owner's own lines and opinions belong in every song.

## Gemini sings what you separate; most failures trace to the lyric sheet

**Google documents a recipe and almost nothing else.** The official prompt structure is genre and era, mood, instruments, tempo, vocal style and language, then lyrics. Exact lyrics go after the word **"Lyrics:"** ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-lyria-3-pro)). The API guide adds "Separate lyrics from instructions", recommends [Verse]/[Chorus]/[Bridge] tags, and says to put BPM, key and structure in the prompt text. It states that **"iterative editing across multiple prompts isn't supported"** and that identical prompts give different outputs ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/music-generation.md.txt), Official). The app adds a vocals on/off dropdown, a Short (~1 min) or Full (~2-3 min, "Pro") length, and the advice to keep supplied lyrics short ([Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16901237); [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/tips-prompting-lyria-3/)). Google documents no negative prompts, no phonetic spelling, no pause control and no section repair.

**Instrumental-only output is a parsing failure, and our own A/B confirms it.** The only documented way to get no vocals is to ask for "instrumental" or switch vocals off ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-lyria-3-pro)). An unexpected instrumental therefore points to one of four things (Inference): the dropdown was off, beat or lo-fi wording read as instrumental, the lyrics were not marked off with "Lyrics:", or the tool was built on Lyria RealTime, which "generates instrumental music only" ([Lyria RealTime docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/realtime-music-generation)). The channel's history fits the third explanation. The failed "Quiet Kind" sheet put stage directions inside its section tags ("spoken softly, no beat", "beat drops out, piano only, almost whispered"). The "Soft Hands" prompt opened "Create a song WITH VOCALS. A soft, low male voice sings these exact lyrics", used plain tags and a "Lyrics:" marker, and Gemini sang every line (First-party). **Keep the "Soft Hands" frame as the template.** Put every performance direction in the description above "Lyrics:" and nothing but words and plain tags below it.

**"Cry" became "lie" because the line made it easy.** Lyria offers no pronunciation control (Official, by omission). In "The Quiet Kind" the word sat at the end of a verse built on one long-I rhyme chain (hi, why, time, night, fine, fire, cry), in a song about pretending to be fine. On a long held note the vowel carries the word and the short opening consonants shrink, and "lie" shares the vowel and fits the theme (Inference). The practitioner fixes come from one Suno pronunciation guide and are untested on Lyria. Give the at-risk word its own short phrase, followed by a comma or full stop, and shorten the lyric around any word that gets rushed. Respell a word only after it fails, keep that spelling everywhere, and change one thing per test. Write acronyms and numbers as they are spoken ("A-I", "twenty twenty-six") ([Jack Righteous](https://jackrighteous.com/de-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-v5-multilingual-english-pronunciation-guide), Practitioner). To that, add keeping near-homophones out of the same rhyme slot, so "cry" never sits beside "lie" or "my" (Inference). On Gemini the remaining fix is to re-roll several takes and run Whisper medium on each. That check catches the swap, and the channel already applies it to narration.

**Rushed pauses come from word count, not from punctuation alone.** Practitioner guidance for Suno says short lines produce "a more restrained, intimate delivery", and ellipses or broken sentences make the model "pause and hesitate naturally" ([AceTagGen](https://acetaggen.com/blog/vocal-trick-sheet-make-suno-sing-like-you-imagine)). A second-hand r/SunoAI roundup puts the useful ceiling at about 100-120 words of lyrics, past which parts get treated as optional ([Medium roundup](https://medium.com/@aitooldiscovery/suno-reddit-tips-and-prompts-what-r-sunoai-users-actually-do-65b59d51bcad), unverified). Google's own "keep them short" points the same way. A 20-second Short at 2-3 words a second holds **40-60 words**. Anything beyond that forces the model to choose between rushing and dropping words (Inference).

**Generic delivery comes from diluted direction.** Practitioners report that one tone word works and three "dilute the signal" ([AceTagGen](https://acetaggen.com/blog/vocal-trick-sheet-make-suno-sing-like-you-imagine)). "Male vocals" alone is not enough; stack two or three texture words instead ([Medium roundup](https://medium.com/@aitooldiscovery/suno-reddit-tips-and-prompts-what-r-sunoai-users-actually-do-65b59d51bcad), unverified). Google's own examples name sex, range, texture and a dynamic arc ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-lyria-3-pro)). A mixing guide adds that AI vocals come out "loud and even, with the life squeezed out of them" and "dry and detached". Its fixes are a WAV export, gentle cleanup, moderate compression only (2:1-4:1) and shared reverb ([Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/mixing-ai-generated-stems-2026)). No source documents how to widen an AI singer's melodic range. The best available levers, untested (Inference), are three. Ask for a fully sung voice with a named range ("warm baritone that rises into a strained high note"). Script the arc ("verse low and close; the chorus climbs higher on its last line"). And give the melody open vowels on held ends to climb on.

**Suno and Udio give more control at a price.** Suno's official Exclude field is "guidance" ([Suno Help](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/3161921)). Practitioners rate section tags such as [whispered] ("one of the most reliable") and [spoken word] as dependable ([AceTagGen](https://acetaggen.com/blog/vocal-trick-sheet-make-suno-sing-like-you-imagine)). The decisive difference is **Replace Section**: it re-sings one bad word's region without regenerating the song, whereas "changing displayed lyrics alone does not rewrite existing audio" ([Jack Righteous](https://jackrighteous.com/de-gb/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-v5-multilingual-english-pronunciation-guide)). Udio's manual mode passes prompts through unchanged but accepts tags only ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/how-to-create-realistic-songs-with-udio-ai/), unverified). Udio's downloads have been disabled since October 2025, so it is out regardless (next section).

A prompt in the shape the evidence supports, untested, for the next Gemini or Suno take:

> Create a song WITH VOCALS. A warm male baritone sings these exact lyrics, clearly, every line. Emotional melodic rap ballad, 68 BPM, half-time, minor key. Sparse felt piano, soft sub bass, quiet lo-fi drums, lots of space between lines. The voice is fully sung, tender and tired; the verse stays low and close-mic, and the chorus climbs noticeably higher on its last line, with soft backing vocals there. Singing in English.
> Lyrics:
> [Verse] (4 short lines, 8-10 syllables each, key word last)
> [Chorus] (identical every time; held ends on open vowels)

Disclosure "doesn't limit a video's audience or affect monetization eligibility", while repeated non-disclosure risks removal ([YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Content ID requires "exclusive rights to a substantial body of original material" ([YouTube Help 2797370](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370)). Both Suno and Google say they may produce similar output for others, so registering these songs risks claims against other creators or against our own Shorts. Monetizing the Shorts does not need Content ID (Inference).

Copyright has one wrinkle the notes did not anticipate. The research assumed the owner writes the lyrics, but on this channel **Claude drafts them**. In the US, protection covers human expression; purely AI-generated material is excluded, and AI elements must be disclosed and disclaimed when registering ([Rimon Law summary of USCO Part 2](https://www.rimonlaw.com/how-copyright-office-guidance-applies-to-music-that-includes-ai-generated-material/)). India's Copyright Act names "the person who causes the work to be created" as the author of a computer-generated work. A DPIIT committee is reviewing AI authorship, with no final position yet ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/who-owns-what-the-machine-makes-authorship-and-ownership-of-ai-generated-works-under-indian-law/), search summary). The practical consequence lines up with the monetization defence: the more lines, ideas and edits come from the owner, the stronger both the authorship claim and the "not mass-produced" case. Keeping dated drafts of the owner's changes is cheap evidence (Inference; not legal advice).

## Conclusion

The references are not better produced than our songs. They are **narrower**: one hidden idea, one reversal, one voice that moves a long way in pitch over very few words. Our pipeline already matches them on the easy measures (pace, sustain, correct words with the "Soft Hands" frame). It loses on the two things that carry feeling: melodic movement and the economy of a single conceit written to fit 20 seconds. Both are fixable in the lyric sheet and the prompt before any money is spent. The tool switch is a measured bet to run after those fixes, not a substitute for them, and Suno's Replace Section matters as much as its ranking because it turns a "cry"/"lie" failure into one cheap repair.

There is also a strategic point. The emotional power of these songs and the channel's monetization defence come from the same place. The references spread because a listener can tell that a person meant every word. YouTube pays creators whose videos show their own narrative and ideas. Lines and opinions from the owner are therefore not decoration but the product. Each song should carry at least one line that only the owner would have written.

## Revision checklist for the next song

- [ ] **One idea.** The conceit fits in one sentence, and the song never names it outright (the listener works it out).
- [ ] **Opening.** Line 1 is a quote, a confession or a contradiction a stranger understands with zero context.
- [ ] **The turn.** One negation-then-reveal ("you'd think X / it's Y") lands inside the first 20 seconds.
- [ ] **Show first.** Every emotion word (sad, broken, pain, lost, alone) is replaced by an object, place or action; at most one plain feeling statement, placed after an image.
- [ ] **No borrowed images.** No image heard in another song (tears like rain, fighting demons, chasing dreams).
- [ ] **Point of view locked.** One speaker, one addressee, one reason for speaking; pronouns never drift.
- [ ] **Syllable budget.** Sung lines 8-10 syllables, rap-leaning lines at most 12; the 20-second core is 40-60 words.
- [ ] **Stress check.** Spoken over a 4/4 click, key words and the title land on beats 1 or 3, with no function word on a downbeat unless it carries a contrast.
- [ ] **Open held vowels.** Chorus line-ends use AY, OH, AH, EE or long I; no closed IH/EH/UH on a held note.
- [ ] **Rhyme matches feeling.** Assonance or family rhyme where it aches, perfect rhyme only where it resolves, one unrhymed line before the title.
- [ ] **Misheard words.** Each rhyme-critical word has its own phrase and punctuation, and no near-homophone shares its rhyme slot (cry/lie/my).
- [ ] **Repetition with a flip.** The hook comes back once changed (question, command, or swapped pronoun), and that flip is inside the clip.
- [ ] **Owner's voice.** At least one line or idea comes from the owner, kept in their words.
- [ ] **Prompt hygiene.** Starts "Create a song WITH VOCALS"; sex, range, texture, one emotion and a dynamic arc are set above "Lyrics:"; only plain [Verse]/[Chorus] tags below it; no artist names.
- [ ] **Takes and checks.** At least 4 takes; Whisper medium confirms every word; range ≥12 semitones, 2-3 words/s, ≥83% held notes.
- [ ] **Publishing.** "AI use" disclosed, no Content ID, watermark untouched, visuals original, and the format varied from the previous video.
