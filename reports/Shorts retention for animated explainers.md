# Win the swipe, then never stop talking

For a 2D animated finance-parable Short with voiceover, retention depends on two things, and they are measured separately. The first is the swipe decision. YouTube scores it with an official "viewed vs. swiped away" metric, and Instagram with a first-3-second "skip rate". Frame 1 and the first spoken line decide it. The second is how the body holds engaged viewers: continuous, energetic narration, a visual change about every second, captions and on-screen numbers that repeat what the voice says, and a but/therefore story that holds back its payoff and ends the moment the payoff lands. The strongest evidence available is official metric definitions, one disclosed 5,400-Short dataset (Galloway, 2023), ad-creative research from Meta and TikTok, and academic work on speech rate, cutting pace, subtitles and curiosity. Almost every hard threshold that circulates ("70% viewed or you're dead", "cut every 2.5 s", "captions +25% retention", "decide in 1.7 s") is either anecdotal, from the ad era, or untraceable. Nothing public studies 2D animated or finance Shorts specifically, so every application to this video is an inference and is labelled as one. The reference Short the user admires was measured in this session (a first-party measurement, not a web source). It speaks at roughly **258–276 words per minute with essentially zero dead air**, and it changes something on screen about **every 0.92 s**. The current Pumpkin Trick render speaks at **about 150–165 wpm, with gaps of up to 4.2 s and speech covering only about 70% of its 82 s**. The production targets at the end close that gap without copying numbers that don't carry over from Hindi to English TTS.

**Evidence labels used throughout:**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[Official]** | Platform documentation or statements by platform staff |
| **[Data]** | A dataset or controlled study with a stated method: platform ad research, disclosed creator datasets, or academic studies |
| **[Anecdotal]** | Creator advice, vendor blogs, tool defaults, or numbers with no disclosed method |
| **[First-party]** | Measured in this session |
| **[Inference]** | This report's reasoning from the evidence |

Guidelines are numbered G1–G34 so the production targets can point back to them.

## The swipe decision is scored separately from everything after it

YouTube's Shorts analytics split the viewing funnel in two. The "How many chose to view" card reports "the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)). Separately, **average percentage viewed is defined as the "average percentage of a video watched among those who stayed to watch"**, and engaged views count viewers who "stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)). So the hook shows up in viewed-vs-swiped, and APV measures only the body. Instagram draws the same line with its Reels **skip rate, "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds,"** alongside a retention graph where "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)). Since March 31, 2025, a public Shorts "view" counts every start or replay with no minimum watch time. Engaged views still govern YPP and revenue share ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)).

**YouTube publishes no viewed-vs-swiped target.** The only disclosed dataset behind the popular benchmarks is Paddy Galloway and Chris Gileta's April 2023 analysis of **5,400 Shorts from 33 channels (3.3B views)**. Top performers sat at **70–90% viewed**, and Shorts **below 60% "typically did not perform well"** ([BAM summary](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315)). The same "3.3 billion views" numbers also circulate through a Medium write-up that could not be opened ([Medium](https://medium.com/@antoinelacombled/cracking-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-study-of-3-3-billion-views-4711fdf7931b)), and a "5,400 Shorts" study credited to "Inflow Network" ([Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)) is very likely the same dataset under a different name. It is correlational and predates the 2025 view-count change.

Hard evidence on the first seconds comes from advertising. Facebook IQ's 2016 analysis of 850+ video ads found people spend **1.7 s per piece of mobile feed content**, and that content can be recalled after a quarter-second of exposure ([Facebook IQ](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative)). TikTok's auction-ads research reports that **"over 63% of all videos with the highest click-through rate highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds."** In the same research, text overlays appear in 40% and direct address ("breaking the 4th wall") in 33% of top view-through ads ([TikTok for Business PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)). No public study compares hook *types* (question vs. bold claim vs. mid-action) on organic Shorts.

**Guidelines: hook and first 1–3 seconds**

1. **G1.** Diagnose the two halves separately. A low viewed-vs-swiped rate (YouTube) or a high skip rate (Instagram) points to frame 1 and line 1. A high viewed rate with a falling curve points to pacing or the middle. **[Official]** metric definitions ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)); the diagnostic split is **[Inference]**.
2. **G2.** Benchmark viewed-vs-swiped against your own channel median. If you want an external bar, treat ≥60% as a floor and 70–90% as "top performer" territory from one 2023 dataset. **[Data]**, correlational ([BAM/Galloway](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). There is no official threshold.
3. **G3.** Put the key message or stakes on screen and in the voiceover within the first 3 seconds. **[Data]** from ads ([TikTok PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)); carrying it over to organic Shorts is **[Inference]**.
4. **G4.** Frame 1 is the thumbnail. It must already be moving and already show the stakes: no logo sting, no title card, no "hey guys". **[Anecdotal]**: Galloway's "treat the first second like a thumbnail" ([BAM](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)); ClipFlip's claim that "the first frame is already interesting" is the top differentiator across its 44,000 self-selected clips ([ClipFlip](https://www.clipflip.io/resources/how-to-make-viral-clips)). This is consistent with Meta's quarter-second recall finding **[Data]**.
5. **G5.** Phrase the hook as a question or an expectation-break, not a statement. Hoyos: "think about it as a question rather than just making a statement"; "start with the setting your viewers already expect… then change one thing" ([KDCC summary](https://blog.kdcc.social/make-anything-go-viral-how-to-hook-and-reel-in-your-viewer/)). **[Anecdotal]**, backed by the **[Data]** information-gap literature in the story section.
6. **G6.** Use direct address ("you") or a character who looks at camera, and back the spoken hook with a short on-screen text line. **[Data]** from ads (33% of top view-through ads break the 4th wall; 40% use text overlays) ([TikTok PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)).
7. **G7.** Don't separate the viewer from the hook with throat-clearing ("let's get started", "I'm not going to waste your time"). **[Anecdotal]** ([Jay Clouse on Hoyos](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jayclouse_jenny-hoyos-gave-me-her-exact-script-that-activity-7262878750684459010-NIZu); [MoneyPrinterTurbo prompt](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo/blob/main/app/services/llm.py)).

## Fast, continuous narration outperforms the "natural" 150 wpm read

The best measured evidence on speaking rate comes from lectures, not Shorts, but it points one way. Guo, Kim and Rubin's edX study of **6.9M sessions across 862 videos** found that "engagement usually increases (up to 2x) with speaking rate." The **fastest quintile (185–254 wpm) did best, and the 145–165 wpm band did worst**, because those speakers "were the least energetic." Viewers followed even 254 wpm easily "since the same information was also presented visually" ([Guo et al. 2014](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)). The authors warn that rate is "a surface feature that correlates with enthusiasm," so a fast but monotone TTS voice won't automatically reproduce the effect.

On comprehension, audio-only compressed speech holds up to about **250–275 wpm** and then declines rapidly ([Foulke & Sticht via ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED066080.pdf)). With slides on screen, Murphy et al. (n = 231) found **no significant comprehension loss at 1.5x (210–234 wpm) or 2x (314–332 wpm)**. The only significant loss was at 2.5x (393–416 wpm), relative to 1x ([Murphy et al. 2022](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)). Voiceover-industry norms put explainers near 160 wpm and commercials near 180 wpm ([Debbie Grattan VO](https://www.debbiegrattan.com/blog/how-to-write-a-voice-over-script/)). Short-form "WPM targets" online contradict each other (140–160 wpm vs. 170–180 wpm) and show no measurements ([FlowShorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/words-per-minute-speaking); [ScriptTimer](https://blog.scripttimer.io/average-words-per-minute/)).

No study measures inter-sentence gaps against Shorts retention. Tool defaults set the working range: auto-editor keeps **0.2 s** of padding around speech ([auto-editor](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor/blob/master/README.md)), a "YouTube hard-cut" style uses **100–150 ms** and 0 ms "will feel choppy" ([VidPickr](https://vidpickr.com/blog/remove-silence-from-youtube-videos-2026)), and RedditVideoMakerBot inserts **0.3 s** between TTS segments ([config](https://github.com/elebumm/RedditVideoMakerBot/blob/master/utils/.config.template.toml)). The counterpoint is that editors keep a breath-length pause because "it's during the breaths that people assimilate what was in the previous sentence" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/audition-discussions/how-do-i-remove-breaths-from-long-recordings-to-save-time/m-p/10982965)). There is no independent evidence that TTS as such hurts retention. The claims that robotic TTS loses viewers (e.g., "up to 15% higher retention" with expressive voices) come from TTS vendors with no methodology ([Supertone](https://www.supertone.ai/en/work/top-tts-voices-for-youtube-shorts)).

**Guidelines: narration rate and dead air**

8. **G8.** Narrate at roughly 180–230 wpm (English) while speaking, well above the 145–165 wpm "natural" band. **[Data]** from lecture video ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf); [Murphy](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)); the exact band for Shorts is **[Inference]**.
9. **G9.** Go above about 230–250 wpm only when the same information is on screen. Stay clear of the ~275 wpm audio-only comprehension cliff for anything conveyed by voice alone. **[Data]** ([Foulke & Sticht](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED066080.pdf); [Murphy](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)).
10. **G10.** Energy matters more than raw speed. Punctuate the script so TTS phrasing rises and falls, because "a script without punctuation will sound like a breathless, robotic wall of text" ([Narration Box](https://narrationbox.com/blog/why-ai-voice-sounds-robotic-on-youtube)). **[Anecdotal]** (vendor). The underlying rate-is-a-proxy-for-enthusiasm point is **[Data]** ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)).
11. **G11.** Keep line-to-line gaps at about 100–250 ms. Treat 0 ms as choppy and anything over ~0.4 s as dead air. **[Anecdotal]**: tool defaults ([auto-editor](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor/blob/master/README.md); [VidPickr](https://vidpickr.com/blog/remove-silence-from-youtube-videos-2026); [RedditVideoMakerBot](https://github.com/elebumm/RedditVideoMakerBot/blob/master/utils/.config.template.toml)).
12. **G12.** Any deliberate pause must carry visual action: a number counting, a reveal, a time-skip transition. Then the audio gap is never visual dead air. **[Inference]**.
13. **G13.** Set speed at synthesis time rather than time-stretching the finished file, and verify the result by transcribing it with word timestamps (words ÷ speaking time, the same method Guo used). **[Inference]**. Pipelines leave TTS at 1.0x by default and expose speed as a setting **[Anecdotal]** ([MoneyPrinterTurbo](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo/blob/main/config.example.toml)).

## A visual event every one to three seconds, carried by motion rather than cuts

The film evidence is solid, but it is film. Cutting et al. measured Hollywood average shot length falling **from about 10 s in the 1930s–40s to under 4 s after 2000**, while motion inside shots rose (Visual Activity Index r = .583; Toy Story 3 scored 0.122 vs. Barry Lyndon's 0.008). Their conclusion was that shorter shots "demand a reorientation of visual attention" ([Cutting et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/)). Annie Lang's work shows cuts and edits trigger involuntary orienting responses ([Lang 1990](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/009365090017003001)). The same research line has a warning for explainers: **memory for non-arousing content peaks at medium pacing and declines at high pacing, and fast pace combined with arousing content overloads processing** ([Lang et al. 1999](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08838159909364504)). A finance parable asks viewers to hold onto prices, so relentless hard cuts work against it.

The Shorts-specific numbers are weak. The claim that "2.5 s clips correlated with 35% higher completion" gives no method ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/video-clip-length/)). ClipFlip's "fast-paced clips outperform slow ones by 2.4x" comes from self-selected users of the tool, and it doesn't define "fast" ([ClipFlip](https://www.clipflip.io/resources/how-to-make-viral-clips)). TikTok officially says only that "faster scene changes typically draw viewers in early" ([TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)). Experienced editors push back on the "cut faster, add more elements" doctrine as "confusing fatigue for finesse" ([George Blackman](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/rr11-were-all-trapped?open=false)). "Pattern interrupt" is a creator term with no academic study behind it. The closest academic construct is Lang's orienting response.

**Guidelines: visual pacing and pattern interrupts**

14. **G14.** Change something on screen every ~1–3 s. **[Anecdotal]** as a number. It is consistent in direction with **[Data]** from film shot-length and orienting-response research ([Cutting](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/); [Lang](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/009365090017003001)).
15. **G15.** In 2D, make most changes *inside* a held set rather than hard cuts: camera scale steps (100%→110–120%), a character entering, a pose change, a number write-on, a prop. This matches Cutting's finding that modern short-shot editing carries more motion per shot **[Data]**. The technique list is **[Inference]**.
16. **G16.** Never hold a fully static frame. Line boil, idle breathing and blinking, and a slow camera drift make a hold count as "alive". **[Inference]** from the same motion data; craft basis in secondary animation ([School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/secondary-animation)).
17. **G17.** Keep every key number on screen long enough to read (≥1 s). Don't stack a burst of cuts on an already emotional beat (the price crash, the betrayal). **[Data]**: Lang's overload effect ([Lang et al. 1999](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08838159909364504)); Szarkowska's ~1 s subtitle reading time ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0306251)). The ≥1 s rule is **[Inference]**.
18. **G18.** Make the number itself the visual event: pop it in with overshoot and settle, timed to the spoken word. TikTok reports that "creative attributes that get people to read increase view time" ([TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)). **[Official]** (ads); applying it to number pops is **[Inference]**.

## Captions are now table stakes; the kinetic-style lift is unproven

The famous caption statistics date from the sound-off feed era. Meta's internal tests found captioned video ads **increased view time by an average of 12%** ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads)). The 2019 Verizon/Publicis survey (5,616 adults) found 69% watch with sound off in public and 80% say captions make them more likely to finish a video. That is self-report, not measured retention ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)). Vertical feeds today are largely sound-on: **88% of TikTok users say sound is vital** to the experience ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)).

Captions still help, as a second channel for attention and comprehension. In Szarkowska et al. (2024, n = 161), viewers kept reading subtitles with the sound on, and comprehension edged up (81.3% vs. 77.7%) ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0306251)). Subtitle reading also correlates with comprehension ([Kruger & Steyn 2014](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rrq.59)). Animated captions are close to universal: across **13.5M clips, 78.6% used animated captions vs. ~1.6% static**, but that is adoption data with no retention figure ([OpusClip research](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-caption-strategy-short-form)). No independent study shows word-by-word captions beating phrase captions. One platform-specific risk is official: Instagram reduces distribution of reels that are "majority text" ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained/)).

**Guidelines: on-screen text and kinetic captions**

19. **G19.** Burn in captions for the full narration. Their proven value is comprehension and insurance for muted starts, not a proven retention lift on sound-on feeds. **[Data]** ([Szarkowska](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0306251); [Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads), ad-era).
20. **G20.** Show 1–3 words per chunk on one line, highlight the current word, force breaks at punctuation, and time chunks from word-level timestamps rather than proportional estimates. **[Anecdotal]**: repo defaults ([captacity](https://github.com/unconv/captacity/blob/master/README.md); [Captions](https://github.com/kaushal07wick/Captions); [MoneyPrinterTurbo subtitle.py](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo/blob/main/app/services/subtitle.py)). Short chunks suit fast narration **[Inference]**.
21. **G21.** Show prices as large, separately animated graphics rather than inside the caption strip, and don't let text cover most of the frame. **[Official]**: Instagram's "majority text" demotion ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained/)). The layout is **[Inference]**.
22. **G22.** Keep captions and key elements inside the platform safe zones. Meta asks for roughly 14% top, 35% bottom and 6% sides clear; YouTube guidance is reported as top 10%, bottom 25% and right 10% ([behaviour.digital](https://behaviour.digital/post/meta-reels-safe-zone-14-top-35-bottom-6-sides-the-2026-official-guide); [Strike Social](https://strikesocial.com/blog/maximize-ad-visibility-and-cut-through-the-noise-with-safe-zone-guides/)). **[Official via secondary]**. A band roughly 55–62% down the frame satisfies all three platforms **[Inference]**.

## Duck the music hard and spend sound effects on turning points

Sound matters on TikTok by the platform's own research. In TikTok-commissioned Kantar work, **88% say sound is essential** and 73% say they would "stop and look" at ads with audio ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-insights-into-the-importance-of-sound-for-marketing-promo/601569/)). TikTok's ad research also links **tracks above 120 BPM to higher view-through** ([TikTok PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)). Google reports that sound in Shorts ads raises conversions by over 20% ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en)). The mixing numbers are craft consensus, not experiments: **voice near −14 LUFS, music 18–25 dB under the speech, ducking 15–25 dB with a 10–30 ms attack and a 200–500 ms release**. "If you notice the music while the person talks, it's too loud" ([Pure Audio Insight](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/background-music-volume-how-loud-should-it-be); [Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/music-ducking-explained/); [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-ducking)). For sound effects, editors warn that "a sound effect on every single cut starts to feel chaotic," and reserve them for the 3–5 most important beats ([EseCut](https://esecut.com/blog/sound-effects-that-boost-engagement)).

**Guidelines: sound design and music under voice**

23. **G23.** Normalize the voice to about −14 LUFS, and keep music about 20–25 dB below it while the narrator speaks, with sidechain ducking at ~20 ms attack and ~300 ms release. **[Anecdotal]** (mix guides).
24. **G24.** With continuous narration, the music bed is almost always ducked, so its energy has to come from tempo and rhythm, not volume. Favor a bed at or above 120 BPM. **[Data]** from ads for the BPM link ([TikTok PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)); the continuous-narration reasoning is **[Inference]**.
25. **G25.** Keep small, quiet pops for number write-ons. Give full-weight hits (whoosh, impact, ka-ching) only to the 3–5 plot turns. **[Anecdotal]** ([EseCut](https://esecut.com/blog/sound-effects-that-boost-engagement)).
26. **G26.** For a voiceover explainer, the voice *is* the audio. Trending-sound mechanics apply to music-led content. No data shows that a trending track under a voiceover helps. **[Inference]**.

## Promise the payoff early, reveal it late, and cut right after

The most transferable science is on curiosity. Loewenstein defines curiosity as "a form of cognitively induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge" ([Loewenstein 1994](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232440476_The_Psychology_of_Curiosity_A_Review_and_Reinterpretation)). Kang et al. found curiosity is **highest at moderate confidence (an inverted U)**, and that it predicted better recall of surprising answers about 10 days later ([Kang et al. 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x)). Gruber et al. showed that high-curiosity states also improve memory for *incidental* material encountered while waiting for the answer ([Gruber et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25284006/)). Green and Brock found narrative "transportation" increases story-consistent beliefs ([Green & Brock 2000](http://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/the_role_of_transportation_in_the_persuasiveness_of_public_narratives.pdf)). That matters for a parable meant to change how viewers read price spikes. None of these studies tests Shorts retention directly.

Creator craft converges on the same shape. Parker and Stone's rule is that beats joined by "and then" mean "you're in trouble"; use "but" or "therefore" instead ([Aerogramme](https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2014/03/06/writing-advice-from-south-parks-trey-parker-and-matt-stone/)). Hoyos applies it to Shorts: short hook, foreshadow, smooth transitions, but/therefore ([Jay Clouse](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jayclouse_jenny-hoyos-gave-me-her-exact-script-that-activity-7262878750684459010-NIZu)). She also uses visible progress markers so "the viewer can always see how close they are to the payoff" ([KDCC](https://blog.kdcc.social/make-anything-go-viral-how-to-hook-and-reel-in-your-viewer/)). Blackman withholds "the specific payoff only at the end" ([Write With AI](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/write-a-killer-youtube-script-like)), and notes that viewers leave once "they have seen the main payoff and don't have anything they need to stick around for" ([Blackman RR#2](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core)).

On length, YouTube allows Shorts up to 3 minutes since October 15, 2024 ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)). Staff frame it as "what length do I need to tell my story?" (Sherman, secondary quote) and "as long as it needs to be, but no longer" (Ritchie, secondary quote) ([Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)). In Galloway's dataset, **50–60 s Shorts performed best, and Shorts with average view duration above 50 s averaged 4.1M views vs. 1.8M for 40–50 s** ([BAM](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). An Adobe survey of 507 creators leaned the other way: 53% named "under 30 s" best for views. That result is self-reported ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study)). Loops are mechanically real: engaged views exclude loops but watch time does not, so APV can pass 100%. One creator reported **103.9% APV while still stuck near 10k views**, so rewatch alone doesn't guarantee reach ([YouTube Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/296504058/i-have-average-percentage-viewed-103-9-on-my-one-short-but-still-i-get-views-no-more-than-10k-and)).

**Guidelines: story structure**

27. **G27.** Open a gap on something the viewer partly knows (market prices, a "too good" offer), not something wholly unfamiliar or fully understood. **[Data]** ([Kang](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x)).
28. **G28.** Join every beat with "but" or "therefore". Cut "and then", "the next day" and other list-like transitions, or make them causal. **[Anecdotal]** ([Parker & Stone](https://www.aerogrammestudio.com/2014/03/06/writing-advice-from-south-parks-trey-parker-and-matt-stone/); [Hoyos](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jayclouse_jenny-hoyos-gave-me-her-exact-script-that-activity-7262878750684459010-NIZu)).
29. **G29.** Foreshadow the payoff in the hook, hide its value until the last few seconds, and place the teaching content inside the open loop. **[Anecdotal]** ([Blackman](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/write-a-killer-youtube-script-like)), backed by **[Data]** on incidental memory during curiosity ([Gruber](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25284006/)).
30. **G30.** Open a second loop before closing the first, and give the middle a visible progress marker (such as a price ladder that keeps climbing). **[Anecdotal]** ([KDCC/Hoyos](https://blog.kdcc.social/make-anything-go-viral-how-to-hook-and-reel-in-your-viewer/)).

**Guidelines: length and ending**

31. **G31.** Make it as long as the story needs, then cut every second that doesn't advance it. For a single parable, 45–60 s is a defensible band. **[Official]** for the principle (secondary quotes); **[Data]**, correlational, for 50–60 s ([BAM/Galloway](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)); the band is **[Inference]**.
32. **G32.** End right after the payoff: no spoken "subscribe", no moral that runs on, and put any call to action in the description. **[Anecdotal]** ([Blackman](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core); [ShortGPT prompt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RayVentura/ShortGPT/stable/shortGPT/prompt_templates/reddit_generate_script.yaml); [PrePublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention)).
33. **G33.** Make the last frame and line lead back into the first, so a replay feels seamless. This raises APV mechanically **[Official]** by the metric definition ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)); any effect on reach is unproven.
34. **G34.** On Instagram, design for sends: the ranking signals Mosseri names are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, with sends "slightly more important" for reaching non-followers ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). **[Official]**. A single quotable takeaway invites sends **[Inference]**.

## The reference Short talks nonstop and changes the picture every 0.92 s

This measurement was made in this session **[First-party]**. It is not a web source. Speech was transcribed with faster-whisper ("small" model, word timestamps). Visual change came from ffmpeg scene scores: hard cuts at a score >0.25, and "visual change events" at a score >0.03 sampled at 10 fps, with events closer than 0.35 s merged. The reference, "How the Rich fools you" (80.2 s, Hindi/Hinglish), runs **258–276 wpm in every 10-second window from 0 to 50 s**, 240 wpm at 50–60 s, and 198 wpm during the talking-head outro. Speech effectively ends at about 70 s. **The median gap between words is 0.0 s, and only one gap in the whole video reaches ≥0.4 s** (1.38 s at 47.3 s). It has **87 visual change events in 80 s**, and almost every stretch without change is under 2.5 s (the longest is 5.3 s). It has only 21 hard cuts. Most change is animation and text inside a held set. Handwritten prices and profit maths pop on screen as they are spoken, a dim transition marks "next day", and the creator cuts back on camera for commentary and the ending.

Two caveats limit how directly these numbers carry over. Whisper's Hindi word counts are approximate (±10%). Hindi tokenization also splits out short function words, so **260 Hindi "wpm" is not the same load as 260 English wpm** from an English TTS voice **[Inference]**. The current Pumpkin Trick render (v2) is 82.1 s with 16 narration lines at roughly 150–165 wpm while speaking. It has gaps of 1.4–4.2 s between lines (for example 3.7 s at 39.6 s and 4.2 s at 55.3 s), and speech covers about 70% of the runtime **[First-party]**. That puts its narration in the exact band Guo found least engaging, with the dead air the reference avoids. Its script is 201 English words (`build/tts/lines.txt`), and several lines contain ellipses ("He wants to buy... pumpkins!", "Days pass...") that TTS renders as extra pauses **[First-party]**.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes "fast-paced with no pauses" from a style preference into a specific fix. The current render doesn't fail because it is animated or because the voice is synthetic. It fails because it sits in the measured "least energetic" speech band and leaves roughly a quarter of its runtime silent, while the reference fills every second with speech and a visual event. The main risk in pushing speed is not viewer confusion, since comprehension holds to about 2x when visuals carry the numbers. The risk is a flat TTS delivery that sounds fast without sounding energetic, and a price-heavy script overloading viewers if cuts pile onto the emotional beats. That is why the targets pair speed with on-word number graphics and motion inside held shots, not a cut every second.

The deeper gap in the evidence is that nobody has published retention data for animated or finance Shorts. The most reliable benchmark this project will ever have is its own A/B history. Rendering the video from code makes that cheap: two hook variants, or two pace settings, differ by a single parameter. Uploading variants over time and reading viewed-vs-swiped and APV separately will produce better evidence for this format than anything currently in circulation.
