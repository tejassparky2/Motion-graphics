# Lock the look and vary every night

The best animation this CPU-only pipeline can deliver is a deliberately limited, locked style, not a chase for realism. That means big-headed toon characters on Quaternius's free UAL2 skeleton that breathe, blink, look around and lip-sync on every frame, inside dark, fogged, lightly dithered "PS1-lite" sets graded by one fixed LUT. A second "recorded" register (CCTV, phone and intercom feeds with a VHS treatment) is kept for in-world messages. The evidence behind that choice is thin but points one way. In the one direct film study, semi-realistic characters were rated eeriest. Motion and sound decide how frightening a character reads. Low-budget horror's visible hits (Local 58, Kane Pixels, Haunted PS1) made their limits into the look. And no verifiable stylized-3D, second-person horror Shorts channel was found, so the lane is open but unproven. The cheapest big technical win is speed. One production report measured a three.js scene rendering about **4x faster on Mesa llvmpipe than on SwiftShader**, and this container already has llvmpipe, LLVM 20 and Xvfb, so the ~40-minute render per episode can shrink and the saved time can pay for ambient occlusion and grading. For stories, the best-supported hook drops "you" into an everyday place where one fact is already impossible, shown as big text with sound on frame 0. The best-supported spine has five parts: a rule stated in a flat official voice, a trusted channel that contradicts it, an undescribed "you", a planted twist that answers the hook, and a final binary built as a true dilemma and pre-tested toward a 50-50 split. YouTube's policies explicitly allow asking for comments. The real risks are three: a repeated "same situation, same outcome" template; a cartoon look that gets episodes classed as made for kids, which switches comments off and kills the vote; and any sound an average listener could mistake for an emergency-alert tone. No source measures second-person openings, HUD overlays or comment-decided serials on Shorts, so the channel's own logged numbers will become the best evidence for its format.

## A 4x faster rasterizer and living characters lead the upgrade list

Today the pipeline renders three.js r169 frame by frame in chrome-headless-shell with `--use-angle=swiftshader` and reads each frame back with `canvas.toDataURL`. It runs half-resolution bloom, PCF soft shadows and ACES tone mapping at about one frame per second, so an 80-second episode's 2,400 frames take roughly 40 minutes (`channels/horror/render.mjs`, `channels/horror/scene_horror.html`; Local). The largest documented CPU-only lever is not a new engine but a different software rasterizer. Microlink ran GPU-less 8-core Intel nodes at 2.1 GHz and timed three.js's `webgl_animation_skinning_blending` example at **about 24 s on SwiftShader against about 6 s on Mesa llvmpipe in isolation (~4x), and 7-14 s under load (~2x)**. The switch was four changes: drop `--use-angle=swiftshader`, add `--use-angle=gl`, set `LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1`, and start Xvfb before Chrome. Without an X display, WebGL silently fell back to flat 2D ([Microlink](https://microlink.io/blog/webgl-without-a-gpu); Report). This container already has Mesa 25.2.8's llvmpipe, LLVM 20, Xvfb and AVX-512 on four Xeon cores (`/proc/cpuinfo`, `dpkg -l`; Local), so the trial costs hours. The figure comes from one 8-core report on one example scene, not from a vertical frame heavy with post-processing, so the gain must be measured here. Every test render must also log `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` or be pixel-compared with a SwiftShader frame to catch the silent fallback (Inference). SwiftShader WebGL itself now works only behind `--enable-unsafe-swiftshader`, from about Chromium 135 ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/swiftshader-is-going-away-what-to-do/76140); Report), which is a second reason to get llvmpipe working.

Characters are the second lever and the most visible one. People who call animation cheap point first at characters who are "just standing" ([MyAnimeList forum](https://myanimelist.net/forum/message/72933260?goto=topic); Opinion) and at generic work that is "simultaneously glossy and empty" ([Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/1828444-generic-animation-another-toy-you-don-t-need); Opinion). Praise goes to shorts where "the animation is polished and the sound design is visceral" ([Goodreads Horror Aficionados thread](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/1728696-youtubes); Unverified, search summary). The one survival-horror study on this question had 100 participants rate 13 clips. It found that "attributes of motion and sound do exaggerate the uncanny phenomenon and how frightening that character is perceived to be" ([Tinwell, Grimshaw & Williams 2010](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/63140720/Uncanny_behaviour_in_survival_horror_games.pdf); Study).

The free route to better bodies is already on disk. Quaternius's Universal Animation Library 2 uses a **65-joint UE5-style skeleton with 43 clips**, among them `Idle_TalkingPhone_Loop`, `Idle_Lantern_Loop`, `Zombie_Walk_Fwd_Loop`, `ClimbUp_1m_RM` and `Hit_Knockback`, and its meshes carry no morph targets (`build/assets3d/uaf/ual2/`; Local). Quaternius's Universal Base Characters are "Compatible with the Universal Animation Library" and average 13k triangles. The free Standard download has two bodies and five hairstyles ([itch.io: Universal Base Characters](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-base-characters); Documented). A vendor's hands-on report says every UBC and outfit mesh binds to the same 65 joints, so UAL2 clips play with "no retargeting required" as long as each instance is cloned with `SkeletonUtils.clone` ([Cinevva, part 25](https://app.cinevva.com/de/blog/2026-05-10-open-world-browser-part-25-universal-characters); Anecdote, vendor). UAL1's 46 clips sit on a different 53-joint Rigify skeleton (`DEF-upper_arm.L`, `DEF-shin.L` and so on), so they need a bone-name map and a one-time bake (`build/assets3d/uaf/ual/`; Local).

That bake cannot rely on the stock retarget function. Bare `SkeletonUtils.retargetClip` copies world orientations, so it breaks when rest poses or bone axes differ. The official three.js example needs hand-tuned per-bone offsets ([SkeletonUtils.js r169](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js); Documented), and a core three.js contributor traced failures to how it handles glTF bind matrices ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/fixing-skeletonutils-retarget-and-retargetclip-functions/65149); Report). three-vrm's rest-pose-normalized formula, which multiplies the parent's rest rotation, the track rotation and the inverse rest rotation, is the proven alternative ([three-vrm loadMixamoAnimation.js](https://github.com/pixiv/three-vrm/blob/dev/packages/three-vrm/examples/humanoidAnimation/loadMixamoAnimation.js); Documented). One report on retargeting UAL clips found three traps. Skipping a T-pose capture over-rotated shoulders and elbows by about 30°. Root motion lives on `root`, not `pelvis`. And mapping `root` produced NaNs ([Cinevva, part 17](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-05-01-open-world-browser-part-17-animations-and-search); Anecdote, vendor). Kenney's CC0 characters are no shortcut: they are rigid, toy-rigged or limited to three clips, and none has face shapes (Local check of the packs; [Kenney support](https://kenney.nl/support) for the CC0 terms), and their toy look pulls toward the made-for-kids signals covered in the policy section (Inference). Licence risk on Quaternius assets is low. The zips we downloaded carry CC0 `License.txt` files (Local). Quaternius's newer Asset License v1.0 (28 Aug 2026) still lets any "film, video ... or render" be published and monetized with "No attribution", bans only redistributing the raw assets, and does not apply retroactively ([Quaternius Asset License](https://quaternius.com/license.html); Documented).

None of these free bodies has face shapes. So the face stays procedural, now parented to the `Head` bone, and the life comes from code. three.js r169 already ships additive clip blending, `CCDIKSolver` and morph-target face examples ([r169 files.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/files.json); [CCDIKSolver r169](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r169/examples/jsm/animation/CCDIKSolver.js); Documented). The MIT-licensed TalkingHead implements the whole idle stack as a reference: eye contact, head movement, blinking, mood-driven breathing and a "fear" mood ([TalkingHead](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead); Documented). A horror version of that stack has six layers: a base UAL clip seeked with `mixer.setTime(frame/30)`, chest breathing that speeds up when scared, a head look-at that lags the threat, seeded blinks that stop during the stare, a fear-face ramp, and IK that pins a hand to a torch or a door handle. Every layer should be a pure function of frame time and a seed, so re-renders match frame for frame (Inference).

Lip-sync is solved offline. Rhubarb Lip Sync 1.14.0 is MIT-licensed and its output "belongs to you alone". It accepts the known script through `-d`, and it emits nine mouth shapes (A-H plus rest X) as JSON ([Rhubarb README](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync); [Rhubarb licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync/master/LICENSE.md); Documented). On this container an 8.57 s narration took **14.4 s in PocketSphinx mode, about 1.7x real time on a single core, and 1.5 s in phonetic mode** (`out/voiceover/sample_3d.wav`; Local), so four lines can run at once on four cores.

Real blendshape faces are a later and riskier step, for four reasons. @pixiv/three-vrm 3.5.5 (MIT, works with three ≥0.137) offers five emotions, five visemes, blink and look-at, but no fear preset ([npm: @pixiv/three-vrm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@pixiv/three-vrm); [VRM 1.0 expressions spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/expressions.md); Documented). The CC0 100Avatars VRM we inspected carries only A/E/I/O/U/Blink (Local). MPFB's human core is CC0, and ARKit-style face units and Oculus visemes were added in February 2026, but the licence of those packs is not stated ([MPFB FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/faq/can_i_sell_models.html); [faceunits01](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/faceunits01.html); Documented, pack licence Unverified). And nobody has timed VRM's MToon shader under SwiftShader (Inference).

Every post-process a horror grade needs ships with r169: LUTPass, FilmPass, GTAOPass, SAOPass, SSAOPass, BokehPass, SMAAPass, GodRaysShader, VignetteShader and RGBShiftShader ([three.js r169 postprocessing](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r169/examples/jsm/postprocessing); Documented). But **no source times any of them under SwiftShader or llvmpipe**, so each one needs a 10-frame benchmark before it joins the stack. The sources give only relative costs:

- pmndrs/postprocessing's `EffectPass` "automatically organizes and merges" effects into fewer passes, which suits a combined grain, vignette, chromatic-aberration and LUT grade ([pmndrs/postprocessing](https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing); Documented).
- N8AO (CC0, WebGL 2) claims 2-4x speed from half-resolution ambient occlusion, measured on GPUs ([N8AO README](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao); Documented).
- r169's god-ray example works at quarter resolution, with three radial blurs of six taps each ([r169 godrays example](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r169/examples/webgl_postprocessing_godrays.html); Documented).
- The r169 PCSS soft-shadow example costs up to about 51 shadow-map reads per shadowed pixel ([r169 PCSS example](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r169/examples/webgl_shadowmap_pcss.html); Documented).
- RectAreaLight gives soft panel light, but it has "no shadow support" and works only on PBR materials ([three.js RectAreaLight docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html); Documented).

For static sets such as the lift, bus and tower, baked lighting is the documented route to soft, realistic light ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-get-photorrealism-light-shadows-in-three-js/28874); Opinion). Baking once (ProgressiveLightMap ships in r169) costs nothing per frame, while one live shadow follows the moving characters (Inference).

Sound is the cheapest upgrade with the best evidence, because the pipeline already synthesizes it in numpy:

- **Roughness.** Screams carry "roughness": amplitude modulation at 30-150 Hz that ordinary speech does not use. The roughest sounds drove amygdala responses and drew the highest fear ratings ([ScienceDaily on Arnal et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/07/150716123834.htm); Study, n = 16).
- **Noise darkens mood but no longer raises arousal on video.** Across 102 films, horror soundtracks contained more noise and more noisy screams ([Blumstein et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3001365); Study). But with a video playing, added noise only darkened the mood and stopped raising arousal ([Blumstein et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3440987); Study).
- **Startle.** Arousal spikes therefore come from startle mechanics: an abrupt, fast-onset sound, amplified when the viewer is already anxious ([Inverse, quoting Christian Grillon](https://www.inverse.com/article/7531-the-unexpected-physiology-of-jump-scares); Opinion of a researcher).
- **Heartbeat.** False feedback of a fast heartbeat raised self-reported fear; slow feedback did nothing ([Hill et al. 2024](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-false-interoceptive-feedback-on-emotional-state-/); Study, n = 60).
- **Phone speakers.** They resonate around 500-1000 Hz and lose about 12 dB per octave below that ([audioXpress](https://www.audioxpress.com/article/microspeakers-anatomy-how-to-design-audio-systems-with-very-small-drivers); Unverified, search summary). Infrasound raised cortisol in a 36-person lab study with hidden subwoofers ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/haunted-sounds-feelings/); Study, press coverage only), but it never reaches someone watching on a phone. Low dread has to be built from harmonics, which the ear completes into the missing fundamental ([Aalto thesis on the missing fundamental](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/28f25581-3beb-49e4-8418-506dcb5b3446); Study).
- **Loudness.** YouTube turns loud uploads down toward about −14 LUFS and never turns quiet ones up ([MeterPlugs](https://meterplugs.com/blog/2019/09/18/youtube-changes-loudness-reference-to-14-lufs.html); Report; no official YouTube page states the figure).

On the voice side, Chatterbox (MIT) is still the best balance of licence and control ([resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox); Documented). Turbo's speed problem is about 40-60 s per sentence in PyTorch on this CPU (Local), and it has two published fixes. One is Resemble's official ONNX export with fp32-to-q4 variants ([Chatterbox Turbo ONNX](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox-turbo-ONNX); Documented). The other is the early-stage chatterbox.cpp port, which reports Turbo at Q4_0 running at RTF 0.82 (wall time divided by audio length) on a Ryzen 9 9950X, 1.2x faster than ONNX Runtime ([chatterbox.cpp](https://github.com/gianni-cor/chatterbox.cpp); Report). The ONNX sample sets `apply_watermark = False`. Any switch must therefore re-apply Resemble's PerTh watermark, to honour the rule never to strip provenance watermarks, and must re-run the Whisper-medium and tag checks, because Q4 quantization changes the takes (Inference). Turbo's mood tags are weak, and `[whispering]` produced no real whisper in local tests (`out/turbo_tags_research.md`; Local). The open models with a working whisper control either need a GPU or forbid commercial use, so a whisper texture has to come from a DSP layer under the narrator (Inference).

### Options the evidence rules out on this machine

Several popular routes add work without adding quality on a GPU-less box. Blender's EEVEE "only uses the power of the GPU", with "no plan to support CPU (software) rendering" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/eevee/limitations/limitations.html); Documented), and EEVEE Next failed on a shader-storage limit in a GPU-less Docker container ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/belender-4-2-1-eevee-does-not-work-inside-docker/1551491); Report). Cycles runs on CPU, but dark interiors are path tracing's noisiest case, and no source gives a per-frame time for a scene like ours. OIDN denoising alone took about 1.2 s per frame of the Junkshop demo scene on an i9-13900K ([Blender 4.1 Cycles notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.1/cycles/); Documented). Godot's `--headless` mode "disables all rendering code" ([godot-proposals #5790](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/5790); Documented), and its software-GL route is "extremely slow" ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/can-godot-render-directly-to-frame-buffer-without-x-on-linux/55812); Opinion). Remotion and HeyGen's HyperFrames both seek frames in headless Chrome, so without a GPU they use the same software path we already use. Remotion's docs warn "Rendering might be slow", and its free licence covers only individuals and companies of up to three people ([Remotion --gl](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options); [Remotion licence](https://www.remotion.pro/license); [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes); Documented). Theatre.js's public development stalled when the team moved to a private 1.0 rewrite ([Theatre.js](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre); [issue #504](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre/issues/504); Documented). Good monocular mocap (GVHMR, WHAM, 4D-Humans) needs CUDA and the SMPL body model, licensed for "non-commercial scientific research" ([GVHMR licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zju3dv/GVHMR/main/LICENSE); [SMPL licence](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); Documented). FreeMoCap says a single camera "will not produce reliable 3D estimates" ([FreeMoCap](https://docs.freemocap.org/documentation/software-hardware-prerequisites.html); Documented). Ready Player Me avatars are gone: vendor blogs report the public service shut on 31 Jan 2026 ([Avatar SDK](https://avatarsdk.com/blog/2026/08/31/avatar-platforms-2026-whos-alive-whos-gone/); Unverified).

## A locked "PS1-lite toon" look with a second, recorded register

### Limits that look chosen read as craft, not cheapness

Four looks dominate low-budget horror, and each has a real-time recipe:

- **PS1 / low-poly retro.** It grew into a scene around Haunted PS1, founded in 2018 for "small-scale, low-resolution horror games", whose demo discs aim at "evoking the feeling and aesthetic of late nineties games" ([Wikipedia: Haunted PS1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunted_PS1); Documented).
- **Analog horror.** It defines itself by "low-fidelity graphics, cryptic messages, few – if any – traditional jump scares", and its works use their formats' "supposed limitations to their advantage" ([Wikipedia: Analog horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror); Documented).
- **Liminal 3D found footage.** Kane Parsons's Backrooms, made in Blender with a videotape treatment, reached 3.68M subscribers and an A24 feature ([Wikipedia: Kane Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Parsons); Documented, count undated).
- **Toon shading with outlines,** which three.js supports through `OutlineEffect` ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OutlineEffect.html); Documented).

In practice PS1 and VHS treatments get stacked. Puppet Combo's Murder House is described as "an early PS1 graphics aesthetic with a hint of 80s VHS distortion" ([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/video-games/3638659/puppet-combo-returns-babysitter-bloodbath-successor-murder-house/); Unverified, search summary). Why low-fi frightens is a matter of opinion, not data. One view: 32-bit graphics are "detailed enough to show what's happening but blurry enough that players' imaginations fill in the rest" ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/playstation-era-graphics-indie-horror-games-limitations-uncanny-unsettling-quality/); Opinion, search summary). One Hacker News commenter calls PS1-style horror "an art-form now" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21909773); Opinion).

The uncanny-valley research decides where the characters should sit. Kätsyri, Mäkäräinen and Takala (2017) had 54 people rate matched film clips. They concluded that "semirealistic animated film characters are more eerie than cartoonish characters or real actors". "Somewhat unexpectedly", the cartoonish characters scored highest on strangeness and, after correction, lowest on likability ([Maastricht University record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/testing-the-uncanny-valley-hypothesis-in-semirealistic-computer-a/); Study, abstract only). Evidence for the valley as a whole "has remained inconsistent" ([Kätsyri et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00390/pdf); Study) and "is not generalizable across different individuals, stimuli, situations, tasks, and time" ([Cheetham 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01738/full); Study). Two mechanisms hold up better. Mixing realistic and unrealistic features pushes a character into eeriness ([Chattopadhyay & MacDorman 2016](https://www.evl.uic.edu/documents/journalofvision2016_debaleenac.pdf); Study, search summary), and odd motion and sound raise perceived fright ([Tinwell et al. 2010](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/63140720/Uncanny_behaviour_in_survival_horror_games.pdf); Study). The design rule that follows: keep "you" and allies stylized and readable, and give the threat **exactly one deliberate inconsistency** at the scare beat (Inference). Kätsyri's likability result is a warning too. Stylization removes accidental eeriness but does not create appeal, so expressive eyes and clear silhouettes still have to be designed (Inference).

The market points the same way. The large animated-horror channels with verifiable numbers are 2D or narration-led: Llama Arts, hand-drawn, has 2.57M subscribers ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/llamaarts/); Documented, 7 Oct 2026) and Horror Shorts Party has 1.69M ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCsCAWoG-SagS7aFuWT1IZPw/); Documented, 24 Oct 2026). **No verifiable stylized-3D, second-person horror Shorts channel was found**, so the lane is open but untested (Gap). Meanwhile, of the first 500 Shorts shown to Kapwing's fresh test account, 21% were AI-generated and 33% were "brainrot" ([AOL, reporting Kapwing](https://www.aol.com/articles/ai-slop-brainrot-content-now-222039416.html); Report). A visibly hand-built, consistent 3D world stands out from that only if it avoids the glossy, inconsistent look of AI output (Inference).

### The look bible: what is locked and what changes

Recognisability should come from the rendering, the grammar and the framing device. The story never repeats. Each row below is a recommendation; the evidence column shows what it rests on.

| Element | Locked spec for every episode | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Characters | One construction kit for every human: the same proportions, eye/brow/mouth shapes, toon ramp and outline, all on the UAL2 skeleton, with faces readable at phone size. "You" is a recurring neutral stand-in who never speaks and is never given a backstory; seen over the shoulder, in hands, and in reaction shots | Readers reject a "you" that is characterised ([Bell 2022](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/29372/); Study, qualitative); [Ondricek](https://twine.twinery.org/archive/forum/discussion/1794/queries-on-perspective.html) (Opinion); owner's "big readable faces" (`channels/horror/README.md`; Local); Inference |
| Threat | Mostly unseen: one partial view at most (a reflection, a shape behind frosted glass, a hand on the outside of the door). | Sound identity | One short signature sting for the HUD and the vote card; the music bed, ambience and scare sounds change every episode | YouTube's spam policy names "the exact same background music" as a mass-production marker ([YouTube Help 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en); Official); Parsons's team avoided repeating melodic material ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/kane-parsons-backrooms-liminal-horror-a24-interview-20260528/); Opinion) |
| Narrator | One stock or designed voice, never a clone of a real person, one sentence per take | Impersonation policy ([YouTube Help 2801947](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947?hl=en); Official); README (Local) |

Everything not in the table changes every episode: the setting, the rule, the threat, the resource, the music bed, the camera moves, the outcome and the hidden rewatch detail. That split is the policy's own test. YouTube allows "a series following a set of characters across episodes" as long as "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept" ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); Official).

Consistency has to live in files, not in memory. Studios hold their palettes in colour scripts that map "color, lighting and emotional beats" ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/05/29/unravelling-the-mystery-of-the-color-script-and-color-keys-in-scoob/); Unverified, search summary). Analog-horror series fix one format per era (Documented, above). A code pipeline needs five equivalents (Inference):

- a versioned look file holding the LUT, grain, fog density, dither strength, outline width and colour hex values;
- the character kit kept in code;
- procedural motion driven by seeds;
- a golden-frame test that re-renders one reference frame per set after any change and flags pixel drift;
- an asset-licence log with download dates.

The same look decisions protect the channel's audience setting. Big-headed toon characters are exactly the "animated characters or cartoon figures" that YouTube lists as a made-for-kids factor ([YouTube Help 9528076](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en); Official). Local 58's animated "Show for Children" was labelled made for kids in 2022 until Kris Straub appealed ([Wikipedia: Local 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_58), citing [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2022/07/youtube-flags-horror-video-as-for-kids-wont-let-creator-change-rating/); Documented). Mature proportions, a dark palette, no bright primary-coloured mascots or nursery-style music, and metadata that says "horror" keep a toon look on the right side of that line (Inference).

## Hooks win on an everyday place, one impossible fact and a fair final choice

### The swipe is decided by text, sound and a wrong thing you can picture

YouTube scores a Short in two stages. First comes "the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away". Then comes watch behaviour among those who stayed, with average percentage viewed counted only over them ([YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts); [YouTube Help 12220281](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281); Official). YouTube publishes no hook timing and no target. The "one second" rule is Jenny Hoyos's opinion, voiced on the YouTube Blog, not a metric ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/); Opinion).

The best data comes from Snapchat. Across 90,000 Spotlight videos, staying past 5 s correlates **0.926** with normalised watch percentage. On-screen text and background-sound features predicted it; speech transcripts did not ([SnapUGC, arXiv 2410.00289](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1); Study). Paddy Galloway's 5,400-Short dataset put top performers at 70-90% viewed vs swiped ([Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); Study, self-described early research). The sister channel's own analytics agree. Openings that dropped the viewer into a scene they could picture, with something already wrong, kept 75-78%; topic statements, quiz setups and bare philosophy questions kept 36-52% (`CLAUDE.md`; Local).

Horror creators say the same. Scholar Kinitra D. Brooks: "you're usually most successful with horror when you're playing with the everyday". Creator Charles Robitaille: the twist "can be the difference between a good video and a viral video" ([TIME](https://time.com/5825674/horror-tiktok-videos/); Opinion). Make the hook's question one the viewer can almost answer, because curiosity peaks at intermediate confidence ([Kang et al. 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x); Study). Skip clickbait wording: it added no measurable clicks or curiosity in controlled tests ([Molina et al., CHI 2021](https://pike.psu.edu/publications/chi21.pdf); Study). Direct address has one data point, from ads: a third of TikTok's highest view-through auction ads "break the 4th wall" ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf); Study, sample undisclosed). Nothing has tested second person in organic horror.

Length is the format's structural risk. In SnapUGC, average watch percentage fell as duration rose ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1); Study, videos of 10-60 s). The sister channel's talky explainers over 50 s stalled near 60% viewed, against a ~90% channel average (`CLAUDE.md`; Local). Against that, Galloway found that Shorts holding viewers for more than 50 s averaged 4.1M views ([Paddy Galloway on X](https://twitter.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898363466481664); Study, early). Rene Ritchie's guidance is "as long as it needs to be, but no longer" ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length); Official via press). The defensible reading: 70-90 s works only if every beat raises the stakes. Cut any sentence that does not move the resource, the rule or the threat, and prefer 70 s over 85 s when both tell the story (Inference). The ending matters as much. In one of Hoyos's Shorts, cutting a single surplus final second raised retention from 83% to 88% ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); Anecdote). So the vote is the literal last line, cut within about 0.5 s.

### A spine borrowed from rules horror, analog horror and choice design

Rules horror gives the episode its spine. The Chinese rules-horror boom traces to *Zoo Rules Horror* (November 2021). It is told entirely as visitor guidelines and staff notices in a neutral, manual-like voice, and every rule points at an unnamed "it" that the reader assembles from what is forbidden. The fear comes from rules that contradict each other: as one reader put it, "having more than one safe zone is no different from having none" ([王玉玊, 中国作家网](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2023/0221/c404027-32627956.html); Opinion, literary analysis). SCP entries work the same way: deadpan procedure on top of horror, with SCP-173's line-of-sight rule as the danger itself ([Wikipedia: SCP Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCP_Foundation); Documented). Analog horror adds the compromised official voice. Local 58 shows an emergency alert whose "DO NOT" is made unreadable so that the text reads "LOOK AT THE MOON" ([Wikipedia: Local 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_58); Documented). In The Mandela Catalogue, a training tape orders "Do not help a caller reporting an alternate encounter", and the fear lies in not knowing who issued the order ([F Newsmagazine](https://fnewsmagazine.com/2022/01/ghosts-in-the-machine-the-fearful-symmetry-of-alex-kisters-the-mandela-catalogue/); Opinion). Kane Parsons warns against "lore bloat" ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/kane-parsons-backrooms-liminal-horror-a24-interview-20260528/); Opinion), which argues for one rule, one entity and one twist per episode (Inference). Copy the principles, not the famous moves: a moon you must not look at, a GPS route to a monster, or a 911 "do not help" tape would get named in the comments at once (Inference).

Second person needs restraint. In Alice Bell's reader study of a second-person app fiction, readers were "just as likely to reject the 'you' position as they are to adopt it". They gave three reasons: being forced into choices they did not want, being characterised in ways they disliked, and finding the address intrusive ([Bell 2022](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/29372/); Study, qualitative). Twine author Hanon Ondricek warns that games stumble when the author "assigns a trait to 'you'" or puts words in the player's mouth ([Twine forum](https://twine.twinery.org/archive/forum/discussion/1794/queries-on-perspective.html); Opinion). Tim Waggoner says second person's "chilly distance" suits weird horror ([Nightmare Magazine](https://www.nightmare-magazine.com/nonfiction/author-spotlight-tim-waggoner/); Opinion). So "you" gets senses and actions only ("You hear...", "Your light flickers."), never feelings, traits, backstory or spoken lines (Inference). Since r/nosleep requires first person ([Higgypop](http://www.higgypop.com/news/how-to-write-a-nosleep-story); Documented, 2019 guide), this format takes its conventions from interactive fiction and choice games, not from NoSleep.

Short horror runs on a setup and then a turn. Creepypastas that work "are short and to the point" and move like jokes, with crimes reported second-hand so imagination fills the gaps ([David Wellington](https://davidwellington.net/2017/10/11/the-lessons-of-creepypasta/); Opinion). A twist "with no foreshadowing can feel cheap", and the question the hook asks must be answered at the end ([Higgypop, Edwin Crowe](http://www.higgypop.com/news/how-to-write-a-nosleep-story); Opinion). Fredric Brown's "Knock" (1948) is the model contradiction: "The last man on Earth sat alone in a room. There was a knock on the door..." ([Wikipedia: Knock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock_(short_story)); Documented). In a voting format this splits the work cleanly. The twist answers the hook's question, and the vote opens a new one (Inference).

The beat map below combines these findings. The combination is this report's synthesis, not a sourced formula.

| Beat | Sentences / time | What happens | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | S1-2, 0-6 s | "You", a place you can picture, and one impossible fact; the rule or contradiction as big text on frame 0; sound from frame 0 | "Knock"; [SnapUGC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1); sister-channel analytics |
| 2. Rule | S3-4, 6-12 s | Quote the sign, card or logbook word for word, in a flat official voice; the reason for the rule is only implied | [Zoo Rules Horror analysis](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2023/0221/c404027-32627956.html); [SCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCP_Foundation) |
| 3. Resource | S5 | A number the viewer can watch (battery, fuel, minutes); every later number derives from it | FNAF power meter; the owner's number rule |
| 4. Wrongness grows | S6-9 | Sound first (a knock, a scrape, a voice), then one partial glimpse; the hidden rewatch detail planted in passing | [Analog horror restraint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror); [Crowe on foreshadowing](http://www.higgypop.com/news/how-to-write-a-nosleep-story) |
| 5. The message | S10-12 | A trusted channel (radio, intercom, phone, alert) contradicts the rule, in an official voice | [Mandela Catalogue](https://fnewsmagazine.com/2022/01/ghosts-in-the-machine-the-fearful-symmetry-of-alex-kisters-the-mandela-catalogue/); "two safe zones" |
| 6. Twist | S13-15 | One detail in the message is wrong or impossibly informed; it answers the hook's question | [Crowe](http://www.higgypop.com/news/how-to-write-a-nosleep-story); mimic horror |
| 7. Pressure | S16-18 | The resource drops; the threat moves one step closer, heard rather than seen; both options become forced | Telltale's time pressure ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-the-walking-dead-i-and-the-art-of-providing-no-good-choice)) |
| 8. Vote | S19-20 | One story reason for each side, then two parallel actions as the literal last line; cut within ~0.5 s | [Mawhorter et al. 2014](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/research/papers/towards-choice-poetics-fdg-2014.pdf); Hoyos's end-at-the-peak rule |

Three wording rules sit around these lines. Every sentence describes what "you" sees, hears or does. The words "doppelganger", "alternate" and "not my neighbor" stay out of narration and titles, because they point viewers at existing works ([That's Not My Neighbor](https://destructoid.com/?p=991741) and The Mandela Catalogue; Inference). And nothing in the hook spoils the twist (Inference, consistent with the CHI 2021 result above).

### A vote people genuinely split on

Choice-design theory separates a real dilemma, "a choice that forces a decision between two highly-valued (or highly-reviled) outcomes", from three fakes ([Mawhorter, Mateas, Wardrip-Fruin & Jhala 2014](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/research/papers/towards-choice-poetics-fdg-2014.pdf); Study, theory paper):

- a **false dilemma**, whose outcomes are not of comparable value;
- a **blind choice**, which gives too little information to "form distinct expectations";
- a **false choice**, where "all of the different options lead to the same outcome".

Designers misjudge their own splits. Until Dawn's team was sure players would shoot a particular character, but "It was much closer to 50-50" in testing ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2015/10/18/afterwords-until-dawn.aspx); Report, designer interview). Telltale let major decisions "play out both ways", so that "the choice taken and the choice not taken" mattered equally, and kept choices under time pressure ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-the-walking-dead-i-and-the-art-of-providing-no-good-choice); Opinion, designer). *No, I'm Not a Human*, a knock-at-the-door decision game, passed one million sales by March 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No,_I%27m_Not_a_Human); Documented). One review notes its weak spot: "the safest bet would be to let nobody in" ([Tryhard Guides](https://tryhardguides.com/no-im-not-a-human-review/); Unverified, search summary). That is a dominated option, and it has to be designed out.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both options cost something the script stated (a true dilemma, not a false one) | [Mawhorter et al.](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/research/papers/towards-choice-poetics-fdg-2014.pdf) (Study) |
| 2 | Each option has one concrete reason from the story, defensible in one sentence (no blind choice) | Same |
| 3 | No strictly safer option: the draining resource closes "do nothing" | [Tryhard Guides](https://tryhardguides.com/no-im-not-a-human-review/) (Unverified); Inference |
| 4 | Two parallel actions of equal length and tone; neither sounds stupid | Inference |
| 5 | A cold read by a few people splits no further than about 70/30 (the cut-off is a rule of thumb) | [Until Dawn](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2015/10/18/afterwords-until-dawn.aspx) (Report); cut-off is Inference |
| 6 | The question asks what "you" do, not what kind of person the viewer is | [Bell 2022](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/29372/) (Study) |
| 7 | The vote visibly changes Part 2 (an item, an injury, an ally, a broken rule), even when both branches lead to the same next setting | [Kashyap](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/meaningful-decisions-in-branching-narratives) (Opinion); [GameSkinny on fake choices](https://www.gameskinny.com/opinion/until-dawn-and-the-illusion-of-choice/) (Opinion) |
| 8 | No rule teaches real-world harm or tells bystanders to ignore someone in danger | Chinese criticism of rules stories ([gmw.cn](https://m.gmw.cn/2025-09/19/content_1304154161.htm); Unverified); policy section |

Part 2 opens in a fixed order (Inference). S1 is the callback with the real percentage. S2 shows the consequence. S3 gives one line on the road not taken; choice theory says regret is stronger "when an alternative outcome is known with certainty" ([Mawhorter et al.](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/research/papers/towards-choice-poetics-fdg-2014.pdf); Study, theory). Then comes a new rule. Part 2 also has to work cold, for three reasons (Inference). "Shorts don't trigger notifications on upload" ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-28-2024-edition/); Official). Ritchie says "recency weighs more for your short form video" ([Elite Content Marketer, NAB 2026](https://www.elitecontentmarketer.com/get-more-youtube-views-for-free/); Official via press). And subscribers skip about 90% of uploads in their feed ([ppc.land, quoting Todd Beaupré](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/); Official via press). So the recap has to be a hook in itself, and Part 2 should land within about a day. A small vote log keeps delayed callbacks consistent. Choice theory notes that delayed effects make a choice feel "more important than it originally appeared" ([Mawhorter et al.](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/research/papers/towards-choice-poetics-fdg-2014.pdf); Study, theory).

The ballot itself should be the **Shorts poll sticker**, not a comment count. The sticker shows each option's percentage and total votes, and it rolled out to all creators in October 2024 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-adds-poll-stickers-shorts-ad-category-blocking-channels/730826/); Official via press). YouTube describes it as "a custom question with two straightforward options" ([YouTube Blog, Jul 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/grow-youtube-channel-interactive-shorts/); Official). Comments make a noisy ballot. Shorts average about 10 comments each, against 50 on TikTok ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/); Study, brand accounts). So comments should carry the arguments, and the sticker should carry the count (Inference).

A video reply to the best comment puts that comment on screen as a sticker and notifies its author ([YouTube Help 12816427](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12816427); Official, wording via search summary). That makes a natural Part 2 frame 0. Shorts series, the official season-and-episode container, is limited to Partner Program creators ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/youtubes-new-short-series-feature-brings-episodic-viewing-to-shorts/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/418231/youtube-rolls-out-feature-to-encourage-episodic-co.html); Official via press). Until the channel qualifies, playlists and the related-video link (which needs advanced features) connect the parts ([YouTube Help 14075157](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en); Official).

One more lever serves the owner's "meaningful" rule. A mimic premise carries a real question: do we trust authority, familiarity or evidence? Framing it that way turns the vote into an argument rather than a coin flip (Inference).

## The vote is allowed; templates, kid looks and alert tones are the traps

Asking for a vote is explicitly allowed in two separate policies. The spam policy says: "It's okay to ask viewers to like, comment, or subscribe" ([YouTube Help 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en); Official). The fake-engagement policy says: "You're allowed to encourage viewers to subscribe, hit the like button, share, or leave a comment" ([YouTube Help 3399767](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3399767?hl=en); Official). What is banned is engagement from "coercion or deception", "offering rewards in exchange for" engagement, and "content that solely exists to incentivize viewers for engagement" (same pages; Official). A story vote with no prize and a real follow-through is none of these. It turns into a problem if the vote is not honoured or Part 2 never comes, which would be "misleading viewers to boost content engagement" ([YouTube Help 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en); Official; the application to this format is Inference). Comment automation is separately restricted. API clients "must not automate or trigger ... comments ... without the user's prior specific and express consent" and must not offer incentives for comments ([YouTube API Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies); Official). That rules out "comment SURVIVE to get the guide" mechanics.

The monetization risk is the template. Since 15 July 2025 the rule is called "inauthentic content". It excludes "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" and "templated storylines". It excludes content that "repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative" or that is "designed to shock or surprise viewers for the sole purpose of getting views". And it applies at channel level, so reviewers look at "Newest videos" and "Most viewed videos" ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); Official). On 1 October 2026 YouTube added that Shorts ranking will "further prioritize original content" and asked creators to bring "your own voice, storytelling, or distinct point of view" ([YouTube Help Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en); Official). A fixed HUD and vote card is the permitted "same intro and outro" case, provided the bulk of each episode differs (same page; Official).

The reach risk is the audience classification. Content with "violence ... or other mature themes not suitable for young audiences" is not made for kids ([YouTube Help 9528076](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en); Official). But a made-for-kids classification disables "Comments", "Cards or end screens" and the "Notification bell", and YouTube's machine learning "may override your audience setting choice" ([YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en); Official). One wrong classification silently kills the vote. Age restriction is the other reach-killer. Dramatized violence showing people "tortured, dismembered, or decapitated" can be age-restricted, as can cartoons with adult themes that "could easily be confused with family content". Age-restricted videos are invisible to under-18 and signed-out viewers and "are not shown in certain sections of YouTube" ([YouTube Help 2802008](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802008?hl=en); [YouTube Help 2801999](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999?hl=en); [YouTube Help 1153367](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1153367?hl=en); Official). Animation is treated leniently: YouTube does "not remove dramatized violence ... when it's apparent from the content itself, such as with animated content" ([YouTube Help 2802008](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802008?hl=en); Official). The advertiser guidelines define "Dramatized" as including "animated content" and give full ads to "Dramatized content depicting non-graphic violence or graphic violence" ([YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en); Official).

The legal risk is sound. US rules forbid any person to transmit the Emergency Alert System attention signal or codes, or the Wireless Emergency Alert tone (853 and 960 Hz together; one 2 s tone, then two 1 s tones), "or a recording or simulation thereof" outside real emergencies, tests and authorized PSAs ([47 CFR 11.45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/11.45); [47 CFR 10.520](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/10.520); Official). The FCC's 2019 advisory names "dramatic, entertainment, and educational programs". It counts as a simulation any sound "an average listener could reasonably mistake" for the real one, but excludes "general alarms or other loud noises, including bells; klaxons; and police, fire, or civil defense sirens". It cites past fines of $1,120,000 against Viacom and $280,000 against ESPN ([FCC DA 19-758](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-19-758A1.txt); Official). In December 2025 a public-radio producer paid $86,400 over alert tones in a history documentary ([FCC DA 25-1035](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1035A1.txt); Official). No action against a YouTube-only creator was found (Gap). But a viral Short can be re-aired by broadcasters, which is exactly what the FCC enforces against (Inference).

AI disclosure is not required for this format. YouTube requires it only for content that makes a real person appear to say or do something, alters a real event or place, "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", or creates music that is the main focus ([YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en); Official). The 2024 launch post exempted content that is "clearly unrealistic, animated" ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/); Official). The same post listed "synthetically generating a person's voice to narrate a video" as an example, but it sits under the likeness of a realistic person. A stock voice that is nobody's likeness falls outside it, so "AI use: No" is defensible (Inference). Ticking "Yes" also costs nothing: disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" ([YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en); Official).

For context on monetization, the Partner Program bars for Shorts are 500 subscribers plus 3M Shorts views in 90 days for the lower tier, 1,000 subscribers plus 10M views in 90 days until 31 January 2027, and 20M from 1 February 2027 ([YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en); [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en); Official, as read for the sister channel's report).

## Most format folklore is unmeasured, so the channel must measure it

Much of what circulates about this format is either unproven or answers a different question. The table sorts the common claims by what the sources actually show.

The technical unknowns can be settled locally in a day. First, render 10 identical frames per configuration (SwiftShader against llvmpipe, then each pass added on its own) and record seconds per frame. Second, time Turbo on the same five sentences in PyTorch, ONNX q8 and chatterbox.cpp Q4_0, running Whisper-medium and the tag check on every take. Third, play each final mix through a 300-500 Hz high-pass as a phone-speaker stand-in (Inference).

On the channel, log the following for every episode (Inference):

- viewed vs swiped and average percentage viewed;
- the retention value at 5 s and the drop over the final 3 s;
- Part 2's engaged views as a share of Part 1's;
- the poll split;
- comments and subscribers per 1,000 engaged views;
- hook pattern, setting, outcome, length and look-file version.

The metric definitions come from YouTube ([YouTube Help 12942217](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en); Official). Compare each episode with the channel's own rolling median once ten episodes exist, change one variable at a time, and run 8-10 episodes per arm before deciding (Inference). YouTube warns that a retention spike can mean "your content wasn't clear and your audience had to rewatch a part" (same page; Official), so a bump on the rule or the twist is a clarity warning before it is a success.

## Conclusion

The CPU budget and the evidence point the same way. The things this machine cannot afford (photoreal faces, raymarched volumetrics, path-traced darkness, GPU mocap) are also the things the research values least. The things it can afford (stylized characters, procedural motion, rough synthesized sound, a lo-fi treatment that looks chosen, threats left mostly unseen) are the ones the uncanny-valley, sound and analog-horror evidence rewards. The channel's distinctiveness therefore comes from three design choices nobody else in the feed has been shown to combine, not from fidelity: a recurring stand-in for "you" who never speaks, an invented institution that files every night, and a threat that breaks exactly one rule of its toon world.

The same split solves the two biggest risks at once. Locking the render, the HUD grammar and the framing device gives viewers something to recognise. Varying the setting, rule, threat, music and outcome every night keeps the channel outside YouTube's "same situation, same outcome" clause, and mature, dark styling keeps the cartoon look out of the made-for-kids classifier that would switch the vote off. The vote itself is best treated as a dilemma-design problem, not an engagement trick: pre-test it toward 50-50, count it with the poll sticker, honour it on screen, and make every episode satisfying even for a viewer who never sees Part 2. No public dataset covers this format, so a few months of disciplined logging will give the owner better evidence than exists anywhere else.
