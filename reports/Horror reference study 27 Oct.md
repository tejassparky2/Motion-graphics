# Why two 10-12k horror Shorts beat our 1.5k (27 Oct 2026)

Owner, 27 Oct 2026: "This video got more views like 10-12k even though its same new page as mine has 2 video i got
only 1.5k views study this". The two videos are another creator's interactive horror Part 1 and Part 2 (a Halloween
"copy" story). They are studied for technique only. Their videos and story are never copied or committed. Compared
with our Tower 9 Night 1 and Night 2 (out/horror/tower9_2d.mp4, tower9_n2_2d.mp4).

Method: Whisper medium word timings, ffmpeg scene detection and EBU R128 loudness, frames sampled at 1 fps.

**Things that are the same, so they don't explain the gap:**
- speaking pace;
- brightness (both are dark);
- big hook text and sound on frame 0;
- a status bar with a clock and the part number;
- word captions with one coloured key word;
- an A/B card at the end.

Platforms normalise loudness to about -14 LUFS, so our louder mix makes no difference.

## What they do that we don't (most likely impact first)

1. **The premise is the viewer's own life, at the right time of year.** It's Halloween night, at home, with Mom and a
   little brother, at the front door, and the phone's date reads Saturday, October 31 (this Saturday). Ours is a
   fire-tower ranger in a forest.
2. **Frame 0 looks like the viewer's own phone.** An emergency alert on a lock screen, which scrollers read in one
   second.
3. **The rules are tells.** Three numbered rules arrive in the first 15 s, and each one comes back later as evidence
   (the smile). The vote is a deduction ("Which one is Mom?") that people can argue in the comments with clues. Our
   Tower 9 votes are gambles with nothing to argue from (open the hatch or keep it shut).
4. **A test the viewer plays along with.** "What's the dog's name?" Both answer right, so the easy answer is gone.
5. **A late flip at about 75%.** The narrator gives the answer ("So the kitchen mom is real"), then one fact takes it
   away ("Unless someone already opened the door"). This sends people back to rewatch and to argue.
6. **The picture changes every 2.5-4 s and nearly always shows a face or the creature.** They use circular close-up
   insets. Night 2 had 6 hard cuts in 66 s, and from 6 s to 30 s it was dark sets with nobody in them.
7. **Part 2 opens on the vote result with comment cards on screen** ("Almost all of you trusted the kitchen"). That
   proves commenting steers the story, so people comment again.
8. **Shorter.** 57-65 s against our 66-72 s.
9. **The final card stays up for about 2.5 s** with the comment instruction, where ours cuts at 0.5 s. This one is
   minor and untested.

Caveat: two videos per page is a small sample, and early reach on a new page is noisy, so part of a 10x gap can be
luck. Their analytics aren't visible; everything above is read from the videos themselves.

## What we change (our own stories, never their plot)

- **Premises.** Put them in places the viewer lives: home, phone, school, family. Date them when seasonal (Halloween
  is Saturday 31 Oct).
- **Rules and vote.** Bring in 2-3 rules early that act as tells. The vote is "which one is real" (or a moral fork),
  with evidence on screen for both sides.
- **Test and flip.** Each episode gets one test beat and one late flip, where the narrator states the answer and one
  fact takes it away.
- **Shots.** A new shot every 2-4 s, with a face or the threat in every shot. No empty dark set for longer than 2 s;
  use circular close-up insets.
- **Part 2 and later.** Open on the vote result plus 4-5 real top comments from the page (names shortened).
- **Length.** 55-62 s.
- **Final card.** Hold it for 2 s with "Comment A or B · most comments decide Part 2" written on it, instead of saying
  it aloud.
