# Three Pokémon episodes set our anime's rules

Write each 60-90 s episode as one compressed Pokémon act. Open on a cute creature doing something wrong, show one real animal fact on screen in the first fifth, and let the obvious move fail. The hero wins only when that fact and his bond turn the creature's own trait. Then stop on the reveal of the next problem and ask the A/B question as the last spoken line. All three owner-supplied episodes follow this pattern once measured: "Evolving Research!" (S22E14 = SM106), "Beauty Is Only Crystal Deep!" (S22E22 = SM114) and "When Regions Collide!" (S20E43 = SM043). Every climax pays off something shown in the first third. The decisive turn is a reframe or a moral choice, never raw power. A held reaction shot follows almost every beat. Their animation follows an equally stable grammar. Dialogue holds about 3-4 s, action cuts every 1-2 s and transformations cut faster than once a second. A hit runs 0.5-1.2 s of anticipation with a 2-5 frame eye close-up, then 1-3 abstract contact frames and a 1-2 s result hold. Darkness carries fear, a single hue carries transformation and warm dusk carries safety, and the sound falls to near-silence just before the biggest reveals. The research on isekai premieres, bonds and branching stories adds three guardrails for our powerless 19-year-old biologist. Prove his knowledge on a physical problem in episode 1, and let it fail first. Earn the first bond by mutual rescue. Run the vote as branch-and-bottleneck with a state ledger, so that each of the two options carries a visible upside and risk and the losing branch still echoes later. Production fits the style. About half of Pokémon's frame steps show no change, so the VRM hero can be stepped on 3s and the code-built creatures on 2s, with pycairo impact frames and backgrounds on 1s. At 30-45 s per frame, a 75 s episode costs 15-22 CPU-hours if every frame is rendered, and these holds should roughly halve that [estimate]. The numbers have known limits. Three shot detectors disagree by up to 2x on absolute cut rates, Whisper timestamps drift, and Bulbapedia, Anime News Network and Reddit were blocked. The rules below therefore lean on comparisons within each episode and on cut counts checked by eye.

The detectors fail in opposite directions. In dark or same-coloured scenes they **miss cuts**: one E14 "27.6 s shot" (18:24.9) is really about five shots and a fade. In effect-heavy action they **invent cuts** from 1-2 frame flashes, inverted impact frames and frame-filling dust. That is why E43's raw 1.13 s median shrinks to **1.88 s (Kiawe's battle) and 1.06 s (Ash's climax)** once it is corrected by eye. Absolute medians should therefore be read as ±0.5-0.8 s, while comparisons between scene types *within* an episode hold up well. The tables in this report say which numbers are eye-corrected.

## Every payoff in the three episodes was planted in the first third

### A 75-second episode compresses one TV act

**Observed.** E14 runs two parallel plots, a warm canyon plot and a cool sea plot, and both end in an evolution. Its cold open takes **128 s (10% of the file)**. It reaches a stated want at 0:36-0:41 and its first mystery hook only at 1:55. Measured against its 1,081 s body, the race rule lands at **39%** (9:49) and the lowest point (alone, dark, swarmed) at **43-48%** (10:35-11:28). The climactic choice comes at **59-62%** (13:20-13:58) and the fact-based twist, "It isn't evolving!", at **66-67%** (14:37-14:50). E22 opens with **50 s of no dialogue**: a rip in the sky and a white creature that descends looking *down* at the camera (0:12-0:18). Every one of its 14 payoffs was planted in act A, and the rescuer was planted twice (8:25; 15:57 → 16:03). E43 escalates through four battles. Two small comic ones (~94 s, ~54 s) come first, then a ~4:08 loss and a ~4:10 win, and after each battle comes talk cut at 3-6 s medians. **None of the three dub files has a next-episode preview.** Each ends softly on "To Be Continued" (E14 20:44; E22 20:44-20:47; E43 20:42.8). Chief director Kunihiko Yuyama says the team starts "by imagining what would happen if these Pokémon actually did exist in a given place" ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/17625-pokemon-anime-director-explains-why-ash-was-paired-with-pikachu)). Tomioka writes first for small children, aiming at what they find "cool" and want to act out ([Nerima interview](https://nerima-eizobunka.com/interview/vol29/)). On YouTube, **Shorts series with seasons, episodes and sequential playback launched on 23 Sep 2026** ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/made-on-youtube-creators-shorts-series-tv-features/)) and are rolling out to Partner Program creators ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/youtubes-new-short-series-feature-brings-episodic-viewing-to-shorts/)).

### Episode one proves the knowledge edge on a physical problem

**Observed.** The isekai premieres that critics praise keep the protagonist **weaker than the world**. They handle the death or transition fast and give it one tone: bleak in *Slime*, where his powers even come from how he died ([Anime Feminist](https://animefeminist.com/review-that-time-i-got-reincarnated-as-a-slime-episode-1)), and "surprisingly grim" in *Bookworm* ([Anime Feminist](https://animefeminist.com/review-ascendance-of-a-bookworm-episode-1)). *Re:Zero* was praised for a "damn near defenseless" hero who can die ([Wherever I Look](https://wherever-i-look.com/tv-shows/anime/rezero-starting-life-in-another-world-season-1-episode-1-the-end-of-the-beginning-and-beginning-of-the-end-series-premiere-overview-review-with-spoilers)). *Made in Abyss* hides a "subtle whiff of wrongness" under a lush, rounded world ([Anime Feminist](https://animefeminist.com/review-made-abyss-episode-1)). In *Dr. Stone*'s first episode, Senku's nitric-acid hypothesis fails on birds and on Taiju before it works ([Wherever I Look](https://wherever-i-look.com/tv-series/anime/dr-stone-season-1-episode-1-stone-world-series-premiere-recap-review-with-spoilers)). Its writer frames the story's limits as "the rules of science" ([SLJ](https://goodcomicsforkids.slj.com/2019/12/20/animenyc-a-peek-behind-the-scenes-of-dr-stone/)). Reviewers called *Delicious in Dungeon*'s step-by-step monster lessons "of little interest" ([But Why Tho?](https://butwhytho.net/?p=183070)) and *Spider* "a checklist of isekai tropes" ([Yattatachi](https://yattatachi.com/so-im-a-spider-so-what-winter-2021-anime-first-impressions-spoiler%E2%80%91free)). Truck-kun is a called-out cliché ([Otaku USA](https://otakuusamagazine.com/?p=320836)), and Kadokawa banned alternate-world entries from a 2017 contest ([SoraNews24](https://soranews24.com/2017/05/22/anime-style-novel-contest-in-japan-bans-alternate-reality-stories-and-teen-protagonists/)). ## Wins come from a planted fact and a choice, never from power

### Clever, fair challenges

**Observed.** E22 states its problem as one physical fact: the thief is "so fast-moving... couldn't collect any data" (4:51-4:56). It states the strategy as one line: "If we can't keep up... catch it when it comes to us." (12:22-12:26). The obvious moves fail first. Quick Attack misses every pass (10:29-10:40), and the heroes' Z-Move power-up *feeds* the thief, which snatches the glowing crystals (14:39-14:53). The win comes from stillness against speed: Bewear tracks, waits and steps in (17:12-17:31). In E14 the rival's expensive "Bug Stone" (9:12-9:35) does nothing (14:37). The real cause is the canyon's magnetic field, read on a device the episode shows four times. The turning point is a moral choice, to save the rival rather than win the race (13:20-13:58), and the prize is then given away (14:23). In E43 a small Geodude uses Gyro Ball as a defence (6:20.8-6:29), and Steelix later uses it against the big attack (8:53.2). The rival says no one has broken her Hurricane (17:30), and the hero turns it into a ladder (17:43.6-18:02). In Ash's first gym battle he surrenders to protect Pikachu, then refuses a fluke win ([Serebii EP005](https://serebii.net/anime/epiguide/indigo/005.shtml)). Fans mock rule-bending "Heart of the Cards" wins ([Yugipedia](https://yugipedia.com/wiki/Heart_of_the_Cards)). Sanderson's Second Law is "Limitations > Powers" ([Sanderson](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-second-law/)).

## Both branches must cost something the viewer cares about

**Observed.** None of the three episodes leaves its plot open. Each resolves completely and closes softly, so Pokémon offers no model for a viewer vote. What it does offer is the **act-out**: E43 breaks for ads on the reveal of the opponent's escalation (Mega Steelix 10:24.1, the rival shocked 10:25.4, sound down 10:26.75, eyecatch 10:28.4). E22's act-out lands on its worst turn so far, the thief leaving with a guide (11:47-11:56). Studies of branching stories show what voters punish. Critics called *Bandersnatch*'s options "pseudo-choices" and found its course-corrections and walls ("You're not scripted to jump out") tiring on rewatch ([/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/563285/black-mirror-bandersnatch-spoiler-review/)). Telltale was faulted because choices "don't always 'remember'" ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2015/02/03/why-your-choices-dont-matter-in-telltale-games.aspx)), while references to past actions keep players invested ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/the-illusion-of-player-choice-in-the-walking-dead/)). Ashwell's branch-and-bottleneck pattern rejoins branches at common events and "almost always" relies on heavy state-tracking ([IFWiki](https://www.ifwiki.org/Standard_Patterns_in_Choice-Based_Games)). MS Paint Adventures dropped reader commands to tell a coherent story ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Paint_Adventures)). Forum quests split votes into preference votes and "puzzle" votes ([Sufficient Velocity](https://directforums.sufficientvelocity.com/posts/12146863)). A meta-analysis found that the urge to *resume* interrupted tasks is general, while the memory advantage is not ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). YouTube bans "offering rewards in exchange for likes, views, or subscribers" ([spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)), and made-for-kids videos lose comments ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en)). "Characters... that appeal to children" is one factor in that classification ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)). No source measured retention or comment rates for comment-voted Shorts.

### Attack, impact and transformation staging

**Observed.** Every attack measured frame by frame across the three episodes follows one order. E14's tail attack runs 2.2 s of command, a 5-frame eye close-up, a 2.4 s wind-up where the body barely changes while the flame grows, a 4-frame strike and a 3-frame flash. Then come 6 frames of abstract impact graphics alternating on 2s, a 2-frame hit, a 0.8 s knock-away and a 6.1 s reaction (6:42.1-6:48.5). E43's grammar matches it: 0.5-1.2 s of anticipation with a 2-3 frame eye insert, contact in **1-3 abstract frames** (white, solid colour or inverted line art) and a 1-2 s result hold. ## A hold-heavy grammar roughly halves the render bill

**Observed.** The production facts are as follows. Blender 4.5 EEVEE on the 4-core CPU costs about **30-45 s per 720x1280 frame**, and the toon style test measured 26-32 s at 16 samples (`channels/monsters/NOTES.md`). The hero is the VRM Consortium's Seed-san, restyled. Its VRM Public License 1.0 allows commercial use and modification with credit, so every description carries "Hero base model: Seed-san by VirtualCast, Inc. (VRM Public License 1.0)". The VRM add-on v4.10.3 supports Blender 4.2 LTS up to, but not including, 5.3 ([Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/vrm/)) and is scriptable ([scripting API](https://vrm-addon-for-blender.info/en-us/scripting-api/)). Here, headless import works only with `-y` and `addon_enable`. It takes 17-34 s and yields a 141-bone humanoid. **All 18 expression presets** work: happy, angry, sad, relaxed, surprised, neutral, the visemes aa/ih/ou/ee/oh, blinks and four look directions. MToon rendered at 32 s per frame at only 360x640, after an 87 s first shader compile (`channels/monsters/NOTES.md`). VRM expressions can also bind texture transforms and material colours ([VRM 1.0 spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/expressions.md)). Mixamo is royalty-free for commercial projects with a free Adobe ID, but raw files may not be redistributed ([Adobe FAQ, via search extract](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html)). CMU mocap is "free for all uses" ([CMU](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/)). Rokoko's retargeter is LGPL-3 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Rokoko/rokoko-studio-live-blender)), and MoMask's authors report that Rokoko leaves large foot error, recommending Keemap ([momask-codes](https://github.com/EricGuo5513/momask-codes)). AMASS forbids "production of other artefacts for commercial purposes" ([AMASS licence](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html)). The Bandai Namco and LAFAN1 sets are non-commercial ([Bandai Namco](https://github.com/BandaiNamcoResearchInc/Bandai-Namco-Research-Motiondataset); [LAFAN1](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-animation-dataset)). ToonCrafter needs about 10 GB of VRAM even after community optimisation ([GitHub](https://github.com/Doubiiu/ToonCrafter)). Blender 5.3's Material Lighting nodes target toon shading, but 5.3 is in beta until 4 Nov 2026 ([5.3 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.3/eevee/)). In Pokémon's own timing, **about 50% of frame steps show no change**. Talking is acted on 3s (E43 talk 31% on 3s plus 35% held 6+ frames; E22 monologue 45% on 3s), fast action on 2s (E22 clash 53%) and camera moves and effects on 1s.

## Conclusion

The three episodes have the same shape as our constraints. Pokémon spends motion only where the eye needs it: effects on 1s, action on 2s, talk on 3s and long holds for feeling. That timing is the cheapest path through a CPU-bound EEVEE pipeline, and it lets a VRM hero with 18 presets look like TV anime instead of mocap. Its engagement also comes from planting, not power. Every climax we measured was set up in the first act, often twice, and the decisive turn was a reframe or a moral choice. That suits a hero with no powers. In practice the trainer's "command" beat becomes the biologist's "observation" beat, the creature owns all the action, and the bond kit takes the place of Z-Moves and Mega Evolution.

The biggest change in thinking is where the vote belongs. Pokémon episodes close softly, but their act breaks land on the reveal of an escalation. The research shows that an unresolved overarching story, not a cliffhanger, keeps people coming back, and that stories with their resolution removed are rated as barely stories at all. So each Short should resolve its danger with a real fact and then open the next fork, with two costly, defensible paths recorded in a state ledger so the losing one still matters. Whether this lifts stayed-to-watch, vote rates and next-episode views on this channel is something no source could measure. The first three episodes are the experiment.
