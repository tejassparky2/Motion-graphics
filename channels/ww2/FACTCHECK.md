# WW2 Episode 1: fact-check of the narration (helper 4, 31 Oct 2026)

I checked all 131 narration lines of `channels/ww2/ep01_script.md` independently. The script itself is not edited.

## Result
- **116 lines are fine as written.**
- **15 lines need a fix.**
  - 5 are errors: a wrong date, a wrong word or a wrong sequence.
  - 10 are contested or misleadingly simplified points that history commenters reliably call out.
- **Optional additions** for fair credit: China, Burma, 9 May, the Tokyo trials.
- **Sensitivity:** nothing blocking. The Holocaust chapter is careful and accurate, with one wording improvement.

Every fix keeps the sentence short and speakable.

## Errors (fix before recording)

| # | Problem | Source | Suggested line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **51** | Germany didn't *surrender* in 1918: it signed an **armistice** in the Compiègne carriage. | IWM, "The Armistice"; Britannica, "Armistice of Compiègne" | **Hitler makes them sign it in the very same railway carriage where Germany signed the armistice in 1918.** |
| **57** | Wrong order. The Blitz on London began on **7 Sept 1940**, and Operation Sea Lion was postponed on **17 Sept**. The night bombing started while the invasion was still planned; it didn't replace it. | IWM, "The Blitz"; Britannica, "Battle of Britain" | **By autumn, the invasion is called off, and German bombers pound Britain's cities at night.** |
| **77** | The codebreaking (Station HYPO, Rochefort) happened in **May** 1942; the battle was 4-7 June. As written, the code is broken in June. | Naval History and Heritage Command (NHHC), "Battle of Midway" | **But American codebreakers have learned where Japan will strike next. A tiny island called Midway.** (The June date moves to line 78: **"On June 4th, American dive bombers…"**) |
| **107** | The Bulge (16 Dec 1944) wasn't his *last* reserves: Operation Nordwind (Jan 1945) and Lake Balaton (Mar 1945) came after it. | Britannica, "Battle of the Bulge"; National WWII Museum | **In December, Hitler launches his last big attack in the west, back through the Ardennes forest. The Battle of the Bulge.** |

## Contested or misleadingly simplified (strongly recommended)

| # | Problem | Source | Suggested line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10** | "Make Germany accept the blame" is the popular reading of Art. 231. Historians note the clause assigned **responsibility for war damages**, though Germans read it as war guilt. Commenters argue this both ways. | Britannica, "Treaty of Versailles"; Sally Marks, *The Myths of Reparations* (1978) | **…the winners make Germany accept responsibility for the war, give up land, shrink its army and pay huge sums of money.** |
| **28** | 30 Sept 1938 to 15 Mar 1939 is about **5½ months**. Also, Slovakia became a German puppet state rather than being "taken". | Britannica, "Czechoslovakia: Munich and after" | **Less than six months later, Hitler seizes what's left of Czechoslovakia anyway.** |
| **32** | The secret protocol split **Eastern Europe** into spheres (Poland, the Baltic states, Finland, Bessarabia), not just Poland. Baltic and Finnish viewers will notice. | USHMM, "German-Soviet Pact"; Britannica | **And in secret, they agree to split Poland and Eastern Europe between them.** |
| **35** | "Blitzkrieg" was not an official German doctrine. It was popularised by the Western press, and the Poland campaign was largely conventional (Frieser, *The Blitzkrieg Legend*). "It's called" invites "well, actually". | IWM; Karl-Heinz Frieser, *The Blitzkrieg Legend* (2005) | **Newspapers call it Blitzkrieg. Lightning war.** |
| **52** | "Britain stands almost alone" erases the Commonwealth: Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and India were all at war from 1939. This is a common complaint from Commonwealth viewers. | IWM, "The Commonwealth at war"; National Army Museum | **Now Britain and its Commonwealth stand almost alone.** |
| **64-65** | "Then winter arrives" implies the cold stopped Germany: the "General Winter" myth. The offensive had already stalled on supply, mud and Soviet resistance before the deep frost. Both facts in 65 are right; only the framing needs a fix. | National WWII Museum, "Battle of Moscow"; David Stahel, *The Battle for Moscow* (2015) | 64: **Exhausted, short of supplies, and slowed by autumn mud, the Germans stall. Then winter arrives.** (65 unchanged) |
| **90** | Enigma is a **cipher** (machine), not a code. Bletchley read Enigma from 1940, and the naval key was only broken for good at the end of 1942. Pedants will flag "code". | Bletchley Park Trust; IWM | **…crack the German Enigma cipher.** (or **"…read the Germans' secret Enigma messages."**) |
| **114** | VE Day is 8 May in the West, but **9 May** in the Soviet Union and Russia (the surrender took effect after midnight Moscow time). A real fairness point for Eastern viewers. | Britannica, "V-E Day"; IWM | **May 8th becomes Victory in Europe Day. In Moscow, it's May 9th.** |
| **119** | "Around 200,000" is the **upper** end. End-of-1945 estimates run from about 110,000 to about 210,000 (the script's own sources note lower estimates). | Radiation Effects Research Foundation (RERF), "Frequently asked questions"; Britannica | **By the end of the year, the two bombs have killed as many as two hundred thousand people.** |

Collaboration is part of the history and commenters raise it. | USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, "Introduction to the Holocaust" | **Nazi Germany and its collaborators are carrying out the Holocaust. A plan to murder every Jewish person in Europe.** |

## Fair credit: optional additions (not errors)
The script already credits Poland (codebreakers, line 90), the Soviets (Moscow, Stalingrad, Kursk, Bagration, Berlin)
and the Commonwealth (El Alamein, line 80; D-Day, line 103). The gaps:
- **China** appears only at line 19 and in line 74's list. China fought Japan from 1937, tied down most of Japan's
  army and lost an estimated 15-20 million people, the second-highest toll of the war (Britannica; Rana Mitter,
  *China's War with Japan*). One line after 109 would cover it:
  **"In China, millions of soldiers have held down most of Japan's army since 1937, and in Burma, British and
  Indian troops drive Japan back."**
  - Optionally, add at 123: **"China loses more than fifteen million."**
- **Burma and India** (Imphal-Kohima 1944, the Fourteenth Army) are the main Commonwealth war against Japan. The
  sentence above covers them too.
- **Line 126:** add **"…and Japanese leaders in Tokyo."** so the war-crimes trials aren't Europe-only.
- **Line 55-56 (optional):** Polish, Czech and Commonwealth pilots flew in the Battle of Britain (No. 303 Polish
  Squadron had the highest score of any squadron). Polish viewers love the credit.

## Sensitivity and advertiser-friendliness
- **Holocaust chapter (92-97):** accurate and careful.
  - 6 million is correct. The perpetrator method (ghettos, mass shootings, killing centres) is correct.
  - The other victim groups in line 96 are correct and fairly listed. Writing "Roma and Sinti" would be more
    precise. Gay men, Jehovah's Witnesses and political prisoners could be added if a line can be spared.
  - No stick-figure victims and no symbols: good. Keep the camera off any recognisable photo of victims unless it is
    licensed and captioned.
- **Hitler's suicide (112):** a brief, factual historical mention is fine under YouTube's advertiser guidelines. Keep
  the visual as described: a door and silence, no method.
- **Atomic bombs (116-119):** fine as described, a distant flash and no casualties shown.
- **Symbols:** no swastika is right. Also keep the **Rising Sun flag** off Japanese units. It is offensive in Korea and
  China, and a big audience complaint. Use colour and label as for Germany.
- **Caricatures:** leader trademarks (moustache, cigar, pipe and so on) are fine for historical figures.
