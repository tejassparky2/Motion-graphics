**Title:** `Speaker Awaaz Banata Hi Nahi? 😳 Kaise Kaam Karta Hai` (52 characters)

**Sources** (in the description as names only):
- Rice & Kellogg 1925, the moving-coil loudspeaker (General Electric); Wikipedia "Loudspeaker".
- Apple Support, "Use Water Lock on Apple Watch" (the speaker pushes water out).
- Knowles microspeaker datasheets and audioXpress: the common 11×15 mm phone speaker, with excursion of about ±0.3 mm.
- Electroacoustic reciprocity (IEC Electropedia): a speaker works as a microphone in reverse.
- Basic physics: sound travels at about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C, and human hearing covers about 20-20,000 Hz.

**Checked wording:**
- "Kai speakers mein kaagaz ka cone" is true for many woofers. Phone speakers use plastic film, so the line says
  "kai", not "har".
- "Kuch watches" refers to the Apple Watch's Water Lock, named in the sources but not on screen (no brand logos).
- The Mumbai local is an analogy for how a push travels while the air itself stays in place. It is not a claim.

**Sources** (names only in the description):
- Suraweera et al. 2020, *eLife*, "Trends in snakebite deaths in India from 2000 to 2019": about 58,000 a year,
  mostly at home in rural areas, in the monsoon.
- Government of India (MoHFW) standard treatment guidelines, snakebite: kraits are active at night and often
  bite people sleeping on the floor; first aid.
- WHO SEARO, Guidelines for the management of snakebites, 2nd edition: no cutting, suction or tight tourniquet;
  immobilise the limb; go to hospital.
- Review of Indian common krait bites (Chandigarh): usually painless, minimal swelling, neuroparalysis.
- Chappuis et al. 2007, *American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene*: sleeping under a bed net protected
  against snakebite in southeastern Nepal.
