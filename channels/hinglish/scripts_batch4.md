**Facts it rests on (checked):**
- GPT-4 scored more than 20 points above the pass mark on official US medical licensing exam (USMLE) practice
  questions (Nori et al. 2023, Microsoft and OpenAI). The script says "solves doctors' exam questions", not that it
  got a licence.
- These models are trained to predict the next word piece, and they write answers one piece at a time (that is
  why the answer appears as if typed).
- GPT-3 has 175 billion parameters (Brown et al. 2020, OpenAI). The video calls them "knobs".
- Meta's Llama 3 was trained on more than 15 trillion tokens (Meta, April 2024). At ~0.75 words a token that is
  about 11 trillion words; reading 8 hours a day at 250 words a minute (120,000 words a day) takes about 2.5 lakh
  years, so the script says "do lakh saal se zyada".
- The same question gives different answers because each word is picked with a little randomness. Models can be
  confidently wrong ("hallucination") because they pick the likeliest-sounding word, not a checked fact.

**Built on the rules:**
- An impossible-sounding hook: something that passes doctors' exams "only guesses the next word".
- Everyday anchors: the phone keyboard's next-word suggestion, a chai example, a fan regulator.
- One recurring metaphor: the **regulator knobs** (parameters), turned a little after every wrong guess.
- A re-hook at about 60% (line 11), a "socho" bookend, and a two-sided closing question.
- About 1:40.
