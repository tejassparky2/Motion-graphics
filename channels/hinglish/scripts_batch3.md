**Fact check (what really happened):**
- Tufts University (Michael Levin's lab, first author Junji Morokuma) sent planarian flatworms (*Dugesia
  japonica*) to the International Space Station on SpaceX CRS-5, launched 10 Jan 2015. They stayed about 5 weeks.
  15 worms had their heads and tails cut off before the flight; a control group stayed on Earth.
- On return the worms were curled up and almost unable to move. They took about 2 hours to move normally.
- **1 of the 15 fragments regrew a head at both ends.** The lab said it had never seen this happen on its own in
  18 years with this species.
- Back on Earth they cut that worm into three; the headless middle piece **grew two heads again**, so the change
  looks lasting.
- The space worms also spent less time in the dark (70.5% vs 95.5%). Their behaviour and gut bacteria were still
  different 20 months later.
- **Nobody knows why.** Other scientists (Sluys and Stocchino) argued it may be a fluke with only 15 worms; the
  authors replied that it is extremely rare on Earth. The script says both.
