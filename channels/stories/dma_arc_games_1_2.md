## The rule for every game from now on (owner's direction)

1. Every game hides a **loophole in its own rules that lets all players survive**. Ren finds it and fights to save
   everyone, including people the class would rather let die.
2. **People break it**: greed, fear, betrayal, panic, or someone's sacrifice. Only a few games end with everyone
   alive (Game 1 is one); from Game 2 on, most games kill.
3. **Hard:** the obvious answers fail on screen first (at least two), so the real answer feels impossible until Ren
   finds it. The real answer always needs **two rules combined** or **one exact word** in a rule, never a new rule.
4. **Clear:** one step per sentence, one card per step, and a short proof card at the moment of the answer (e.g.
   `0 tokens = not a player → give everything to Momo → 0 players → nobody is the poorest`). Every number on screen is
   exact.
5. **Fair:** the clue is on screen before Ren explains it, and the betrayal's clue is planted before it happens
   (bible rules 1-3, 9).

Everything below that is not in the bible is **my proposal** and becomes canon only when the owner approves it.

## Game 1: how it ends (Part 3, "The Bids")

Already shown in Part 2: Riku announced "Nobody beats a hundred"; Aoi warned that two hundreds tie and die; Kota
showed all-zero kills everyone (rule 5); Mio said "somebody has to bid, and everyone will remember who"; Ren said a
hundred might be the most dangerous number in the room, and "I already knew my number."

**Ren's number is one.** His reasoning, one step at a time:
1. Somebody has to spend, or rule 5 kills all twenty.
2. Two equal bids at the top tie, and rule 3 kills both. So **exactly one** person may bid.
3. Spent tokens are lost **permanently** (rule 2), so that bid must be as small as possible: **one token**.
4. Mio is right that everyone will remember who bid, so Ren bids it himself.
5. **The hard part, betrayal:** someone will lie. So Ren makes lying safe: "If you want the key, bid more than one.
   You win, and nobody dies. Just never bid a hundred. Riku already called it." The only lie that could kill is
   copying Ren's one, and that would kill the liar too.

**What happens:** Riku agrees too fast. Momo: "Highest unique bid: thirty! Kanzaki Riku!" The reveal: Riku never meant
a hundred. He said it in Part 2 so nobody would dare bid high, and thirty was enough. Riku: "You trusted a plan. I
trusted a number." Ren: "He thought he beat my plan. My plan was built for a liar." No tie: **20/20 survive**. The exit
key opens the exit, into Game 2. Tokens after Game 1: Ren 99, Riku 70 (fewest), the other 18 have 100.

Answers this needs (proposed): every bid is spent (Q1: losers aren't otherwise punished); the exit key opens the door
for everyone, and it leads to Game 2 (Q2); you may bid 0 while others bid (Q4); bids are secret and final (Q5);
tokens carry over (Q7). Q3 and Q6 stay open.

## Game 2: THE CHARITY (rules fixed once approved)

A white room. A screen that shows one number: **PLAYERS: 20**. Momo sits in the middle of the room, swinging its legs.
Ten minutes.

- Rule 1: Nobody can take your tokens.
- Rule 2: You may give tokens to **anyone in this room**. Gifts are final.
- Rule 3: When time runs out, the poorest player dies. If several tie for poorest, they all die.
- Rule 4: Zero tokens, and you're not a player anymore.

Momo: "Tokens are precious! You'll want them later!"

**Why it looks impossible (the wrong answers, all on screen):**
- Give Riku tokens? Then the giver is the poorest.
- Make everyone equal? A tie for poorest kills **all** of them.
- Give everything to one person? Then they're the only player, and Momo confirms: "One player is always the poorest!"
- Tokens can't vanish (rule 1: nobody can take them; there is no box), so somebody always holds them, and whoever
  holds the fewest dies.
- Go: "Let Riku be the poorest. He lied to all of us." Then the quietest girl in the class, **Nishida Saki (seat
  14)**, says "He's still one of us." Riku hears her. (This is the clue for Part 6.)

**The loophole (two steps):**
1. Rule 4: zero tokens means you're not a player. If **nobody** is a player, nobody is the poorest.
2. But the tokens must go somewhere. Rule 2 doesn't say "give them to a player". It says **anyone in this room**.
   Momo is in this room, and Momo isn't a player.

Everyone gives everything to Momo, so there are zero players and **all 20 live**. Momo: "For Momo? How generous!"
The price: everyone walks into Game 3 with nothing.

**What people do to it (greed, and a betrayal of kindness):**
- The class gives everything to Momo. Saki: "It's okay. I never wanted them." PLAYERS falls to **1**: Riku.
- Riku refuses: "I'm not walking into game three with nothing." Ren: "Then you're the only player. And the only
  player is always the poorest." Riku smiles.
- With three seconds left, Riku puts **one token** in Saki's hand. Gifts are final. **PLAYERS: 2.** One token makes
  her a player again, and the poorest one. Nobody else has a single token left to stop it.
- If she gives it to Momo, Riku is alone again and **he** dies. Ren shouts "Saki! Give it to Momo!" (he is telling her
  to kill Riku, and he knows it). She can't. Riku knew she couldn't: he heard her say "He's still one of us."
- Time's up: Riku 69, Saki 1. Saki is executed (lights out; when they come back she's gone and her token lies on the
  floor; no gore). **19 survivors.** Riku: "She was the only one I trusted." Ren: "I found the loophole. Riku found
  the loophole in my loophole."

**Why it's fair:** rule 2's "anyone in this room" is on the card, and Momo is in every shot of the room before Ren
sees it. Riku's move uses the same rule 2 (you can give to anyone, even someone who is out). Saki's "He's still one of
us" is planted two parts before it kills her.
