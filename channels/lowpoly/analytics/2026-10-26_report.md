## What it says (first read)
1. **Short comedy keeps people longest.** The four videos of 40 s or less average 72% viewed (one at 85%); the six of
   60 s or more average 53%. The single best video is the 30-second "Explain It Like I'm Five".
2. **Longer emotional skits win subscribers and likes.** 60 s+ videos: 1.6 subscribers and 22 likes per 1,000 views;
   40 s or less: 1.0 and 15. "My Boss Made AI Mandatory" (4) and "Retiring" (3) brought the most subscribers.
3. **Stayed-to-watch is about the first seconds.** Best: Git news 68.5% (early, small sample), Sister 65%, Disagree
   63.5%, LiteLLM 61.5%. Worst: "Forgets the Whole Project" 43.8% and "OpenAI on Google Cloud" 45.4%: both open on a
   concept (a forgotten project, a cloud deal) rather than something you can picture going wrong.
4. **News works for reach when it hits developers directly.** LiteLLM ("your keys") got 768 engaged views in about a
   day and a half with 61.5% stayed; the cloud-business story kept fewer people.
5. **The 4:30 / 7:30 PM IST switch is too new to judge** (the Git video at 4:30 PM had 204 views at export; the
   Google Cloud one at 7:30 PM had 901). Compare after a week of posts at the new times.

## What we change
- Keep the mix, and make it deliberate: short comedy (30-40 s) for reach, emotional/longer skits for subscribers,
  news that touches developers personally (security, tools they use, limits, prices) for fresh reach.
- News explainers: aim for 40-50 s, open on the personal stake ("Why are you changing my keys?"), avoid
  business-deal stories unless they change something for a normal developer.
- Every opener: something already going wrong that you can picture in one second (the lowest stayed-to-watch videos
  open on a concept).
- Ask for the subscribe in the pinned comment as now; returning viewers are only 28, so a recurring hook ("Ep. N of
  Dev vs the bots") matters.
